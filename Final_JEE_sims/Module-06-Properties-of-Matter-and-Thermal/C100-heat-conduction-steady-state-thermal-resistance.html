Guided Inquiry

The wall

An outside wall in winter — 20 °C inside, 0 °C outside. It is built in three layers: 10 cm brick, 5 cm insulation, 2 cm plaster. The arrows show heat current through the wall. Look at the red temperature profile. Where is it steep, and where is it shallow?

Predict

You want to lose less heat. You can double one layer's thickness. Which saves more heat?

Now try it

Drag the insulation slider in the sidebar from 5 cm → 10 cm and watch the heat rate. Then reset it, and try doubling the brick instead (10 → 20 cm).

The insulation slice is thin but steep — it takes almost the whole 20 °C drop. That is why doubling it matters. The brick is thick but shallow.

The rule

In series, thermal resistances add: R = L/(kA), same shape as electrical resistors. The heat current
H = ΔT / Rtotal
is the same in every layer — so the temperature drop across each layer is proportional to its own R.

Thin bad conductor beats thick good conductor. That is why houses have insulation, not extra brick.
