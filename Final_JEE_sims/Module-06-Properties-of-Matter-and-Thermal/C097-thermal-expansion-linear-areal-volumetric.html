About: Thermal Expansion — the Hole Gets Bigger Too

Source

Topic
Thermal properties of matter (Class 11)
Concept
Every linear dimension of a heated solid scales by (1 + αΔT), including lengths measured across empty space — so a hole in a plate gets bigger, not smaller.
Serves
JEE / NCERT Class 11 — linear, areal and volumetric expansion; expansion of cavities.
Inquiry
What does the hole do as the plate is heated — shrink, stay the same, or grow?
Misconception
“The expanding metal closes the hole.” No: every distance scales together.

Guided Inquiry

Predict: what does the hole do?

A steel ball sits stuck on a metal ring — the ball is 0.030 mm wider than the cold hole. You are about to heat the ring. Commit to a prediction first: as the ring heats up, the hole will…

Heat it — free the ball.

Drag ΔT up slowly and watch the middle panel: the true clearance creeps from negative toward zero… and the ball drops through. The pass/fail moment uses the real micrometre numbers, not the drawing scale.

Meanwhile the rail on the left eats into its expansion gap. Push ΔT far enough and the gap closes — the rail buckles. That gap is exactly why rails and bridges are built with one.

The 1 : 2 : 3 ratio, live.

Watch the three strain bars along the bottom while you drag ΔT: length, area and volume strains grow together, always in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3. Switch material — the bars rescale but the ratio never changes.

That is because (1 + αΔT)² − 1 ≈ 2αΔT and (1 + αΔT)³ − 1 ≈ 3αΔT to first order. Try Invar: α is so small the ball never drops.

Why the hole grew.

Pick any two points of the cold ring — one on each side of the hole. After heating, every distance between two points of the metal is multiplied by (1 + αΔT), including the one measured across the empty gap.

So the hot ring is a photographic enlargement of the cold ring. The hole grows by the same fractional amount as a solid disc of the same material would — which is why heating a stuck jar lid loosens it.
