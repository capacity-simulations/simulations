Topic
Geometric optics — refraction at a flat interface
Concept
The ray bends because one side of the wavefront is slowed before the other; the same slowing raises the apparent depth of a submerged object.
Audience
JEE (NCERT Class 11–12): Snell's law, refractive index, apparent depth.
Serves
Mechanism-first understanding of refraction; connects two textbook results (bending toward the normal; d′ = d/n) to a single cause (slower light in the denser medium).
Inquiry
What happens to the bending at exactly normal incidence — bends most, bends least, or not at all?
Misconception
“Light bends because the water pushes it,” or “refraction is a property of the ray, not the wave.” The pivoting wavefront makes both wrong.

Guided Inquiry

The setup

A ray of light in air strikes the flat surface of water at some angle. In water, light travels slower — that single fact drives everything you're about to see. On the left, watch the ray for a moment; drag the angle of incidence slider in the sidebar to feel how the refracted ray tilts.

Angles are measured from the normal — the dashed line perpendicular to the surface.

Predict: normal incidence

Now suppose the ray comes straight down — perpendicular to the surface, angle of incidence = 0°. What happens to the bending?

The mechanism — a pivoting wavefront

The blue bars are wavefronts — surfaces where the wave is in step. In air they march forward at c. The moment one end of a bar enters water it slows; the other end is still in air moving at full speed. The bar pivots, and the whole wave now points in a new direction — bent toward the normal.

Move the incidence slider between 60° and 0°. At 60° the pivot is dramatic. At 0° both ends enter together and nothing pivots.

The consequence — the pool looks shallow

Look at the right panel. A coin sits on the bottom of a pool of real depth d. Rays from the coin bend away from the normal on their way out to your eye. Your brain ignores the bend — it extrapolates the outgoing rays back in a straight line, and they cross at a shallower apparent depth.

Change the Real depth. Notice: the apparent depth equals real depth ÷ n. For water, a 1 m pool looks about 0.75 m deep.

Two effects, one cause

Set the incidence angle to exactly 0°. On the left the bending has vanished. But the coin on the right still looks raised. Why?

Why the wavefront pivots
In a time Δt the end of the bar still in air travels v₁ Δt; the end already in water travels only v₂ Δt < v₁ Δt. The wavefront is the line joining the two ends, so it turns — and since the ray is perpendicular to the wavefront, so does the ray.
