Guided Inquiry

The starting picture

Light of wavelength 550 nm hits a slit of width a = 100 µm. The central bright band is 11 mm wide.

You are about to halve the slit to 50 µm. What happens to the central band?

Now do it

Drag the Slit width a slider down from 100 µm to 50 µm and watch the central band. Notice the screen scale doesn't move — the pattern really does grow.

Read the central band width readout as you drag. It should reach about 22 mm at a = 50 µm.

Why? The first minimum

The first dark fringe sits where light from one edge of the slit arrives exactly one wavelength behind light from the other edge — they cancel. This gives a·sinθ = λ, so sinθ = λ/a.

Smaller a ⇒ larger θ ⇒ wider central band. The width on a screen at distance L is W = 2λL/a — the whole spread goes as 1/a.

And the wavelength?

Try dragging Wavelength λ from 550 nm (green) toward 700 nm (deep red). The central band grows again — longer waves spread more, because sinθ = λ/a.

The faint bumps outside the central band are side lobes. The first one reaches only about 4.7% of the central peak — the dark bands really are dark.

The one thing to notice

Narrowing the slit makes the pattern wider. Confine a wave in space and it must spread in angle — the tighter you squeeze, the more it fans out. It is not a shadow of the slit; it is diffraction.

Play freely with both sliders. The screen scale stays fixed at ±25 mm so the growth stays visible.
