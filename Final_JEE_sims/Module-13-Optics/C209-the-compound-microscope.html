About: The Compound Microscope

Source

Topic
Geometric optics — optical instruments (Class 12, NCERT Physics Part II, Ch. 9.9)
Simulation
One optical bench: specimen, objective, the real intermediate image inside the tube, and the eyepiece used as a magnifier on it.
Sign convention
Real-is-positive (Cartesian). Light travels left to right; distances are measured from the lens; u > 0 for a real object on the left; v > 0 for a real image on the right; 1/u + 1/v = 1/f and m = −v/u.
Tube length
L is the NCERT tube length: the distance from the objective's second focus Fo′ to the eyepiece's first focus Fe. The lens separation drawn on the bench is fo + L + fe.
Serves
Thin-lens equation applied twice; linear vs angular magnification; M = mo × me; normal adjustment vs near-point adjustment; why the final image is virtual and inverted.
Inquiry
Predict — before touching the objective slider — what a LONGER objective focal length does to the magnification.
Misconception
"A longer / bigger objective always magnifies more." The two instruments that share this pair of lenses do not agree, and the sim puts both numbers on screen.

Guided Inquiry

1 · Two lenses in a tube

A compound microscope has two converging lenses. The specimen sits just beyond the objective's focal point, so the objective forms a real, inverted, enlarged image inside the tube. The eyepiece is then used as a magnifier on that image.

2 · A longer objective

A telescope magnifies more when its objective has a longer focal length. Keeping the eyepiece and the tube unchanged, what does a longer objective do to this microscope's magnification?

3 · Compare two objectives

Set the objective's focal length to 20 mm, then to 4 mm, leaving the eyepiece and the tube alone. Compare the real image the objective forms inside the tube in the two cases.

4 · A shorter eyepiece

The eyepiece is replaced by one of half its focal length; the objective and the tube are unchanged. What happens to the total magnification?

5 · The same pair as a telescope

An astronomical telescope built from these two lenses has magnification −fo/fe. With fo = 8 mm and fe = 25 mm, what would that telescope do?

6 · The one thing to notice

In a microscope the objective's focal length divides; in a telescope it multiplies. That is why a microscope objective is a stubby few-millimetre lens while a telescope objective is long. The image you finally look at is virtual and upside down.

Fixed: near point D = 250 mm, specimen height ho = 0.10 mm. A negative magnification means the image is upside down.

Objective — the thin-lens equation

Real-is-positive convention: uo > 0 for the real object on the left, vo > 0 for the real image formed on the far side, m = −v/u. The object sits just beyond Fo, so uo is a little larger than fo.

Eyepiece — used as a magnifier

D = 25 cm is the least distance of distinct vision. The upper line is normal adjustment (final image at infinity, eye relaxed); the lower line is near-point adjustment.

Total magnification

The total is the PRODUCT of the two, never the sum. L is the tube length measured from Fo′ to Fe, which is why the standard form is exact here in normal adjustment.

Microscope against telescope

The same fo that DIVIDES a microscope's magnification MULTIPLIES a telescope's. One pair of lenses cannot be good at both jobs.
