Guided Inquiry

1 · Draw your first jump

On the left, the ladder shows the first few hydrogen energy levels. Set ni = 3 and nf = 2 in the controls below to draw the arrow. A line appears on the right at the wavelength that jump emits — in the colour it would actually have.

What colour appeared, and where on the spectrum did it land?

2 · Predict where 4 → 2 lands

4 → 2 is a bigger energy jump than 3 → 2. Does the new line land further into the red, or shift toward the blue?

3 · Build the Balmer series

Now set ni = 4, then 5, then 6, always ending at nf = 2. Watch what happens to the SPACING between successive lines.

The lines crowd together toward a limit near 365 nm rather than spreading out evenly — because the energy LEVELS crowd toward zero. Each line is a level gap, so the pattern of lines is the pattern of gaps.

4 · Predict: where does 2 → 1 land?

All the visible hydrogen lines land on n = 2. What about a jump landing on n = 1 (the Lyman series)? The 2 → 1 gap is much bigger than any Balmer gap. Where should the line fall?

5 · The one thing to notice

Every line in the spectrum is one arrow on the energy ladder. Only the arrows landing on n = 2 happen to be visible; the rest are UV or IR. Try any jump — a 4 → 3 Paschen line lands at 1875 nm in the infrared. Same physics, invisible photon.

The picture the eye sees (a few coloured lines) is a slice of a much larger fingerprint. That fingerprint is the level ladder.
