About: Photoelectric Effect

Source

Topic
Quantum physics — the photoelectric effect (Einstein, 1905)
Concept
Light delivers energy in discrete quanta of size hf; ejection requires hf ≥ φ regardless of intensity.
Serves
Predicting the threshold frequency, distinguishing intensity from frequency, seeing that frequency alone sets K_max through K_max = hf − φ.
Key inquiry
Why can a dim blue lamp eject electrons while a bright red one cannot?
Misconception
“More intense light ⇒ more energetic electrons.” (Reality: more intense ⇒ more electrons, same K_max.)

Guided Inquiry

1 · Predict — turn up the brightness

The lamp is red (below the metal's threshold). You crank the intensity way up so the plate is dazzlingly bright. What happens?

2 · Observe — swap red for blue

Now switch the lamp to blue. Even a dim blue lamp ejects electrons instantly. Watch the scene: photons carry hf, and once hf ≥ φ the electron leaves with K_max = hf − φ.

Try the Frequency slider yourself — cross the threshold f₀ and electrons start streaming off the plate.

3 · Verify — what controls K_max?

You want to make the ejected electrons FASTER (higher K_max). Which knob do you turn?

4 · Change metal — thresholds differ

Different metals have different work functions φ. Try Cesium (φ ≈ 2.1 eV) vs. Zinc (φ ≈ 4.3 eV) at the same frequency. Notice how the threshold moves — and how K_max drops when φ rises.

Watch the sidebar: Threshold frequency f₀ = φ/h updates with the metal.

5 · The full picture

Below-threshold red at any intensity → nothing. Above-threshold blue even dim → instant ejection. K_max grows linearly with f, slope h, intercept −φ. This is why Einstein got the 1921 Nobel Prize: light is quantised.
