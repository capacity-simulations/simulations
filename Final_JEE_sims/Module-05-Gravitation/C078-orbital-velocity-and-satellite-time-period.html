About: Orbital Velocity — Climb Higher, Go Slower

Source

Topic
Gravitation — circular orbits around a spherical planet
Simulation
Drag a satellite between the surface and beyond geostationary; watch orbital speed and period react.
Concept
A higher orbit is a slower orbit, yet the period grows dramatically longer because the orbit is far bigger.
Serves
NCERT Class 11 Gravitation — circular orbital speed v = √(GM/r), Kepler's third law T² ∝ r³, specific orbital energy E = −GM/(2r).
Misconception
"Higher orbit means more energy, so it must be moving faster." It has more total energy but less kinetic energy.

Guided Inquiry

1 · Meet the space station

The ISS orbits 400 km up and takes just 92 minutes to lap the Earth — that's 7.67 km/s. Press ▶ Play and watch it go round. Notice the two readouts: speed and period.

2 · Predict: raise it four times higher

Now imagine dragging the satellite from 400 km up to ~1 600 km — four times the altitude. What happens to its orbital speed?

3 · And the period?

You said the speed drops. What about the period — how long one lap takes?

4 · Drag it out — see for yourself

Grab the satellite and pull it outward. Watch the two readouts move in opposite directions: speed falls, period climbs — fast. Stop at the GPS preset and the GEO preset.

GPS (20 200 km): 3.87 km/s, ~12 hours. Geostationary (35 786 km): 3.07 km/s, exactly 24 hours — that's why the satellite hangs over one spot on Earth.

5 · The energy resolution

The specific orbital energy E = −GM/(2r) stays negative at every bound orbit and rises toward zero as the orbit grows. So a higher orbit has more total energy (less negative) — but the KE went down. The extra went into climbing out of the gravity well.

Turn on ∑ Formal in the top bar to see the equations. When Play is on the satellite keeps orbiting — try switching planets in the sidebar and see how Mars, with a fifth Earth's mass, changes everything.
