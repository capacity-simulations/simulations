About: The Beam That Will Not Turn

Source

Topic
Static equilibrium of a rigid body — a light beam on two end supports carrying a sliding load.
Concept
Balancing the forces tells you the two supports carry the whole weight — but only balancing the torques tells you how they split it.
Audience
JEE / NCERT Class 11–12. Standard result named: law of the lever (moments about a support).
Misconception targeted
“The supports each take half.” They only do at the midpoint; a load over one support puts the entire weight on it.

Guided Inquiry

Predict — load at two-fifths of the span

A 10 kg load hangs at x = 2 m on a 5 m beam resting on two end supports. How is its weight shared between the LEFT and RIGHT supports?

Read the arrows

Look at the two blue and green arrows. Their combined length stays visibly constant as you drag the load — that's ΣF = 0. But their individual lengths TRADE — that's the torque condition doing work you can see.

Notice: it's the support closer to the load that grows taller. The lever arm from the far support is longer, so the far support gets a smaller share.

Slide to the midpoint

The load has been placed at x = 2.5 m — the two arrows equalise at exactly N_L = N_R = 49.05 N. This is the ONE position where “each takes half” is true.

Now slide the load all the way to x = 0 (over the left support). The right arrow vanishes; the left takes the whole 98.1 N. The share is fixed by geometry, not by fairness.

Verify — which condition sets the split?

Both readouts sit at zero at every position. What does that mean the split N_L : N_R is fixed by?

The lever arm is a perpendicular distance

The dashed line under the beam is the lever arm from the LEFT support to the load's line of action — a distance, not a position. Torque is force times PERPENDICULAR distance, and it's this length that appears in Στ = 0.

You now own the full simulation. Both conditions sit at zero because both are enforced — remove either and the beam either falls or turns.
