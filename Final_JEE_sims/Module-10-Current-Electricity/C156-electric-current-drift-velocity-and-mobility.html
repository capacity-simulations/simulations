About: Drift Velocity — the Bias in the Average

Source

Topic
Current Electricity — drift velocity of free electrons in a metallic conductor.
Concept
A current is a statistical bias in a fast, random electron swarm — it is invisible in any single electron and lives only in the average.
Serves
Interpreting I = nAevd; distinguishing thermal speed from drift speed; the sign convention for electron flow vs conventional current.
Inquiry
With the field OFF, will switching it ON turn the electrons round to march along the wire, or leave each one looking random while the AVERAGE creeps?
Misconception
"The field makes the electrons turn and march." In fact each electron drifts about 10−18 m between collisions — less than a hundred-millionth of an atomic spacing — while its thermal speed is 1010 times the drift speed.

Guided Inquiry

1 · Watch the field-OFF swarm

The field is OFF. Every electron is fast (its thermal speed is about 1.57 × 106 m/s in copper), moves in a random direction, and rebounds off obstacles. The orange marker is the average position ⟨x⟩ of the whole swarm.

Watch it. ⟨x⟩ jitters around a fixed point and goes nowhere. Now predict what happens next.

2 · Predict — when you switch the field ON…

You are about to flip the field switch. What will you see on the canvas?

3 · Flip the switch, watch ⟨x⟩

Use the Field control in the sidebar (or press F) to switch the field ON. Watch the canvas — no single electron does anything new — and watch the graph below: ⟨x⟩ leaves its jitter and becomes a straight line.

The slope of that line is the drift velocity. The sidebar shows the slope measured from the graph next to the predicted I/(nAe) — they should agree.

4 · Vary I and r — the standard result

Now play with current and wire radius. Two things to check:

• Double the current → the drift doubles (slope doubles).
• Double the radius → area quadruples → drift is quartered (slope shrinks by 4).

This is I = n A e vd, the NCERT result. And remember: the drift on screen is exaggerated so you can SEE it — the real numbers stay in the readout.

Copper: n ≈ 8.5 × 1028 m−3, e = 1.602 × 10−19 C. A = π r². The tiny drift speed is the reason a torch lights instantly (the field, not the carriers, propagates near c) yet a given electron takes hours to cross a metre of wire.
