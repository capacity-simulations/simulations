Guided Inquiry

1 · Predict — the push climbs to 20 N

Set the Applied force F slider to 20 N. The block (5 kg, μₛ = 0.5) resists with at most μₛmg = 24.5 N. The push ramps from 0 to 20 N and the block does not move. What is friction doing over that range?

2 · Watch — friction tracks the push

With F at 20 N — safely below the 24.5 N peak, so the block stays put — press ▶ Play. F ramps from 0 to 20 N. Watch the friction arrow on the LEFT: it grows in lockstep with the applied arrow, so the two exactly cancel. On the RIGHT, the working point climbs the f = F line. This is what a self-adjusting force LOOKS like.

3 · Predict — the instant F exceeds 24.5 N

You keep ramping. At F = 24.5 N the friction is at its maximum. Push a tiny bit harder — F = 24.6 N. What happens to the friction force at that instant?

4 · Explore

Set F back to 30 N and Play again — watch the graph climb the f = F line to the peak at μₛmg, then fall vertically to μₖmg. The gap between those two values is the size of the lurch. Change the mass or the coefficients and see how the peak and the drop rescale together. The one thing to remember: static friction is whatever cancels the push, right up to the moment it gives — and then it DROPS.
