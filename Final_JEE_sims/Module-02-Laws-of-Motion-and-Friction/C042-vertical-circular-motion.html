About: The Vertical Circle — Where the String Nearly Lets Go

Source

Concept
In a vertical circle the tension varies round the loop; it is greatest at the bottom (speed largest, gravity opposing centripetal) and least at the top.
The one thing to notice
Tbottom − Ttop = 6 m g, no matter the speed or radius.
Serves
Newton's second law along the radius; energy conservation between top and bottom; the string-only-pulls constraint.
Misconception targeted
“The string is under most tension where the ball moves fastest, so both are at the bottom, and at the top gravity pulls the ball off the circle.”

Guided Inquiry

Step 1 · Predict — where does the string strain most?

The ball is swung in a vertical circle just fast enough to get round. Where on the circle is the string closest to snapping?

Step 2 · Watch the string

Press ▶ Play. Watch the string's thickness — it strains (thick and taut) at the bottom and goes slack (thin, curving) near the top. Read the tension number rising and falling with each pass.

The tension bar on the right shows the live value; zero is marked.

Step 3 · The invariant

Change the bottom speed. Change the radius. Watch Tbottom and Ttop both change — but what about their difference?

Step 4 · Free exploration

Drop the bottom speed toward the critical value. The top tension slides toward zero and the string goes slack at the top of the loop — the ball leaves the circle. Below the critical speed, it never gets to the top at all.

Play freely with both controls. The one thing to remember: Tbottom − Ttop = 6 m g, always.
