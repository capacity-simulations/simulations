Simulation
Rear view of a car on a banked curve, with force diagram and safe-speed band
Concept
The design speed of a banked turn depends only on radius and bank angle — friction never enters it, it only widens the tolerance band around it.
Serves
Recognising the design speed as a geometric quantity; distinguishing it from the friction-dependent safe range.
Inquiry
Set μ = 0, read the design speed. Predict what happens to that design speed as μ rises, then check.
Misconception
"A banked road has a safe speed, and more friction raises it."

Guided Inquiry

Step 1 — the frictionless case

The friction coefficient has been set to μ = 0. With no friction, only the bank itself can turn the car. Read the design speed off the band beneath the scene, then press Next.

Step 2 — predict

You are about to drag the friction slider μ up from 0. What will happen to the design speed — the speed marked vd on the band?

Step 3 — drag μ and watch

Drag the μ slider from 0 up toward 0.4. Watch the green marker vd on the band — and the two ends of the shaded safe range.

The band widens in both directions. The design speed marker doesn't move.

Step 4 — the whole picture

Now try the speed slider. Below vmin the car slides down the bank (into the turn); above vmax it slides up and off. Between them, static friction closes the gap between the bank's pull and what the turn requires.

Change the bank angle θ. That does move vd — because the design speed is geometry.
