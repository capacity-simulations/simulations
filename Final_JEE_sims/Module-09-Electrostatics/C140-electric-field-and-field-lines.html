Guided Inquiry

1 · Look at the dipole

Two point charges, +q on the left and −q on the right. Every curve on the canvas is a field line: the engine puts a seed near a charge and steps along the local field direction, over and over, until it hits another charge or leaves the frame.

Notice where the lines crowd close together, and where they thin out. That spacing is not artistic — it is what the tracer produced.

2 · Predict — like charges

Right now the second charge is negative. If we flip it to positive — so both charges are +q — what happens to the lines running from one charge to the other?

3 · Flip the sign and watch

Use the Sign of q₂ control in the panel below to flip the right charge from − to +. The lines re-trace live: watch the region between the two charges empty out.

Every curve is still doing the same thing — starting near a charge and walking along the local field. There is simply nowhere between two like charges for such a walk to lead but outward.

4 · Read the local field

A yellow test point appears on the canvas. Drag it. The arrow is the force a +1 test charge would feel, and the number is |E| in natural units (k = 1 — a shape number, not V/m).

Now move the test charge along a single field line. The arrow stays tangent to the line — that is why the curve exists at all.

5 · Density is field strength

Move the test charge from where the field lines crowd (near a charge, or on the dipole axis) to where they thin out. |E| shrinks in step with the spacing.

That is the one thing to notice: the tracer's density really does mean field strength, because both come from the same field. Nothing was drawn by hand.
