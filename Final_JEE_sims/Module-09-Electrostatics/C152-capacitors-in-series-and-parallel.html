About: Capacitors in Series and Parallel

Source

Electrostatics — capacitor networks
Concept
Capacitors in series give LESS than either one alone — the exact opposite of what the same word does to resistors.
Level
NCERT Class 12 / JEE Physics
Misconception
"Series adds, like resistors." For capacitors, parallel adds directly (C₁+C₂) and series adds reciprocally (1/C₁+1/C₂).

Guided Inquiry

1 · Predict, before you connect

You have two capacitors, C₁ and C₂, and a battery. You will wire them across the SAME supply two ways: in series, and in parallel. Which network draws more total charge from the supply?

2 · Watch the plates fill

Press ▶ Play. Both networks charge up from the same battery. Watch the +Q shading fill each capacitor.

Notice the geometry the drawing makes literal:

• On the LEFT (series), the two capacitors stack — the effective gap grows. Bigger gap ⇒ smaller C.
• On the RIGHT (parallel), the plates sit side by side — the effective plate area grows. Bigger area ⇒ bigger C.

Read the bars underneath: the parallel bar overtakes both series capacitors before charging finishes.

3 · Verify what you saw

With the defaults (C₁ = 2 µF, C₂ = 3 µF), the series network's effective capacitance is 1.2 µF. Compare that to C₁ and C₂ themselves. What is true?

4 · Which quantity is SHARED?

Two more rules that the picture also tells you:

• In series, the two capacitors carry the SAME CHARGE and SPLIT the voltage. (Charge Q₁ = Q₂ — one path, one flow of charge.)
• In parallel, the two capacitors carry the SAME VOLTAGE and SPLIT the charge. (Voltage V₁ = V₂ — wired to the same two nodes.)

Now play with the sliders. Push C₂ up: which effective capacitance grows faster, series or parallel?

5 · The one thing to remember

Capacitors are the opposite way round from resistors.

• Resistors: series ADDS, parallel is reciprocal.
• Capacitors: parallel ADDS, series is reciprocal.

So the reflex "series adds" is a trap here — and knowing the resistor case makes the mistake MORE likely, not less. When you next see a capacitor network, ask first: which way round am I?
