About: Dipole in a Uniform Field — All Torque, No Force

Source

Topic
Electric dipole in a uniform external field
Concept
In a uniform field the net force on a dipole is exactly zero; the two equal-and-opposite forces form a couple, so the dipole rotates on the spot.
Level
JEE / NCERT Class 12 electrostatics.
Standard result
τ = p × E (magnitude pE sin θ); U = −p · E (= −pE cos θ). Stable equilibrium at θ = 0, unstable at θ = 180°.

Guided Inquiry

1 · A dipole between two forces

A tiny dipole sits in a uniform electric field — the arrows are all the same length and all point the same way. The field pulls the +q charge one way and the −q charge exactly the opposite way, with exactly the same strength.

Predict: when you release the dipole, what does it do?

2 · Watch the couple act

Press ▶ Play. Track the red and blue force arrows on the two charges — they stay equal in length and opposite in direction at every instant. The centre of the dipole (the centre dot) does not move; only the orientation changes. The dipole swings toward alignment, overshoots, and oscillates about θ = 0.

Read the sidebar: net force = 0 N, exactly, at every angle. Nothing pushes the dipole sideways.

3 · The energy curve

Below the scene, the curve U(θ) = −pE cos θ shows the potential energy. The moving dot is the dipole's current state on that curve.

Where does the dipole want to settle?

4 · The other equilibrium — 180°

At θ = 180° the dipole is anti-aligned: +q points against E, −q along E. The torque here is also zero — sin 180° = 0.

Set the angle to 179° — a hair off perfect anti-alignment — and release. What happens?

5 · Explore

Try:

• Change E — the couple gets stronger, so the dipole swings faster.
• Change p — the couple's arm gets longer, so torque grows.
• Set the angle to 90° and release — τ is at its maximum.
• Set the angle to 179° — a hair from the peak — and watch it fall away.

Whatever you do, the net force stays exactly 0 N and the centre dot never moves. That is the whole lesson.
