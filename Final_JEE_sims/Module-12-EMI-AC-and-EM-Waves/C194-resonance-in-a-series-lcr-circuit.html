About: Series LCR — Where the Two Reactances Cancel

Source

Topic
Alternating current — Series RLC circuit, resonance and phase.
Concept
At one drive frequency the inductive and capacitive reactances cancel exactly; the impedance collapses to R and the phase angle passes through zero, changing sign.
Serves
Reading a phasor diagram, distinguishing capacitive/inductive behaviour, understanding resonance and Q for JEE-level AC.
Inquiry
As frequency sweeps up through resonance, does the phase angle grow steadily or change sign?
Misconception
"At resonance the reactances disappear." They are as large as ever — X_L and X_C are both big at ω₀ — but they are equal and opposite, so their phasors cancel and only R is left.

Guided Inquiry

1 · Look at the phasors

V_R points along the current. V_L is a quarter turn ahead of it. V_C is a quarter turn behind. The three arrows rotate together at the drive frequency ω. The green resultant V_net is their vector sum — its angle relative to V_R is the phase φ by which the current lags the applied voltage.

Below, on the right, the impedance Z(ω) is plotted. A marker sits on the curve at the drive frequency you set with the ω slider.

2 · Predict — sweep ω up through resonance

You are about to drag ω from well below ω₀ to well above it. As you do, what does the phase angle φ do?

3 · Sweep it and watch

Drag the ω slider from low to high. Watch three things at once:

· V_L (pink) grows and V_C (yellow) shrinks as ω rises — reactances change in opposite directions.
· V_net (green) rotates from BELOW V_R (capacitive, leading) up through V_R (in phase) and above (inductive, lagging).
· The marker on the right rides the impedance curve, dipping into a valley when ω hits ω₀ = 1000 rad/s.

4 · The misconception — do the reactances "disappear" at resonance?

Park ω at 1000 rad/s. V_L and V_C are pointing exactly opposite and V_net collapses onto V_R. What is happening to X_L and X_C at that moment?

5 · Now change R

Drop R from 100 Ω toward 20 Ω and watch the impedance curve. The valley gets deeper and narrower — the quality factor Q rises. But watch where the bottom of the valley sits along the ω-axis: it does not move. Resonance frequency ω₀ = 1/√(LC) depends only on L and C, never on R.

Push R back up and the peak blunts again — the circuit becomes a less selective filter, but ω₀ is unchanged.
