Guided Inquiry

1 · Predict the between-angle

Two identical billiard balls: ball 1 rolls in along the dashed line; ball 2 sits at rest. You will vary how off-centre the hit is — from grazing to nearly head-on.

Before you touch anything: what happens to the angle between the two outgoing paths as the hit goes from grazing to head-on?

2 · Sweep the star control

Press ▶ Play, then drag the How head-on slider from grazing to nearly head-on and back. Watch the arc between the two outgoing paths and its reading.

Every setting, every mass-equal collision: the arc reads 90°. The two speeds change, the two directions change — but their separation does not.

3 · Why can't momentum alone give you the answer?

You have two unknowns per ball after the collision (a speed and a direction), so four unknowns total. Momentum is conserved in x and in y separately. Energy is conserved (elastic). That's three equations, four unknowns.

What supplies the fourth piece of information?

4 · Break the symmetry

Now change one of the masses. Drag m₁ or m₂ away from equal and watch the between-angle badge.

The 90° collapses the instant the masses differ. That right angle was a special property of equal-mass elastic collisions — not a general truth about 2D collisions. Try m₁ = 3 kg, m₂ = 1 kg with a moderate off-centre hit: the angle drops well below 90°.

5 · The take-away

In one dimension, momentum + elastic energy fixes both outgoing velocities. In two dimensions momentum is two equations, elastic energy is a third, and you are still one short — the impact parameter supplies the fourth piece.

The signature result: with equal masses the algebra collapses to exactly 90° between the outgoing paths, at every off-centre setting — and it fails as soon as the masses differ.
