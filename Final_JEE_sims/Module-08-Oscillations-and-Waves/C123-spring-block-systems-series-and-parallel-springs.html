Guided Inquiry

1. Predict — which one wins?

Two identical 1 kg masses will be released together from the same displacement. One hangs on two springs joined end to end (series); the other on two springs mounted side by side (parallel). Which one oscillates faster — reaches its equilibrium and returns first?

2. Watch the race

Press ▶ Play. Both masses were pulled down by the same amount and released together. Notice which one gets back to the top first, and how the SERIES mass drops further before it turns around — the two springs stretch together, and the extensions ADD.

3. Read off the numbers

The sidebar reports both effective spring constants and both periods. Series is always below the softer of the two springs; parallel is always their sum. Try k₁ = 100, k₂ = 200 — series should read 66.67 N/m, parallel 300 N/m.

4. The one thing to remember

For springs, series is softer, parallel is stiffer — the opposite of the resistor rule. The reason is the picture on the left: the two series extensions add, so the same force stretches the combined spring further; that is what "softer" means. Slide k₁ or k₂ freely — the winner never changes.
