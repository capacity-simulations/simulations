About: Forced Oscillation — the Frequency That Gets an Answer

Source

Topic
Mechanics — forced & damped oscillations
Concept
The amplitude peaks BELOW the natural frequency and slides further below it as damping rises — but the phase passes through 90° AT the natural frequency, for every damping.
Audience
JEE / NCERT Class 11–12
Misconception targeted
“Resonance is when you drive at the natural frequency, and that is where the amplitude peaks.” Those are two different frequencies.

Guided Inquiry

1 · Predict — where does the amplitude peak?

Set light damping. You will drive the mass at every frequency Ω you can and record its steady amplitude. On the frequency axis, where will the amplitude be largest?

2 · Commit before you sweep

Pick one. Damping is already low (α = 0.5 rad/s), and ω₀ = 10 rad/s.

3 · Sweep Ω and watch the marker

Drag the driving-frequency slider slowly from low to high. The ■ marker rides the curve. Two vertical lines are drawn: a solid teal one at ω_res (peak) and a dashed one at ω₀. Notice the gap.

Look at the mass too: below ω₀ it moves nearly IN STEP with the hand; at ω₀ it lags by a quarter cycle; well above ω₀ it moves nearly OPPOSITE to the hand.

4 · Change the damping

Lower α to 0.2 rad/s. The peak rises sharply and slides closer to ω₀ — but never reaches it. Raise α past ω₀/√2 ≈ 7.07 rad/s and the peak vanishes altogether: the curve just falls away from its static value.

Now set Ω exactly equal to ω₀ and try every damping. What does the phase do?

5 · The invariant you can see

At Ω = ω₀ the phase reads 90° for every damping. That is the sharper signature of resonance — the one that does not move. The amplitude maximum moves; the 90° crossing does not.
