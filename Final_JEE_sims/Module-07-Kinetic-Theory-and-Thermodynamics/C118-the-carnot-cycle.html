Guided Inquiry

1 · Meet the ceiling

An engine sits between a hot reservoir at T_H = 600 K and a cold one at T_C = 300 K. It absorbs Q_H = 1000 J each cycle. Watch the three streams: heat in from the hot side, work out, heat rejected to the cold side. Their widths are drawn to scale.

Press Play and let the PV cycle trace once. Then move on.

2 · Predict — which lever helps more?

You want a more efficient engine. Starting from T_H = 600 K, T_C = 300 K, which single change raises the efficiency more?

3 · Verify — try both

Move T_H from 600 K to 700 K. Read η. Then reset it, and move T_C from 300 K to 200 K instead. Read η again. The cold-side change is the bigger win, every time.

Notice that the green (work) stream widens and the blue (rejected) stream narrows — but the blue one never closes.

4 · Predict — could a perfect engine reject nothing?

An advertisement claims an engine that converts all its heat to work — Q_C = 0, η = 1. Which of these must be true for that to be possible?

5 · The signature of reversibility

Look at the entropy readout below. For the Carnot cycle the hot reservoir loses exactly the entropy the cold one gains, so ΔS_total = 0 — the only cycle for which that is true. Every real engine produces some entropy and falls short of this bound.

Move the sliders freely. The efficiency will change with T_H and T_C, but ΔS_total will stay pinned to zero — that is Carnot's signature.
