Guided Inquiry

1. Predict — where do the three markers sit?

Imagine drawing three vertical lines on the Maxwell–Boltzmann curve: the most probable speed vp, the mean ⟨v⟩, and the root-mean-square vrms. Which picture is right?

2. Observe — let the histogram build

Press Play. Each molecule contributes its speed to the histogram every so often, so the bars grow ragged first and then settle onto the smooth Maxwell–Boltzmann curve. Watch the three markers on the histogram — they never move onto each other.

Read the ratio vrms/vp in the sidebar. Keep an eye on it while it fills in.

3. Predict — quadruple the temperature

You are about to drag T from 300 K to 1200 K. What happens to the three speeds and to the gaps between the markers?

4. Observe — T = 1200 K

Watch: T just quadrupled, and every one of the three markers doubled. The whole distribution has widened and flattened, but the ratio vrms/vp in the sidebar has NOT changed.

Now try helium instead of nitrogen — same story. Any gas, any temperature: the ratio is fixed at √(3/2) ≈ 1.2247.

5. The one thing to notice

A gas has no single speed. There are three standard ones — vp, ⟨v⟩, vrms — differing by about 22% end to end. Their ratios are fixed pure numbers, independent of gas and temperature:

vp : ⟨v⟩ : vrms = √2 : √(8/π) : √3
            = 1 : 1.128 : 1.225

Change T or M and every speed scales as √(T/M) together — the three markers move as a rigid unit.
