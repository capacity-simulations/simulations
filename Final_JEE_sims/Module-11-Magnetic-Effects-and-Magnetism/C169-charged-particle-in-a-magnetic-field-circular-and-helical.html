Guided Inquiry

1 · Set the scene

An electron is fired into a region of uniform magnetic field B (into the page). It enters perpendicular to B, so the magnetic force curves it into a closed circle.

Watch the trajectory close. The small dial on the right ticks off each completed orbit.

2 · Predict — the star question

You are about to double the entry speed v, holding B and the entry angle fixed. What happens to the time for one complete orbit?

3 · Observe

Drag the entry speed slider up and down. Watch the circle swell and shrink while the orbit clock keeps exactly the same beat.

Read the two numbers below — radius r changes, period T does not budge.

4 · Tilt the entry

Now the electron is given a component along B: the entry angle tilts from 90° to 45°. The perpendicular component still winds a circle at the same beat; the parallel component is untouched by the magnetic force and drifts straight along B.

The result is a helix, advancing one pitch per turn. The clock still marks the same beat.

5 · Resolve

The period T = 2πm/(qB) depends on the particle and the field — never on how fast it is going. That independence is what makes a cyclotron possible: a fixed-frequency accelerator can kick the particle at the same rhythm no matter what energy it has reached.

And note: |v| and KE never change. A magnetic force is always perpendicular to the motion, so it does no work at all.
