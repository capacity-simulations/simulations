Guided Inquiry

Set-up: the coil in the field

A flat rectangular coil of N turns carries current I in a uniform field B = 0.5 T. The angle θ is measured from the coil's normal to B — draw the normal in your head, not the plane. The coil starts at θ = 90° (plane contains the field).

Look at the two side arms: F = NBIL on each, equal and opposite, offset by the coil width. They form a couple — a pure torque, no net force.

Predict: where is τ largest?

Starting at θ = 90° (plane in the field) and turning the coil toward alignment (θ → 0°), where is the torque greatest?

Watch it swing

Press ▶ Play. The coil starts at θ = 90° and rotates under τ = −μB sin θ. The two red forces stay the same size — F = NBIL does not depend on θ. The lever arm shrinks: at θ = 0° the forces squeeze the coil and τ = 0.

Read τ below. It is largest at t = 0 and is zero each time the coil crosses alignment. Damping settles the coil at θ = 0°.

The commutator idea

An undamped coil oscillates about θ = 0°; a real (slightly damped) one settles there. Either way it does NOT keep rotating — because τ vanishes at alignment and REVERSES sign past it.

To keep it turning, flip the current every half turn. The scene now shows the reversal marker (⇄) when the coil crosses alignment: same field, reversed I, same restoring geometry — continuous rotation. That is a commutator.

Explore

Drag θ, I and N in the panel below. Watch the two readouts move together: τ_max = μB with μ = NIA, and the instantaneous τ = −μB sin θ. The maximum torque scales with I and N; the angle where τ = 0 does not.

Magnetic moment

N turns of area A carrying current I. Direction along the normal n̂, by the right-hand rule.

Torque on the coil

θ from the NORMAL to B. Restoring: signed −μB sin θ drives θ back to 0. τ_max = μB at θ = 90°; τ = 0 at θ = 0.

Potential energy

Minimum at θ = 0 (aligned), maximum at θ = 180° (anti-aligned). τ = −dU/dθ exactly.

Net force

In a UNIFORM field the two side-arm forces cancel — a pure couple. The coil turns; it does not translate.
