Guided Inquiry

1 · The setup

A proton sits between two D-shaped electrodes ("dees") in a uniform magnetic field B pointing out of the screen. An alternating voltage across the gap kicks it faster each time it crosses. Inside a dee, the magnetic force curls it around — and does no work, so its speed there is exactly constant.

Notice the radius on the scene, and the per-lap timer in the sidebar. Then click Next.

2 · Predict

As the proton is accelerated across the gap, it speeds up and its orbit gets bigger. What happens to the time for one full lap?

3 · Watch the timer

Press ▶ Play. The proton spirals outward, gaining a red kick at every gap crossing. Read the Per-lap time and the Radius in the sidebar as the lap counter climbs. The radius grows lap after lap, until the orbit fills the dees. The per-lap time holds steady at 131.19 ns the whole way.

That is the payoff: one clock, thousands of laps, no adjustment needed.

4 · Why it works

The magnetic force is always perpendicular to v, so it changes direction only — never speed, never kinetic energy. The only work is done by the electric field at the gap. Inside a dee, watch the Speed readout: it does not change until the next crossing.

Because T = 2πm/(qB) is v-independent, the oscillator driving the gap can run at one fixed frequency (the cyclotron frequency) and stay in step forever.

5 · Break the timing

Now use the Drive detune slider in the sidebar. Slide it away from 0. The oscillator is no longer at the cyclotron frequency — the kicks arrive when the proton is going the wrong way through the gap, so it gets decelerated. The spiral stalls.

This is why cyclotrons eventually fail at relativistic speeds: m grows with v, T stops being constant, and one fixed frequency can no longer stay in step.
