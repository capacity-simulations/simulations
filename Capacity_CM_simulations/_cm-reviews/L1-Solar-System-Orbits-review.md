# Solar System Orbits
**Lecture 1 · L1-Solar System Orbits.html**

**System:** 2-D top-down Kepler problem — 8 planets as test particles in a fixed central potential (GM☉ = 4π² AU³/yr²), integrated with velocity Verlet and sub-stepped; guided inquiry teaches "orbits are ellipses, traversed at varying speed" against the circle-at-constant-speed misconception (⚠ dashed "ghost" Mercury at constant angular speed).
**Verdict:** Physics is correct and canon-faithful — no physics P0/P1. Orbital elements match the real solar system to 4 significant figures; vis-viva, Kepler II and Kepler III verified numerically and on screen; integrator is genuinely symplectic. **The defects are pedagogical and visual**: the step-4 prediction is asked in a view where the correct answer is physically invisible, and canvas labels collide badly at the centre.
**Browser-probe:** Ran. Bundled sweep useless here (0 sliders, no `window.__audit` → 2 states); 3 custom probes written and run — 30/30/42 s live-readout sampling for Mercury/Venus/Earth, an 11-state interaction walkthrough, and a verbatim node replication of `step()` over 60–100 orbits. `errors: []` on every run.

### P2
- [high] **"Gravity is strongest there, so the planet is moving fastest"** — the causal claim in the correct-answer feedback (`wirePredictions`, L1059) and the wrong-answer rebuttal (L1061) is a non-sequitur. At perihelion the force is exactly perpendicular to **v** and does *zero* work at that instant; the speed is high because of the work done during the infall from aphelion (energy conservation), equivalently because `L = mvr` is fixed and *r* is minimal. The sim's own Formal panel already prints the correct one-liner, `v² = GM(2/r − 1/a)`. → **Fix:** reword to "it has been falling toward the Sun all the way from aphelion — the potential energy it lost has become kinetic energy", or cite `L = mvr`.
- [high] **"the only one whose ellipse is visibly stretched"** (step 5 prose L366; step-4 feedback L1070) misstates what eccentricity looks like. At e = 0.206, `b/a = √(1−e²) = 0.9786` — the ellipse is only **2.1 % narrower than it is long**. Measured on canvas: major−minor = **1.71 px** at inner zoom and **0.10 px** at outer. What is actually visible is the **Sun sitting off-centre** by `a·e` (16.4 px at inner zoom; 33 px between the two foci). Teaching "stretched" trains the wrong visual signature. → **Fix:** "the only one where you can clearly see the Sun sitting off-centre in the orbit".
- [med] **Apsidal precession is a numerical artifact** and is retrograde: measured via the Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector (unaliased), **−0.44 arcsec/orbit at 1×** rising to **−9.2 arcsec/orbit at 8×**, scaling cleanly as O(h²) — so it is pure Verlet truncation, not a coding error. Harmless for every claim the sim makes (it never mentions precession, and 415 orbits ≈ 83 min at 1× would be needed to accumulate 183″). Worth knowing that at 1× the artifact is already ≈4× the real GR advance of Mercury (+43″/century) and of opposite sign. → **Fix:** none needed; add a code comment so nobody extends this sim to perihelion precession.
- [low] All eight planets are initialised at **perihelion on the +x axis** (`initBodies`, L659-663), so t = 0 and every Reset show a grand alignment. Physically an arbitrary IC, but it can seed "the planets line up". → **Fix:** give each planet a distinct fixed true anomaly at t = 0.

## Browser-test evidence
- **Vis-viva holds instantaneously, no drift.** Mercury, 715 samples over 30 s (≈19 orbits at 8×): mean relative residual of `v² − GM(2/r − 1/a)` = **0.0014**, max 0.0028 — and that is display quantisation (`r` shown to 3 dp ≈ 0.16 % at r ≈ 0.31), not physics. First quarter 0.00150 → last quarter 0.00134, i.e. **no growth**. Venus 0.00069, Earth 0.00054. (`custom-Mercury.json`, `custom-Venus.json`, `custom-Earth.json`)
- **Kepler II exact.** Mercury `v_max/v_min = 1.51776` vs `(1+e)/(1−e) = 1.51762` (0.009 %). Venus 1.01380 vs 1.01369; Earth 1.03414 vs 1.03397.
- **Orbital elements recovered from the live readouts.** Mercury `r ∈ [0.308, 0.467]` vs `a(1∓e) = [0.30751, 0.46669]`; recovered a = 0.3875 (+0.10 %), e = 0.2052 (−0.21 %) — both within 3-dp rounding. Earth `r ∈ [0.983, 1.017]`, `v ∈ [29.29, 30.29] km/s`, matching the real Earth's aphelion/perihelion speeds exactly.
- **Kepler III.** Mercury's measured wall-clock period 1.5041 s at 8× vs 1.5050 s predicted from `T = a^1.5` at 0.02 yr/s (**0.06 %**). Earth 6.212 s vs 6.25 s, Venus 3.819 s vs 3.844 s (the −0.6 % on the near-circular pair is the probe's minimum-detection resolution on a 3-dp `r`, not the sim).
- **Integrator is genuinely symplectic** (verbatim node replication of `step()`, 60 orbits per time-rate): relative energy drift **1.7e-14 (0.25×) → 4.5e-11 (8×)**, bounded with no secular growth; angular momentum conserved to **~1e-14** (machine precision, as expected for Verlet on a central force); eccentricity drift ≤1e-10. The Info panel's "no Euler drift" claim is honest. Note the sub-step floor `Math.max(8, …)` (L725) means h is *smallest* at 0.25–1× (4.2e-5 yr) and largest at 8× (1.9e-4 yr) — accuracy degrades with time rate, as designed.
- **Interaction walkthrough clean**, 11 states, `errors: []`: sim loads paused; `▶ Start motion` begins evolution; both `data-gate` prediction steps auto-pause (step 2 and step 4 both showed `▶ Play` on entry); answering resumes motion; step 4 auto-switches to outer view; planet-list clicks correctly repopulate a/e/r/v (Neptune → a 30.061 AU, e 0.009, v 5.48 km/s = its aphelion speed; Jupiter → 5.203 AU, 0.048, 13.70 km/s); Reset returns Mercury to r = 0.308 AU (perihelion) and re-pauses. (`interact.json`)
- **Geometry measured on canvas** at 1440×900 — outer view 11.62 px/AU, Sun corona 14 px: Mercury semi-major **4.5 px** (inside corona), Venus 8.4, Earth 11.6 (both inside corona), Mars 17.7. Inner view 206.49 px/AU: Mercury semi-major 79.9 px, apoapsis 96.4 px, major−minor **1.71 px**, Sun offset from ellipse centre 16.4 px.
