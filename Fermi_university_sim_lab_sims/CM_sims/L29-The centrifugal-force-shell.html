Guided Inquiry

1 · The apparatus

Let’s meet the apparatus. A carousel deck turns at a constant angular speed ω. A ball rests on the frictionless deck at radius r, held there by a backstop that turns with it. The same release can be filmed by a camera fixed to the ground, or by a camera bolted to the deck — and that choice will matter.

2 · The path after release

Let’s start with the ground camera. The deck turns at ω, and the backstop lets the ball go at radius r. Filmed from the ground, what path do you think the ball follows afterwards?

3 · Two cameras, one release

Now let’s bring in the second camera. Both cameras film the same release, so release the ball, then compare the cyan ground trail with the orange deck trail. Which features of the motion are identical in the two records, and which aren’t?

4 · The source of the outward push

Here’s the puzzle. On the deck camera, the released ball moves outward as though shoved. So which object do you think exerts that shove?

5 · Doubling the spin

Now let’s put a number on it. The ball is held at r = 2.0 m while the deck turns at ω = 1.00 rad/s. Then the deck speeds up to ω = 2.00 rad/s at the same radius. What do you think — the outward term in the deck frame becomes

6 · What makes a force fictitious

Let’s pull it all together. Every event and every real force is the same in both films; only the outward term appears or disappears with the choice of camera. A force with no source object and no reaction partner is called fictitious — here it’s mω²r, radially outward, in the deck frame alone.

The Physics

The centrifugal force · a complete revision sheet · ball on a merry-go-round

The centrifugal force isn't something the world does to the ball. It's the fee Newton charges for filming the world from a spinning deck.

01

Where does the outward shove come from?

One release, two films — both paths computed from the sim's defaults, ω = 1.50 rad/s, r₀ = 2.0 m. Dotted: the hold circle.
  • The ground camera is an inertial frame: after release nothing touches the ball, so it runs a straight tangent at constant speed. Newton's first law is the whole story.
  • The deck camera is bolted to the carousel. The same flight, re-filmed, is an outward spiral — the ball drifts out as if shoved.
  • Nothing new happened between the films. Only the camera changed.
Ground camera — zero horizontal force. The sim draws exactly this closed form; there is no integrator.
Deck camera — to keep F = ma you must book two frame terms. No object exerts either one.
02

Which way does it push — and who feels it?

  • Radially outward, along r̂, and growing with distance from the axis: F ∝ r. The sideways frame term is the other underbrace — Coriolis, which needs a deck-frame velocity.
  • It exists only in the deck frame's ledger: no source object, so no reaction partner — the fingerprint of a fictitious force.
  • While the ball is held, the ledger balances. The backstop's push N is the real force — inward, centripetal — and it is what actually keeps the ball on its circle.
Why the held ball sits still on the deck: the real inward push cancels the booked outward term.
03

How big is the bill?

ω r

These are the sim's real sliders — drag here and the carousel follows.

  • Double ω → 4× the force; double r → 2×. That is the sim's own inquiry number: at r = 2.0 m, speeding up from 1.00 to 2.00 rad/s takes the term from 2.00 N to 8.00 N — quadratic, not linear.
  • At the defaults the bill is 4.50 N at r₀, while held. After release r grows, and the booked mω²r grows with it — 7.65 N at the deck edge (r = 3.4 m). The violet label in the deck film keeps climbing.
04

Is it even a force?

The exact transform the sim applies, point by point — the orange spiral is the cyan straight line in rotated coordinates.
  • One point, both films (defaults; clock and axes start at release, ball on +x): at t = 0.5 s the ground film has the ball at (2.00, 1.50) m. Rotate by −ωt = −0.75 rad (−43°) and the deck film puts it at (2.49, −0.27) m: r = 2.50 m, already outside r₀ = 2 m and trailing the release spoke.
  • "Fictitious" is bookkeeping, not slander. Inside the deck frame, add mω²r and F = ma predicts every motion exactly.
  • The test for a real force is: name the object that exerts it. Friction? The deck is frictionless. The backstop? It pushed inward, then let go. Nothing is left to push outward.
  • Step off the carousel and the term is simply gone. That is the entire answer to "is it real?".
05

Where do you ride this carousel every day?

  • Earth turns once a day: Ω⊕ = 7.29×10⁻⁵ rad/s. At the equator the frame term is ≈ 0.034 m/s² — you weigh about 0.3% less than at the poles, and the planet bulges ~21 km to match.
  • A lab centrifuge runs the same law hard: ω ≈ 3000 rad/s at r = 10 cm books ω²r ≈ 9×10⁵ m/s² — roughly 90 000 g (first-order estimate).
  • In the ground film these are all one story: the contents fly straight while the spinning container curves away beneath them — a spin dryer is this sim with holes in the rim.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "Centrifugal force is a real outward interaction on the ball." Nothing exerts it — it lives only in the deck-frame ledger, with no reaction partner.
  • "Released, the ball flies out along the spoke." The ground film shows a straight tangent at v = ωr₀ — never a radial fling. An along-the-spoke path would need an outward force, and there is none.
  • "Centrifugal and centripetal are an action–reaction pair." They act on the same ball, in one frame only. Real pairs act on different bodies: ball pushes backstop, backstop pushes ball.
  • "If the rider feels flung, the force must be real." What the rider actually feels is the seat's real inward push. The outward "force" is the rider's spinning coordinates.
07

Ninety-second self-test

The deck stops turning (ω = 0) with the ball still held at r₀ = 2.0 m. What does the deck frame book now?

Nothing — F = mω²r = 0. With no spin the two cameras agree everywhere, and the backstop has nothing to hold against.

Double ω and halve r₀. What happens to Fcf? To the release speed v = ωr₀?

The force doubles: ω² contributes ×4, r contributes ×½. The release speed is unchanged: (2ω)(r₀/2) = ωr₀.

Which facts are identical in the two films of one release?

The events — release, deck-edge crossing, where the ball ends up — and every real force. Only the description differs: the path's shape and the fictitious terms belong to the camera, not the ball.
