Guided Inquiry

1 · The phase plane

Let’s find our way around. The phase plane is the lower panel of the scene: x across, p up. One point in it — the representative point — fixes the whole state, and H = p²/2m + U(x) is its energy. U(x) is drawn above it on the same x scale. Try it: click anywhere in the phase plane to place a point, then press Play and watch x and p both change while H holds still to three decimals.

2 · Which way round?

Choose the harmonic oscillator and place a point on the positive x axis. Before pressing play, predict which way the point will go round its ellipse.

3 · Inner or outer first?

Still with the oscillator, place one point on the positive x axis close to the origin and another further out on the same axis, and start them together. Which one gets back to the x axis first?

4 · Below the hump

Choose the double well and place a point at rest near the bottom of the left-hand well, so that its energy is below the top of the hump. What does its trace look like, and what does the whole set of contours at that energy look like?

5 · A trace that never closes

Choose the pendulum and raise the energy until the trace no longer closes but runs right across the plane, staying above the θ axis all the way. What is the bead doing?

6 · Lines that seem to cross

Choose the inverted hill and switch on the contours. Two of them are straight lines crossing at the origin. How does this fit with the rule that two distinct trajectories can never cross?

7 · Carry it further

Here’s one to carry further on your own. Switch on Cloud mode and drop 60 points at once, then set Direction of time to Reversed. What do you think — why can no two points of the patch ever arrive at the same place?

The Physics

The phase plane · a complete revision sheet · Hamiltonian flow in (x, p)

A point in the phase plane isn't a position. It's the entire future of the system, written down in two numbers.

01

What does one point in this plane mean?

The sim's two storeys, drawn from its own defaults: U(x) above, the orbit it implies below.
  • Newton needs x and ẋ to predict the next instant. Hamilton uses x and p — the same information, in the coordinates that make the structure visible.
  • So a single dot fixes everything: no hidden state, no extra initial conditions. Drop it once and the whole trajectory is already determined.
  • Height above the well is momentum: the gap between E and U(x) is exactly p²/2m. Where the curve meets the energy line, p = 0 — a turning point.
H is the energy, and it does not change along the motion — so every trajectory is stuck on one contour of H.
02

Which way does the flow run?

Two equations, one rule: the horizontal drift is the momentum, the vertical push is the slope of the well.
  • Above the axis p > 0, so ẋ > 0 — the point moves right. Below it, left. That alone forces the circulation to be clockwise.
  • At the sides ẋ = 0 but ṗ = −dU/dx ≠ 0: the point is momentarily at rest in space yet still being pushed. That is the turning point, not a stopping point.
  • Every point of the plane has exactly one arrow. Trajectories therefore never cross — two crossing curves would need two futures from one state.
03

How long does one loop take?

The period is the time to cross the well and come back — and for a perfect parabola the amplitude cancels out.
h

This is the sim's own Double-well hump slider — drag it here and the sim switches to the Double well and follows.

  • For the harmonic well the integral collapses to T = 2π/ω, independent of amplitude — big swings and small swings share a period. The sim's default Oscillator has ω = 1 rad/s, so every ellipse takes 2π/ω = 6.28 s (its Clock reads 6.2832 s). That is special, not general.
  • For the double well it is not: near the bottom of one lobe the curvature is U″(±a) = 8h/a², so small oscillations run at ω = √(8h/m).
04

What is conserved besides energy?

Liouville's theorem: hand the flow a patch of states and it may stretch and shear it arbitrarily — but never change its area.
  • Release a cloud of nearby starting points in the double well and it deforms into a filament. It gets longer and thinner in exactly compensating measure. (On the Oscillator every orbit shares one period, so the patch just circles round unchanged.)
  • This is why phase space, not configuration space, is the natural home of statistical mechanics: counting states is counting area, and the flow cannot create or destroy it.
  • It is also the sharp statement of reversibility: run time backwards and the filament folds exactly back into the patch it came from.
05

Where does the plane change character?

The double well at the sim's default barrier: two centres, one saddle, and the single curve that separates trapped from free.
Both partial derivatives vanish at a fixed point — a state that never moves.
  • A centre sits at the bottom of a well: nearby orbits circle it. A saddle sits on a hilltop: nearby orbits sweep in and back out.
  • The separatrix is the one contour through the saddle. Inside it you are trapped in a single well; outside, the same energy carries you over both. Cross it and the qualitative motion changes, not just the numbers.
  • The pendulum shows the same anatomy: swinging orbits inside, whirling orbits outside, and the separatrix is the swing that just barely reaches the top.
  • On the Inverted hill (λ = 1 s⁻¹) a point on an incoming line gets 10× closer to the saddle every tdecade = ln 10/λ = 2.30 s — and never arrives. Drop one on a crossing line and the Clock says so: "Ten times closer costs 2.3026 s".

06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "The phase plane is just position against time." No — time is nowhere on either axis. The horizontal axis is x, the vertical is p, and time is how fast you travel along the curve.
  • "A closed loop means the particle returns to where it started." It returns to the same state, which is stronger: same place and same momentum. That is what makes the motion periodic.
  • "Trajectories can cross if the energies differ." Never. One arrow per point, so one future per point — different energies simply lie on different contours.
  • "Turning point means it stops." Only its velocity vanishes, for an instant. The force ṗ = −dU/dx is not zero there, so p keeps changing and the motion reverses.
07

Ninety-second self-test

A trajectory is a closed loop. What does that tell you?

The motion is periodic — the system returns to the same x and the same p, so the next lap is identical. Open curves (the free particle, a whirling pendulum) never repeat.

Why can two trajectories never cross?

Hamilton's equations give exactly one (ẋ, ṗ) at every point, so each state has a unique future. A crossing would mean one state with two futures.

You double the barrier height h of the double well. What happens to the separatrix?

It sits at E = h, so it moves up in energy, and the lobes grow: their height is p = √(2mh), so doubling h stretches them by √2. The two centres stay at x = ±a — the well's width is set by a, not by h.
