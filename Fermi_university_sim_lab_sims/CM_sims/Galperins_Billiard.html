About: Galperin's Billiard — π from Elastic Collisions

The setup

An immovable wall on the left, a 1 kg block at rest, and a heavy block of mass 100N kg sliding leftward at constant speed. The floor is frictionless; every collision is perfectly elastic.

The remarkable result (Galperin, 2003)

Count every collision — block–block and block–wall alike — until the heavy block is finally moving right at least as fast as the light block (no further collision is possible). The total is the first N+1 digits of π:

N = 0
3 collisions
N = 1
31 collisions
N = 2
314 collisions
N = 3
3141 collisions

Why event-driven?

Between collisions each block moves at constant velocity, so the next collision time can be solved analytically. As N grows the collisions become arbitrarily rapid — fixed-Δt stepping would miss them. We jump event-to-event instead.

Why π?

In (√m₁·v₁, √m₂·v₂) coordinates, elastic collisions become reflections of a point off two lines meeting at angle θ = arctan(√(m₁/m₂)). The total number of reflections is Π = ⌈π/θ⌉ − 1 — the number of multiples of θ that fit strictly inside π. That equals ⌊π/θ⌋ except when π/θ is an exact integer, as at N = 0 (θ = π/4, so Π = 3, not 4). For m₂/m₁ = 100N, √(m₁/m₂) = 10⁻ᴺ; for large N, θ ≈ 10⁻ᴺ and Π = ⌊π·10ᴺ⌋ — the first N+1 digits of π.

1 · Blocks, a wall and elastic impacts

Here’s the whole setup. A light block of mass m₁ rests on a frictionless floor, sitting between an immovable wall and a heavy block of mass m₂ sliding towards it.

Every impact — block on block and block on wall — is perfectly elastic, so the total kinetic energy never changes. Your job sounds simple: count every impact until no further contact is possible.

2 · Equal masses

Let’s start with the easiest case: the two blocks have the same mass. How many collisions do you think happen in total?

3 · A hundred times heavier

Now let’s make it lopsided. The heavy block is now 100 times the light one, and nothing else changes. Compared with the 3 collisions you just counted, how many do you expect now?

4 · Ten thousand times heavier

Let’s push it further. The mass ratio is now 10 000. The counts so far are 3 and 31. What do you think comes next?

5 · What the count measures

Here’s a lovely way to see what’s going on. Rescale the state to (√m₁·v₁, √m₂·v₂). Energy conservation pins that point on a fixed circle, and each impact reflects it off one of two lines meeting at a wedge angle θ. So what do you think the count equals?

Step 5 — Why?

For m₂/m₁ = 100N, √(m₁/m₂) = 10⁻ᴺ; for large N, θ ≈ 10⁻ᴺ, so Π = ⌊π·10ᴺ⌋ — the first N+1 digits of π. Open ⚛︎ The Physics for the equations.

The Physics

Galperin's billiard · a complete revision sheet · π from elastic collisions

π isn't hiding in the masses. It's hiding in the angle between two mirrors — and every elastic collision is a bounce off one of them.

01

Why do they keep bouncing?

Left: the apparatus at reset. Right: the same N = 0 run as three reflections on the energy circle.
  • An immovable wall, a 1 kg block at rest, and a heavy block of mass 100N kg sliding in. Frictionless floor; every impact perfectly elastic.
  • The light block is trapped between the wall and the heavy block, and each hit takes only a sliver of the heavy block's momentum. At N = 1 (v0 = 1.00 m/s) the first hit sends the 1 kg block toward the wall at 1.98 m/s and slows the 100 kg block only from 1.00 to 0.98 m/s. It takes 15 bangs (8 block–block, 7 wall) to turn the heavy block around.
  • Count every bang — block–block and block–wall — until the heavy block is receding at least as fast as the light one.
  • That halt is not friction. The energy is still there; the heavy block has simply become uncatchable.
Block–block: conserves momentum and kinetic energy.
Wall bounce: the wall is infinite mass, so v1 just flips sign.
02

What is the count actually counting?

Rescale velocities by √m. Energy conservation becomes a circle of fixed radius.
  • In coordinates (√m1 v1, √m2 v2) each impact is a reflection off one of two lines that meet at angle θ = arctan √(m1/m2).
  • Unfold the wedge: the motion is a straight line through copies of θ. Each crossing is one collision.
  • Count the mirror lines at θ, 2θ, 3θ, … that lie strictly between 0 and π. That number is the count. At θ = π/4 these are 45°, 90°, 135°: 3.
N = 0 computed: θ = π/4, a horizontal path crosses three mirrors, then escapes.

04

Why doesn't the speed matter?

Halt is a statement about the velocity ordering, not the size.
  • Scale every velocity by k and the movie just plays faster. The sequence of events is unchanged.
  • Once v1 ≥ 0 and v2 ≥ v1, the light block is not heading at the wall and cannot catch the heavy one from behind.
  • Only the mass ratio sets θ. Only θ sets Π. That is why the slider hint says the count is independent of v0.
  • Two runs at N = 1: v0 = 0.50 m/s carries KE = 12.5 J; v0 = 2.00 m/s carries 200 J, 16× as much. Both counters stop at 31. The fast run just finishes 4× sooner.
05

Who counted π this way?

  • Galperin, 2003. For m2/m1 = 100N, √(m1/m2) = 10−N ≈ θ, so Π = ⌊π · 10N⌋ — the first N+1 digits of π.
  • N = 0, 1, 2, 3 give 3, 31, 314, 3141. The sim stops at N = 3 because 3141 events is already a blizzard.
  • Not a practical π algorithm: N = 10 is a 1020 kg block. The identity is geometric, not a race against Chudnovsky.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "They bounce forever because nothing loses energy." Halt is kinematic, not dissipative — the heavy block becomes uncatchable.
  • "The count is the mass ratio." 100× mass → 10× collisions, not 100. The count grows as √(m2/m1).
  • "Just take floor(π/θ)." At N = 0, π/θ = 4 exactly and floor would give 4; the correct count is ⌈π/θ⌉ − 1 = 3.
  • "Faster in, more collisions." v0 never enters θ. It only sets the kinetic energy and the clock.
07

Ninety-second self-test

At N = 0, π/θ = 4 exactly. Why is the collision count 3, not 4?

Because Π = ⌈π/θ⌉ − 1, not ⌊π/θ⌋. When π/θ is an integer the last “fit” is the escape, not a collision — so you subtract one. For N ≥ 1, π/θ is not an integer and the two formulas agree.

Go from N = 1 to N = 2. What happens to the count?

It jumps from 31 to 314 — about ten times, not a hundred. Each extra two zeros on the mass ratio (×100) multiply √(m2/m1) by 10, which is one more digit of π.

Why is the count independent of v0?

θ = arctan √(m1/m2) knows only the mass ratio. Scaling all speeds by k scales the movie in time, not the sequence of reflections, so Π is unchanged.
