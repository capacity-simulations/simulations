The Physics

The energy of the orbit · a complete revision sheet · one curve, one number

An orbit isn't chosen out in space. It's chosen on one curve — draw Veff, place the line E, and ellipse, parabola, or hyperbola is already decided.

01

How does a 2D orbit become 1D motion?

One energy, two pictures — the E = −0.35 orbit the sim draws, and the energy diagram it is read from. The green bar is the radial KE ½ṙ² at r = 1.
  • Fix the angular momentum (the sim's State panel: l = 1, never changing) and the planar orbit collapses to one coordinate: r.
  • The radial motion is exactly a marble rolling in Veff(r) — gravity's −1/r plus the centrifugal term l²/2r².
  • Everything the orbit panel shows is read off the energy panel: same E, same turning points, same speeds.
The one curve. In the sim's units GM = m = 1 with l = 1 — exactly the curve in its right panel.
Radial KE cannot go negative — the body lives only where the E line sits above the curve.
02

Who decides the shape — and when?

  • The sign of E is the whole story: below zero the E line cuts Veff twice (trapped), at zero it just escapes, above zero it never comes back.
  • The decision is made at launch — E is conserved, so the regime can never change mid-flight.
  • At the slider's left stop, E = Emin = −1/(2l²) = −0.50: the line touches the well bottom — a circle at r = 1, with e = 0.
The sim's three preset energies on one curve — every dot is a computed crossing E = Veff.
03

What does the energy slider actually set?

Eccentricity, size, and turning points — all from E alone (l fixed at 1; bound case shown).
E

This is the sim's real energy slider — drag here and the orbit follows.

  • Raise E toward 0 → e → 1: the ellipse stretches. rapo = a(1+e) grows like −1/E, while rperi only creeps toward l²/2 = 0.5.
04

Why doesn't it fall in — or stop at the turning points?

  • As r → 0 the barrier l²/2r² grows faster than gravity (r⁻² beats r⁻¹): with any l ≠ 0, getting close costs more energy than gravity supplies. The body cannot reach the Sun.
  • At perihelion and aphelion ṙ = 0, not v = 0: the green bar closes, but the angular sweep continues — the body turns around in r and keeps orbiting.
The sim's θ̇(r) panel is this law drawn as a curve — the marker rides it as r changes.
  • θ̇ is largest at perihelion — the same l that walls off the Sun makes the close approach the fastest part of the orbit (Kepler's second law in disguise).
05

Where does the sky run this diagram?

The E = 0 threshold in SI clothes — that is all "escape velocity" means.
  • Every planet sits at E < 0 — closed ellipses, most nearly circular (Earth: e ≈ 0.017).
  • Long-period comets ride just under E = 0 with e ≈ 1: millennia crawling near aphelion, weeks whipping past perihelion — θ̇ = l/r² at astronomical scale.
  • ʻOumuamua (2017) arrived with E > 0, e ≈ 1.2: one perihelion pass, no outer turning point, gone for good.
  • Leave Earth faster than 11.2 km/s and your E in Earth's well turns positive — you are the hyperbola.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "Given time, an orbit spirals into the Sun." E and l are conserved — the inner turning point is a wall, not a slope. Spiralling in needs drag or thrust, neither of which exists here.
  • "At aphelion the planet stops." Only ṙ vanishes there; θ̇ = l/r² never does.
  • "Veff is the potential energy." The l²/2r² term is kinetic — the angular part ½r²θ̇² rewritten with l. Only −1/r is true potential.
  • "E = 0 means no motion." It is the escape threshold: the body moves everywhere, it just arrives at infinity with nothing to spare.
07

Ninety-second self-test

Slide E to the left stop, −0.50. What orbit do you get — and why can't E go lower?

e = √(1 + 2E) = 0: a circle at r = 1. Any lower and the E line would sit below the entire curve — negative radial KE, impossible. Emin = −1/(2l²) is the floor of the well.

Go from E = −0.35 to E = −0.175. What happens to the ellipse?

a = −1/(2E) doubles (1.43 → 2.86) and e climbs from 0.55 to 0.81 — bigger and more stretched. rapo = a(1+e) jumps from about 2.2 to about 5.2, while rperi barely moves.

The green bar in the Veff panel keeps changing height. What is it, and where does it vanish?

It is the radial kinetic energy ½ṙ² = E − Veff(r) — the gap between the line and the curve. It vanishes exactly at the two coloured dots: perihelion and aphelion, the radial turning points.
