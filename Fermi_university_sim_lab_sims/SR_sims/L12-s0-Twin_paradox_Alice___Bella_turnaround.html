Guided Inquiry

1 · Alice, Bella and the corridor

Alice stays at x = 0 while Bella runs to a marker a distance L away at speed v, turns, and runs back. Both clocks are set to zero at the start event and read out again at the single reunion event.

Natural units: c = 1, so distances are light-years and times are years. τ denotes proper time — what a clock carried along a worldline itself records.

2 · The two readings at reunion

Bella runs out at v = 0.80c, turns at the marker, and runs back. At the reunion the two clocks are held side by side. How do the readings compare?

Press Play to run the trip in Alice's frame: τA = 10.00 yr, τB = 6.00 yr, a gap of 4.00 yr.

3 · What breaks the mirror

Along each straight leg, each twin measures the other's clock running slow — that much really is symmetric. Yet the reunion readings are not equal. What breaks the symmetry?

4 · The turnaround, in Bella's frame

The scene is now drawn in Bella's frame: she is at rest and the corridor slides past. At the instant she swaps her outbound frame for the inbound one, what happens to Alice's clock?

Press Play and watch Alice's clock at the switch; the dashed ghost shows the no-jump bookkeeping.

5 · A longer trip, the same turn

Hold v at 0.80c and double Alice's trip time ΔtA from 10 yr to 20 yr. The turnaround itself is identical. What happens to the age difference at reunion?

Set ΔtA to 20 yr with v unchanged and re-run.

6 · Maximal ageing

Between the start event and the reunion, Alice's straight worldline carries the greatest proper time any worldline can carry, and Bella's bent one carries less — the principle of maximal ageing.

The turnaround selects Bella's path rather than causing the difference; in her frame it is the sweep of simultaneity that supplies the time the leg-by-leg bookkeeping misses. At the current settings τA = 10.00 yr and τB = 6.00 yr.

The Physics

Lecture 12 · The twin paradox · proper time along a worldline

The twin paradox isn't two opinions that can't both be right. It's two paths between the same pair of events — and in spacetime, the straight one is the long one.

01

What are we actually comparing?

Alice's frame S, drawn from the sim's own defaults: v = 0.80c, ΔtA = 10 yr.
  • Two events, not two opinions: the start (both clocks zeroed, same place) and the reunion (both clocks side by side, same place). Every frame agrees on what the two faces read there.
  • What a clock records between them is its proper time τ along its own worldline — not a number anybody else assigns it.
  • Alice never leaves x = 0, so her proper time is her frame's coordinate time: τA = ΔtA. Bella's worldline bends, and a bent worldline is shorter.
Proper time is the length of a worldline. Natural units, c = 1: distances in light-years, times in years, metric signature (+, −).
Each of Bella's legs is at constant speed, so the integral collapses to a division by γ.
02

Why isn't the story symmetric?

  • On each straight leg it really is symmetric. Alice measures Bella's clock slow by 1/γ; Bella measures Alice's slow by exactly the same 1/γ. That part of the mirror is genuine.
  • What is not symmetric is the count of inertial frames. Alice uses one from start to reunion. Bella needs two — S′ going out and S″ coming back — because she turns.
  • "Who is moving?" is frame-dependent and settles nothing. "Whose worldline is straight?" is frame-independent, and it settles everything.
γ is fixed by v alone — not by who calls whom moving.
The same trip in Alice's coordinates. Bella's line of "now" swings across Alice's worldline at the turn, from 1.80 yr to 8.20 yr.
03

How much younger does Bella come back?

The gap: how long the trip lasts, times how much slower her clock runs.
v ΔtA

These are the sim's own two controls — drag here and the corridor follows.

  • γ does all the work; ΔtA only scales it. At fixed v, doubling the trip doubles the gap.
  • Push v toward c and the gap runs toward the whole trip — but τB never reaches zero for a runner. Only light has zero proper time.
04

Is the turnaround what makes the difference?

The simultaneity jump, in Bella's frame. L = v·ΔtA/2 is the marker distance measured in Alice's frame.
  • No — acceleration selects the path, it does not create the gap. Hold v and stretch the trip: the same turn, twice the gap. Δτ = ΔtA(1 − 1/γ) is strictly proportional to ΔtA.
  • In Bella's own reckoning the turn is where the missing time shows up. Leg by leg she grants Alice only 2 × 1.80 = 3.60 yr — the sim draws that as the dashed "no-jump" ghost — and the 6.40 yr jump at the switch closes the gap to exactly 10.00 yr.
  • Nothing happens to Alice at that instant. Her clock ticks steadily throughout. What changes is which of Alice's ticks Bella calls "now" — the swing drawn in chapter 02.
  • The frame-independent statement is maximal ageing: between two events, the unaccelerated worldline carries the most proper time. Every bend costs.
05

Where does nature run this trip?

For v ≪ c the gap is first order in v²/2 — tiny, and measured daily.
  • GPS satellites orbit at about 3.9 km/s, so their clocks lose 7.2 μs a day from motion alone. The gravitational term runs the other way and is larger; both are engineered in.
  • Cosmic-ray muons are born ~15 km up with a 2.2 μs mean life — 660 m of travel at c. At γ ≈ 20 they reach sea level in quantity; in the muon's own frame it is the atmosphere that is contracted.
  • Stored muons at CERN (γ = 29.3) confirmed the dilated lifetime to about 0.1%, and flown atomic clocks (Hafele–Keating, 1971) closed the loop at the scale of hundreds of nanoseconds.
  • Real twins: at a runner's 5 m/s, even 80 years of non-stop running buys about 0.3 μs. The corridor here runs at 0.80c because nothing slower is visible.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "It's symmetric — each sees the other's clock slow, so the readings must match." Symmetric on each leg, yes. But only Bella changes inertial frames, so the two accounts are not mirror images. Compare worldlines, not opinions.
  • "The acceleration is what makes Bella younger." It only selects her path. Same turn, longer trip, twice the gap — the difference accumulates along the legs, not at the corner.
  • "Alice really ages 6.40 yr in an instant." Nothing happens to Alice. The jump is a change in Bella's definition of "now", not in Alice's ticking.
  • "Bella's frame is as good as Alice's, so she can call Alice the younger one." On each leg, yes. At the reunion, no — there both clocks sit at one event, and their readings are facts every frame agrees on.
07

Ninety-second self-test

Bella strolls out and back at v → 0. What do the two clocks read at the reunion?

The same. γ → 1, so τB → ΔtA. Her worldline still has a corner in it, but Δτ = ΔtA(1 − 1/γ) vanishes with v, not with the turn — which is the whole point about acceleration.

Keep v = 0.80c and double ΔtA from 10 yr to 20 yr. What happens to γ, to τB, and to the gap?

γ is set by v alone, so it stays 1.667. τB doubles to 12.00 yr and the gap doubles to 8.00 yr. The turn itself — the same change of velocity — is unchanged; the marker simply moves out to L = 8.00 ly.

In Bella's frame Alice is the one moving. Why isn't Alice the younger twin?

Because "who moves" is frame-dependent and proper time is not. Bella's account needs two inertial frames, and the 6.40 yr simultaneity jump between them is exactly what makes her own bookkeeping return τA = 10.00 yr at the reunion.
