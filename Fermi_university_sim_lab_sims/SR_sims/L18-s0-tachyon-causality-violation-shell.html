Concept
A signal from A ("A sends") to B ("B receives") outside A's light cone must be superluminal (v > c). For such a spacelike pair a boost reverses the time-order (Δct′ flips sign), and a superluminal return signal then arrives before A sent — effect precedes cause.
Serves
Causal structure of the light cone (timelike vs spacelike); superluminal signalling ⇒ frame-dependent time order; the acausal round-trip loop; why tachyons would need m² < 0; the deep link to the relativity of simultaneity (tilted S′ axes).
Inquiry
When does the A→B signal need v > c? At which boost does Δct′ change sign? Can the return signal reach A before it sent — and what would that mean?
Misconception
"A fast-enough signal is fine — going faster than light just means arriving sooner; it can't break cause and effect."

Guided Inquiry

1 · Two events, two frames

Let’s meet the two events. Event A at (x, ct) = (0.00, 0.00) sends a signal received at event B at (3.21, 4.70). Their separation is Δs² = Δct² − Δx², positive when B lies inside A's light cone and negative when it lies outside. A second observer S′ moves at v/c relative to S and, for the same two events, measures Δct′ = γ(Δct − (v/c)·Δx).

2 · Order inside the cone

Let’s start with a signal slower than light. B lies inside A’s light cone: Δs² = +11.79, and the A→B signal travels at 0.68 c. Taking every frame with |v/c| < 1, what do you think becomes of the order of A and B?

3 · Outside the cone

Now let’s push B out past the edge. B now sits outside A’s light cone at (5.00, 2.00): Δs² = −21.00, and the A→B signal travels at 2.50 c. Look at Δct′ = γ(Δct − (v/c)·Δx): what do you think that has changed?

4 · Moving B further out

Let’s see how far that goes. For this pair Δct′ passes through zero at v/c = 0.40. Drag B further from A along x, holding Δct at +2.00. What do you expect to happen to the boost at which the order reverses?

5 · The reply

Now for the twist: a reply. At v/c = 0.60 the order has reversed: in S′, B receives before A sends. B now answers at v₂ = 3.00 c in S′, meeting A’s worldline at E₃ with ct₃ = 0.33 — still above A. What do you think would bring ct₃ below A?

6 · The antitelephone

Let’s step back and see what you’ve found. A signal crossing a spacelike interval has no frame-independent direction in time — that is the content of Δs² < 0, and the tilted S′ lines of constant time are where it shows. Add a reply and the round trip lands on the sender's own worldline before the sending event: the tachyonic antitelephone. The light cone bounds causal order, not merely speed.

And one last thing to think about: a particle permanently above c — a tachyon — would need m² < 0 in E² = p²c² + m²c⁴.

The Physics

Causality & tachyons · a complete revision sheet · two events on a Minkowski diagram

The light cone isn't a speed limit. It's the boundary of "before" — step outside it and no frame-independent order survives.

01

What does the light cone actually divide?

Both pairs are the sim's own: reset gives B at (3.21, 4.70), the guided inquiry moves it to (5.00, 2.00). Same sender A, same cone.
The sim's sign convention: Δs² = Δct² − Δx², so timelike is positive. The course notes write ΔS² = −c²Δt² + Δx² — the same statement with every sign flipped. What matters is which side of zero, not which convention.
  • Δs² is the one number both frames agree on. Its sign labels the pair: inside the cone (timelike), on it (null), outside it (spacelike).
  • The sim reads the connecting signal's speed straight off the pair: v = |Δx / Δct|. Inside the cone that is under c; outside, it is over c by construction.
  • So "faster than light" and "outside the light cone" are the same statement about A and B — not two separate claims.
  • The cone is drawn at A, the sender. Causal questions are always asked relative to one event.
02

Why can a boost reorder anything at all?

The course's Lorentz transformation, written with c = 1 as the sim's axes are. S′ is the frame moving at v/c relative to S.
B's line of constant ct′ at three boosts. The line sweeps through A at v/c = 0.40 — that is the sim's ⚠ order-flipped tag appearing.
  • An observer's "now" is a line of constant ct′, and boosting tilts it. Whether A is before B is just which side of B's tilted now-line A falls on.
  • γ is positive for every |v/c| < 1, so γ can never flip a sign — only the competition between Δct and (v/c)·Δx decides.
  • This is the relativity of simultaneity from the earlier lectures, pushed until it changes an order rather than just a clock reading.
  • Nothing here is about the signal. The transformation acts on the two events; whatever ran between them is irrelevant to the arithmetic.
03

How much boost does it take?

The number the sim prints as Δct′ in the Frame S′ readings.
Reversal needs (v/c)·Δx to overtake Δct while |v/c| stays under 1 — possible only when |Δx| > |Δct|, i.e. only outside the cone, and even then only for boosts past βflip. At v/c = 0 a spacelike pair keeps its order.

04

How does a reversed order become a loop?

The sim's own reply construction: B answers at v₂ measured in S′, and the arrival E₃ is transformed back to S. The threshold form assumes A sits at x = 0, where the sim places it.
v/c = 0.80, reply at v₂ = 3.00 c in S′. E₃ arrives at ct₃ = −1.18 — on A's own worldline, below the sending event. In S the reply leg runs at 1.57 c.
  • A reversed order alone is not yet a contradiction: at v/c = 0.60 with v₂ = 3.00 c the sim still reports ct₃ = +0.33, after A sent.
  • The loop needs the reply to outrun the boost: for this pair it closes at v/c ≈ 0.65, and a faster reply lowers that threshold.
  • The gap between the flip boost and the loop boost is −Δs² / (Δx(v₂Δx + Δct)) — it shrinks to zero as v₂ → ∞. An infinitely fast reply makes reversal and loop the same condition.
  • E₃ and A lie on one worldline, so their order is timelike and absolute. That is why ct₃ < ctA is a contradiction and not a matter of viewpoint.
05

What would a tachyon have to be?

Straight from the course derivation: v = pc²/E, so v > c forces pc > E, and the invariant m² of the four-momentum goes negative.
  • A permanently superluminal particle needs m² < 0 — an imaginary rest mass. Nothing else in the four-momentum is left to adjust.
  • That is not a manufacturing problem. It is the same statement as chapter 01: a worldline outside the cone has a spacelike four-momentum.
  • Nothing has ever been seen above c. The 2011 OPERA neutrino result — about 60 ns early over 730 km — was traced to a loose fibre connector and a clock offset, and withdrawn.
  • "Tachyonic" fields in quantum field theory (the Higgs before symmetry breaking) have m² < 0 but signal nothing: it marks an unstable vacuum, not superluminal transmission.
  • Entanglement correlates faster than any signal could, and still sends no message — the measurement outcomes are random until compared over a slower-than-light channel.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "A fast-enough signal is fine — going faster than light just means arriving sooner." It means arriving outside the cone, and then some frame has it arriving before it was sent. Speed is not the casualty; order is.
  • "Time dilation reorders events, so order is always relative." Only for spacelike pairs. Inside the cone (v/c)·Δx never overtakes Δct for any |v/c| < 1, so timelike order is absolute in every frame.
  • "γ blows up near v = c, so it flips the sign." γ > 0 always; it scales Δct′ and never reverses it. The sign lives entirely in Δct − (v/c)·Δx.
  • "The reversal is an illusion — S sees the true order." No frame is privileged. Δs² < 0 means the pair has no frame-independent order to be right about.
  • "Δs² < 0 is the spacelike case everywhere." Only in this sim's convention. Flip the metric signature and spacelike becomes positive — always check which sign convention a text uses before quoting a rule.
07

Ninety-second self-test

Δx = 0: A and B happen at the same place. Can any boost reverse them?

No. Δct′ = γ(Δct − (v/c)·Δx) = γ·Δct, and γ > 0, so the sign of Δct survives every boost. Same-place pairs are the most timelike pairs there are.

Double Δx at fixed Δct. What happens to the boost that reverses the order?

It halves: βflip = Δct/Δx. For the outside pair (Δct = +2.00), doubling Δx from 5.00 to 10.00 takes βflip from 0.40 to 0.20. The sim's card 4 moves B only to Δx = 7.00 — not a doubling — where βflip = 2/7 = 0.29.

In S the reply arrives before A sent. Another observer boosts. Can they restore the order?

No. E₃ and A sit on one worldline, so their separation is timelike and their order is frame-independent. That is exactly why the loop is a contradiction rather than a disagreement — and why a superluminal signal cannot exist.
