Guided Inquiry

1 · A clock made of light

Each clock is a pair of mirrors held a fixed distance h apart with a single light pulse bouncing between them; one round trip is one tick, and a clock at rest ticks in t₀ = 2h/c. 3 · How long the trip takes

That slanted path is longer than the straight 2h Alice's own pulse covers. In Alice's frame, how long does one round trip of Bella's pulse take, compared with t₀?

The γ and t readouts are live: t is Bella's tick as measured in Alice's frame.

4 · Across the motion

Lengths along the direction of motion do contract. The construction treats Bella's mirror separation — measured across the motion — as the same h Alice has. Does that separation contract as well?

The triangle is drawn now; its vertical leg is that unchanged h.

5 · Raising the speed

At v = 0.60 c one tick of Bella's clock takes 1.25 t₀ in Alice's frame. At v = 0.90 c, how long does one tick take?

Drag v toward c: γ reads 1.25 at 0.60 c, 2.29 at 0.90 c and 3.20 at 0.95 c.

6 · Pythagoras, and the name for it

Half a trip is a right triangle with legs h and vt/2 and hypotenuse ct/2, so (ct/2)² = h² + (vt/2)², and solving for t gives t = (2h/c)/√(1 − v²/c²) = γ t₀. The factor γ is the Lorentz factor, and t₀ — read by the one clock present at both the emission and the return — is the proper time.

The Physics

Light clock & the geometric derivation of time dilation · Lecture 11 · a complete revision sheet

A moving clock is not ticking badly. Its light simply has further to go, and c refuses to change — so the ticks arrive further apart.

01

What makes a pair of mirrors a clock?

Both clocks as Alice sees them, drawn at the sim's default v = 0.60 c: each half-trip lifts the pulse by h while the mirrors advance 0.75 h.
  • Two mirrors held a fixed distance h apart with one light pulse bouncing between them. One round trip is one tick, and a clock at rest ticks in t₀ = 2h/c.
  • Alice's clock stands still (cyan mirrors). Bella's identical clock is carried past to the right at v (amber mirrors, rose pulse path). Everything on the canvas is drawn as Alice sees it.
  • Riding along with her clock, Bella sees a plain vertical bounce and reads t₀. Alice sees the pulse lean, because the mirror that emitted it was already moving.
The tick of a clock at rest. One clock is present at both events — emission and return — so t₀ is a proper time.
02

Why is the moving pulse's path longer?

Pythagoras on the light triangle for one half-trip — the sim's own first formal line.
  • The pulse leaves a mirror that is already moving, and it keeps that sideways motion. Each leg is a straight slanted segment — nothing drags it back, because light has no medium.
  • The second postulate fixes the speed, not the distance: in Alice's frame the pulse covers that slant at exactly c. A longer path at the same speed takes longer.
  • The vertical leg is still h. Contraction acts along the motion only; if transverse lengths shrank, two equal rings sliding past each other would each have to claim the other passes inside it.
  • The base is the mirrors' own travel, vt/2 in a half-trip. Base along the motion, leg across it — the corner is a right angle, which is what lets Pythagoras in.
The sim's stage-4 construction, computed at v = 0.60 c: 0.75 h of travel, 1.25 h of light.
03

The Lorentz factor: 1 at v = 0, and it runs away as v approaches c. Both frames agree on its value.

04

Is the tick really longer, or only drawn longer?

The same answer from the invariant interval, in the lecture's sign convention s² = −c²Δt² + Δx².
  • The triangle is not an artefact of the drawing. Emission and return are two events, and every inertial frame agrees on the interval between them.
  • Bella's clock is present at both, so Δx′ = 0 and the interval collapses to her own elapsed time. That is exactly what proper time means.
  • Nothing inside the clock changed, and nothing about light clocks is special: any clock carried beside Bella must agree with hers, or she could detect her own uniform motion and break the first postulate.
  • Swap the frames and Bella says Alice's clock is the slow one. The effect is reciprocal, not one-sided.
And straight off the Lorentz transformation (Lecture 11a): set x′ = 0 on the moving clock's worldline. In the sim's labels, t is Alice's Δt and t₀ is Bella's Δt′.
05

Where does nature run this clock?

Cosmic-ray muons, the textbook case — both distances measured in the ground frame.
  • Muons. Mean lifetime τ₀ = 2.2 μs, measured at rest — 0.66 km at the speed of light, so they should die 14 km up. At γ = 20 the ground frame gives them 13.2 km and they arrive. In the muon's own frame nothing is dilated; the 15 km of air is contracted instead.
  • GPS. At 3.9 km/s a satellite clock loses about 7 μs per day to this effect alone. Gravity pushes the other way by 45 μs; the net +38 μs/day is corrected in orbit.
  • Everyday speeds. For v ≪ c, γ − 1 ≈ v²/2c². An airliner at 250 m/s accumulates roughly 11 μs of lag per year — measured by Hafele and Keating in 1971, and far too small to notice.
  • The sim's range is the honest one. At 0.60 c the tick is 25% longer; only past ~0.9 c does γ become dramatic.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "Moving clocks run slow — so to find which frame measures the proper time, just work out which one is moving." Neither frame is uniquely the moving one. Proper time belongs to the single clock present at both events — here Bella's, beside the pulse at emission and at return.
  • "Each sees the other's clock slow, so they contradict each other." Both are right, and they are comparing different pairs of events. Bringing the clocks back together to compare readings needs one of them to change frames — that is Lecture 12, not this one.
  • "The mirror separation h must contract too." Contraction is along the direction of motion only. Transverse shrinking fails the two-ring symmetry test, and if h changed the triangle would give a different answer.
  • "The light must travel faster along the slant to keep up." It cannot — c is the same in every inertial frame. That refusal is precisely what forces t above t₀.
07

Ninety-second self-test

Drag v to 0. What happens to the triangle and to γ?

The base vt/2 collapses to zero, the slant ct/2 falls onto the vertical leg h, and γ = 1/√1 = 1, so t = t₀. With no relative motion the two clocks tick together — the only answer consistent with neither frame being special.

At v = 0.60 c one tick takes 1.25 t₀. Does v = 0.90 c make it 1.5 × 1.25 = 1.88 t₀?

No. γ = 1/√(1 − 0.81) = 2.29, so the tick takes 2.29 t₀. γ depends on 1 − v²/c² under a square root: nearly flat at low v, then running away near c — 1.25 at 0.60 c, 2.29 at 0.90 c, 3.20 at 0.95 c.

Alice measures Δt = 10 s between two ticks of Bella's clock at v = 0.60 c. What does Bella's own clock read between the same two events?

Δt′ = Δt/γ = 10/1.25 = 8 s. Bella's clock is at both events, so it records the proper time. Alice needs two different clocks of her own lattice to time the same pair — which is why her figure is the larger one.
