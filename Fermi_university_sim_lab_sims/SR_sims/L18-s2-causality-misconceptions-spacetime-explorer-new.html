The Physics

Lecture 17 · event ordering and causality · spacetime explorer

Two frames disagreeing about which event came first is not a crack in causality. It is the price of asking the question about a pair that no signal could ever have joined.

01

What can E1 actually reach?

The sim at first load: E1 at the origin with its light wedge, E2 three units away in x but only one unit later in ct.
  • ct runs up the page and x runs across, at the scale that puts light on a 45° line. A point on this plane is an event; E1 and E2 are two of them, and either can be dragged.
  • Anything leaving E1 travels at c or less, so after cΔt it has covered at most Δx = cΔt. That is exactly the shaded wedge the dashed yellow lines enclose.
  • At the default pair the Readings print Δx = +3.00 against cΔt = +1.00 — three units of space for one of time. E2 is outside the wedge, so nothing that happened at E1 reaches it.
The reach condition — all of chapter 01 in one line.
The sim prints this as Δs² and turns its sign into the Classification badge.
02

Which pairs can a boost reorder?

The sim's own boost rows — the Frame S′ readings come straight out of these.
  • Switch on S′ axes and grid and a second, tilted pair of axes appears. The x′ axis is the set of events S′ calls simultaneous with E1, and its slope is v/c — the sim jumps to +0.60 the moment you turn it on.
  • Everything below the x′ line happens before E1 in S′; everything above it, after. Tilting x′ as far as slope 0.90 is the most this sim's boost can do.
  • The 45° light lines are steeper than any x′ line, so a point inside the wedge can never end up below one. Only a spacelike E2 can change sides.
The three causality presets against the x′ line at v/c = +0.60: Δs² = +8.00 timelike, 0 null, −8.00 spacelike.
Your lecture notes write the interval with the opposite overall sign, so there spacelike is the positive case. Same three cases — check which sign a question is using.
03

How hard do you have to boost to flip this pair?

v/c

This is the sim's real v/c slider — drag it here and S′ switches on and follows. cΔt and Δx are read live off the Readings panel, so dragging E1 or E2 moves these numbers too.

  • The threshold is a ratio, not a speed: it depends only on how the pair's cΔt compares with its Δx, never on how fast you can go.
04

Why can a timelike pair never flip?

Boost as hard as you like: cΔt′ bottoms out at the proper time between the two events, and never at zero.
  • Take the Timelike preset — cΔt = +3.00, Δx = +1.00, Δs² = +8.00. cΔt′ is smallest at v/c = Δx/cΔt = +0.33, where it reads +2.83, which is √8. Sweeping the slider from −0.90 to +0.90 runs cΔt′ from +8.95 down to that +2.83 and back up to +4.82 — never near zero.
  • That floor is cΔτ, the proper time a clock carried from E1 to E2 would show. It is positive and every frame agrees on it, so zero is unreachable.
  • A null pair (the Light-like preset) is the boundary case: cΔt′ = γ(1 − v/c)·cΔt stays positive for every |v/c| < 1, shrinking towards zero only as v/c → 1.
  • So every pair a signal could bridge keeps its order in every frame. That single sentence is causality in special relativity.
05

What if something could outrun light?

One superluminal A → B signal, drawn in S and then in S′ at v/c = +0.60 — the same arrow, now arriving before it left.
  • Suppose A could send a superluminal signal to B across a spacelike gap. In S, A is first. In S′ the same pair reads cΔt′ = −1.00, so B is first.
  • Let B reply the instant it receives, at the same 3c measured in S′, forward in S′ time. At v/c = 0.60 exactly, the reply lands back on A's send event; from a frame past 0.60, or with a reply faster than 3c, it reaches A before A sent the message.
  • A's sending and A's receiving sit on one worldline, a timelike pair, so every frame agrees which came first — effect before cause.
  • Kinematics closes the escape route: E = γmc² diverges as v → c, so no finite energy takes a massive particle up to c, let alone past it.
  • A particle with v > c would need pc > E, so m² < 0 — the tachyon of the lecture. None has ever been observed; the 2011 OPERA "faster-than-light neutrino" measurement was traced to a loose fibre-optic connector and a mis-timed clock.
A negative squared mass is what "faster than light" costs in the four-momentum invariant.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "If S and S′ disagree about which came first, one of them is wrong." Neither. Both are honest measurements and no inertial frame is privileged — they are only ever allowed to disagree about pairs no signal can link.
  • "A reversed order means an effect came before its cause." Only pairs with no possible causal link reverse. There is no cause and effect between them to put out of order.
  • "It only looks reversed — light takes time to arrive." cΔt′ is a coordinate difference in S′, not what an eye sees. The reversal is genuine there; arrival delay is a separate effect.
  • "Boost hard enough and any pair reverses." The threshold is v/c = cΔt/Δx. For a timelike or null pair that is at least 1 — not a physical frame, and well outside this sim's ±0.90 slider.
  • "Δs² changes when you boost." It is the one number both frames agree on, which is exactly why the Classification badge never changes as you move the slider.
07

Ninety-second self-test

E1 and E2 happen at the same place, so Δx = 0. Which boost reverses them?

None. The threshold v/c = cΔt/Δx is infinite, and cΔt′ = γ·cΔt keeps the sign of cΔt for every |v/c| < 1. A co-located pair has Δs² = (cΔt)² > 0 — timelike, so its order is fixed in every frame.

Hold cΔt at +1.00 and push E2 out from Δx = +3.00 to +6.00. What happens to the flip threshold?

It halves, from v/c = 1/3 ≈ +0.333 to ≈ +0.167 — the further out in space the pair sits, the gentler the boost that reorders it. Δs² drops from −8.00 to −35.00, so the pair is "more spacelike" too.

S says E1 first, S′ says E2 first. Which frame is telling the truth?

Both. Time order is frame-dependent for spacelike pairs. The frame-independent facts are Δs² = (cΔt)² − (Δx)² and the classification it gives, and neither changes under any boost.
