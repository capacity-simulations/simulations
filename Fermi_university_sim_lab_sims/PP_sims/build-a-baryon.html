About: Build-a-Hadron

Concept
Hadrons are colour-neutral quark bundles: baryons (qqq) and mesons (qq̅). Charge, isospin, strangeness, charm and bottomness are simple sums over constituents, tied together by the Gell-Mann–Nishijima relation Q = I₃ + ½(B+S+C+B̃+T).
Serves
Introductory particle physics / Standard Model units. Works before the formalism (build and discover) and after it (open The Physics to verify every build).
Inquiry
Which combinations are missing? Multi-heavy baryons (Ξcc⁺ — its partner Ξcc⁺⁺ was found by LHCb in 2017 — Ωcc⁺, Ωccc⁺⁺, Ξbb, Ωbbb…) are predicted but unseen: their cards estimate the 2mc² pair-production cost and what experiments must deliver (LHC luminosity, HL-LHC statistics). Top-quark hadrons can never form — the top decays (~5×10⁻²⁵ s) before hadronization (~10⁻²³ s).
Misconception
"Three identical quarks (uuu) would violate Pauli exclusion." Colour charge supplies the hidden quantum number: Δ⁺⁺ exists, and its existence is precisely what forced physicists to invent colour.

Guided Inquiry

1 · The hadron factory

Let’s start with the basics. A hadron is a bundle of quarks held together by the strong force. Each quark carries an electric charge — u, c and t at +⅔, d, s and b at −⅓ — so a hadron’s charge is just the sum over its constituents. Here’s your first build on screen: u u d — the proton, Q = +1.

2 · Three of a kind?

Now let’s push that a step further. The proton already holds two identical u quarks. Could a baryon hold three of them — u u u — when quarks are fermions and no two may occupy the same state? What do you think?

3 · White with only two?

So far, white has taken all three colours — yet two-quark hadrons exist too. How do you think a pair can be white?

4 · The strangeness ladder

Now for a little history. On screen: Ξ⁻ (d s s), Strange S = −2 — each s lowers S by one. By 1962, only S = −3 (s s s) was still unfilled. So can a gap in a pattern predict a particle? What do you think?

5 · Not yet ≠ never

One last puzzle. c c c breaks no rule: three colours, charge +2. Yet it’s never been seen, while a top quark can never form a hadron at all. What do you think separates the two cases?

Here’s something to leave you with: twelve of this catalogue’s ground-state baryons are predicted but still unseen, each priced by the 2mc² cost of making its quarks. Why not go and build a few?

The Physics

Build-a-Hadron, a complete revision sheet on quarks, colour and the quantum-number sums

A hadron isn't a new kind of matter. It's a sum over quarks — and colour is the rule that decides which sums nature will let you write.

01

What turns a handful of quarks into a hadron?

The sim's own slot layout and slot colours: left, the default build u u d; right, a meson: u d̄, the π⁺. Wiggly lines are gluons.
  • The strong force never lets a coloured object travel alone — confinement. Only a colour-neutral bundle gets out.
  • There are two ways to be neutral: three quarks carrying r, g and b (a baryon), or a quark paired with its own anticolour (a meson). That is the entire Mode selector.
  • The readout only prints "colour: r g b → white ✓" once every slot is filled. Half a hadron is not a hadron.
  • Colour is not a paint job — it is the charge of the strong force, and the gluons between the slots are what carry it.
The sim's own colour line: εijk picks the one antisymmetric r g b combination; the meson contracts a colour with its anticolour.
02

Where do the numbers in the readout come from?

No dynamics, no binding correction — every label in Current build is a plain sum over the filled slots.
  • An antiquark flips every sign: u carries Q = +⅔ and I₃ = +½, so ū carries −⅔ and −½. That is why a meson can be neutral in every label at once.
  • Only u and d carry isospin (I₃ = ±½). s, c, b and t all sit at I₃ = 0 and bring one heavy label each instead.
  • Mind the signs the sim uses: the s quark carries S = −1 and the b carries B̃ = −1, while c carries C = +1 and t carries T = +1.
  • Baryon number is the one label that ignores flavour entirely: +⅓ per quark, so 1 for any qqq and 0 for any qq̄.
The welcome hero, read as a ledger — the six flavours at their electric charge, each with the extra label it contributes.

  • Nothing here is an extra law. Y is just bookkeeping: add up B, S, C, B̃ and T, halve it, add I₃, and the electric charge falls out.

04

Same three quarks — so why two different particles?

What the Total-spin selector is choosing between. Same flavours; different alignment; different particle.
  • u u d with the spins opposed is the proton — 938.3 MeV, stable. The same u u d with all three aligned is the Δ⁺ — 1232 MeV, gone in 5.6×10⁻²⁴ s.
  • Charge, isospin, strangeness: identical. Only the spin state and therefore the mass and the lifetime change.
  • Two different S's. The sidebar note writes the total spin as "S = ½"; the S in the readout is strangeness. Don't let the letter fool you.
  • Three identical flavours — u u u, s s s, c c c — have no lowest-spin partner at all. Ask for one and the sim answers: "symmetry forces 3⁄2".
Quarks are fermions, so the whole state must be antisymmetric under swapping any two.
  • Colour is already antisymmetric (that is the ε), the ground state has no orbital motion, and identical flavours are symmetric — so the spin part must be symmetric, i.e. 3⁄2.
  • That is why Δ⁺⁺ (u u u) exists at all. Its existence is precisely what forced colour into the theory in the first place.
05

Which of these has nature actually made?

The ten spin-3⁄2 u/d/s states in this sim's catalogue, plotted at their I₃ and S. Right column: the row's mean mass.
  • In 1962 the bottom rung was empty. Gell-Mann read the ladder — roughly 150 MeV per unit of strangeness — and predicted an S = −3, Q = −1 particle near 1680 MeV.
  • Brookhaven found the Ω⁻ in 1964 at 1672.5 MeV, the value this sim carries. A symmetry named a particle before anyone saw one.
  • The frontier runs the other way: twelve of the 35 ground-state baryons in this catalogue are still ✧ predicted only, and each card prices it at 2mc².
  • Ωccc⁺⁺ (c c c) breaks no rule and has still never been seen — it needs three cc̄ pairs in one collision. The sim quotes its pair cost as 2mc² ≈ 9.6 GeV.
Top row: the Pair cost line on every ✧ card. Bottom row: why a top quark never binds at all.
  • Why an antibaryon? The strong force makes heavy quarks only in q q̄ pairs, so building c c c also makes c̄ c̄ c̄. In an e⁺e⁻ collision, which brings in no baryon number and no charm, their cheapest home is the antibaryon — so the collision energy √s must reach about 2mc².
  • It is a rough price, not a law: at the LHC the protons bring the baryon number, but the charm still has to be made in c c̄ pairs.
  • Top is the one true no-go. It decays in about 5×10⁻²⁵ s, roughly 20× faster than the strong force can bind anything, so the sim returns ⊘ no hadron — not "rare", impossible.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "Three identical quarks would violate Pauli." Pauli forbids identical states, not identical flavours — the three u carry r, g and b. Δ⁺⁺ exists, and it is why colour was invented.
  • "Any two quarks make a meson." r + g is still coloured and stays confined. Neutrality needs an anticolour, so a meson is always q q̄.
  • "π⁰ is literally u ū." The sim labels the slot u ū but its own card says the truth: π⁰ is the superposition (uū − dd̄)/√2. The same goes for η and η′.
  • "Not yet observed means forbidden." Only for the top. c c c is allowed and merely expensive; the sim separates ✧ predicted only from ⊘ impossible for exactly this reason.
07

Ninety-second self-test

A hadron with no strangeness, charm, bottomness or topness. What does Gell-Mann–Nishijima collapse to?

Y = B alone, so Q = I₃ + B/2. For a baryon that is Q = I₃ + ½ (proton: ½ + ½ = +1); for a meson B = 0, so Q = I₃ outright (π⁺: +1).

Take the neutron (u d d) and swap one d for an s. What changes, and what doesn't?

I₃ goes from −½ to 0 and S from 0 to −1, so Y drops from 1 to 0 — and Q = 0 + 0 = 0 is unchanged. Same charge, different particle: u d s is the Λ⁰ at 1115.7 MeV, 176 MeV heavier than the neutron because s is heavier than d.

The proton and the Δ⁺ are both u u d. What actually distinguishes them?

Only the total spin: ½ with the spins opposed, 3⁄2 with all three aligned. Every additive quantum number is identical; the alignment costs about 294 MeV and cuts the lifetime from "stable" to 5.6×10⁻²⁴ s.
