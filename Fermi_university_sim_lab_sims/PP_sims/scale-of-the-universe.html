The Physics

Smaller things need higher-energy probes · a complete revision sheet · Emin = ħc/d

Resolution is not an optics problem. Every probe is a wave, you cannot see detail finer than the wave you look with — and a shorter wave has a price, quoted in energy.

01

Why can't a better lens see smaller?

Both panels are the sim's stage, five target-widths across. Left: the matched probe the sim draws, reduced wavelength ƛ = λ/2π equal to d. Right: the sim's dashed ghost wave, ten times too long — the target is no longer distinguishable from a point.
  • A wave only reports structure that is bigger than about one wavelength. Finer detail averages out over a single oscillation and never imprints on the returning wave.
  • So the resolution condition is a statement about the probe, not the instrument: ƛ ≲ d. Polishing the glass cannot shorten the wave you sent in.
  • De Broglie converts that into a demand for momentum: ƛ = ħ/p, so resolving d needs p ≳ ħ/d. Momentum is the only dial.
The sim's first formal line: the reduced wavelength sets a momentum floor.
02

How steep is the price?

Treat the probe as massless (E = pc) and the momentum floor becomes an energy floor.
What the sim literally computes: the energy slider's position is the size slider's position, mirrored about log ħc.
  • One decade of size costs exactly one decade of energy. The two rulers under the stage are the same ruler, read in opposite directions — that is why the cursor drives both.
  • The product is fixed: d · Emin = ħc ≈ 0.1973 GeV·fm. Nothing in the sim ever leaves that hyperbola.
  • Halve d and Emin doubles; divide d by a thousand and Emin is multiplied by a thousand. No regime is exempt — the law is scale-free.
The sim's two locked rulers, drawn with the sim's own mapping and its default cursor: d = 1.70 m sits at Emin = 116 neV.
The invariant behind both rulers — ratios in, ratios out.

04

Is Emin the energy of a machine?

Same momentum, two different bills. The sim always quotes the first line.
  • It is a floor, not a price list. The relation is p ≳ ħ/d, so Emin is the least that could possibly work — the sim's readout is a bound you must clear, not a prediction of any experiment.
  • It is the momentum transfer qc in the target's rest frame, not a beam energy. How much a machine must actually carry to deliver q is a separate question the sim does not model.
  • The sim's probe is massless, so E = pc. Give an electron the same ƛ and it carries the same p, but its kinetic energy is (pc)²/2mc² — at d = 10−10 m that is 3.8 eV instead of 1.97 keV, about 500× cheaper. That is why electron microscopes exist, and it is arithmetic the sim leaves to you.
05

Where does nature hand the bill over?

The sim's four imaging bands on the size ruler, with the smallest Emin each band has to cover. Colours are the sim's own band colours.
  • Above a micron the bill is trivial — at the eye's 70 µm limit Emin is 2.8 meV, far below what any daylight photon already carries. Up there you are limited by optics, not by the wave.
  • The crossover is at about 100 nm, where Emin = 1.97 eV — the energy of a visible photon. That is exactly where the sim hands off from light microscope to electron microscope, and why a virus is where light gives up.
  • At the proton, 1.7 fm, Emin = 116 MeV; at the quark scale of 10−18 m it is 197 GeV — collider territory, where you reconstruct debris and never take a photograph.
The sim's third formal line, with the arithmetic written out.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "A better lens could see smaller." Resolution is set by the probe's wavelength, not by the lens. Only higher momentum shortens that wavelength.
  • "Just use a brighter beam." Brightness adds photons; each photon's wave is unchanged. A million soft photons still cannot resolve what one of them cannot.
  • "197 GeV is the machine's energy." It is the momentum transfer qc needed in the target's rest frame — a lower bound on the collision, not a spec sheet for the accelerator.
  • "λ and ƛ are the same thing." The sim draws the period ƛ = λ/2π. Using λ in ƛ ≈ d misplaces the resolution limit by a factor of 2π.
  • "Quarks are too small for any wave." No size is too small for a short-enough wave. The barrier is the energy that wave costs.
07

Ninety-second self-test

The ruler stops at 10−19 m. What does Emin = ħc/d say about d → 0?

Emin grows without bound: there is no smallest resolvable length in the formula, only an energy you cannot afford. The ruler ends where machines do — at 10−19 m the bill is already about 2 TeV.

You halve d and then halve it again. What happens to p and to Emin?

Both quadruple. p ≳ ħ/d and Emin = pc are strictly reciprocal in d, so any factor applied to d appears inverted in both — that is the single straight line the two rulers share.

Is Emin a property of the target or of the probe?

Of neither alone. It is a property of the interaction: the momentum transfer needed to resolve a length d. The same d demands the same q from any probe, but the energy that buys q depends on the probe's mass — massless, E = pc; an electron of the same ƛ pays only (pc)²/2mc².
