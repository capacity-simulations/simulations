About: Virtual Particle Collider

Concept
Head-on e⁺e⁻ collisions: the Lorentz-invariant energy √s = √((E₁+E₂)² − (p₁−p₂)²) is the creation budget — a particle pair is accessible only if √s ≥ 2m. Above threshold, the annihilation rate σ(e⁺e⁻→μ⁺μ⁻) = (4πα²/3s)·β(3−β²)/2·F_Z(s) rises from zero at √s = 2m_μ, then falls as 1/s through the photon — until the Z⁰ takes over: at √s = M_Z = 91.19 GeV the rate leaps ~200× (≈2 nb at Born level; LEP measured ≈1.5 nb) and then falls away again. The expected yield is N = σ·∫L dt·ε. The model is improved Born (γ/Z interference, no initial-state radiation). Threshold chips are labelled by the particles that emerge: BB̅ marks the open-flavour B⁺B⁻ threshold at 2m_B = 10.56 GeV; bound bb̅ already appears as the Υ(1S) resonance at 9.46 GeV, and tt̅ is a free-quark threshold because the top decays before it can hadronise.
Serves
Intro particle / modern physics units on colliders and cross-sections; works pre-formalism (thresholds unlock visually) and post-formalism via the ⚛︎ The Physics sheet. Numbers are engine-exact: √s = 10 GeV gives σ = 0.869 nb; 1 nb × 1 fb⁻¹ × ε = 0.9 gives 9.0×10⁵ events.
Inquiry
Open the muon channel at √s = 2 GeV → raise both beams to 10 GeV and watch reach grow while rate drops ×25 → compare 25+1 GeV vs 5+5 GeV (same √s = 10: asymmetry only buys a boost) → cross the W⁺W⁻ threshold at 161 GeV → plan a run: luminosity and efficiency set the count.
Misconception
“More energy ⇒ more events.” Wrong twice: (1) away from the Z⁰ pole this s-channel rate scales as 1/s — energy widens the menu of particles, luminosity buys statistics (the one exception, the Z⁰ resonance, is a narrow 2.5 GeV window at 91 GeV); (2) beam energies don’t simply add — unequal beams waste energy on center-of-mass motion, visible as E₁+E₂ − √s.
Decay prongs
Unstable products decay in flight: after the pair flies out, its track ends fan into short prongs tipped with dots — the secondaries of the decay. The sim draws 2 prongs for τ⁺τ⁻ and 3 for B⁺B⁻, W⁺W⁻, ZZ and tt̄; μ⁺μ⁻ tracks stay clean because muons cross the whole detector without decaying. The fan marks the decay vertex schematically — prong counts indicate “a few secondaries”, not the exact multiplicity of each real decay mode.

Guided Inquiry

1 · The creation budget

Let’s start with the budget. Two 1 GeV beams meet head-on, and √s = 2 GeV is the energy available to create new particles. Heavier pairs sit above that budget, at least for now.

2 · What does τ⁺τ⁻ cost?

Now let’s go looking for taus. The tau’s mass is 1.777 GeV, and e⁺e⁻ collisions create matter in particle–antiparticle pairs. So what do you think √s has to reach before taus can appear?

3 · Energy buys reach — and rate?

So energy buys reach — but does it buy rate too? Both beams are 5 GeV, so √s = 10 GeV and τ⁺τ⁻ is open. Now double each beam to 10 GeV. What do you think happens to the muon-pair count in the same run?

4 · Do beam energies add?

Let’s go back to 5 + 5 GeV, where √s = 10 GeV. Now spend 26 GeV unequally instead: 25 + 1 GeV. How much energy do you think is available to create new particles?

5 · Two separate accounts

So here’s the big idea. Energy sets what you can make. How many events you record is a separate account: the cross-section, the luminosity delivered, and the detector efficiency.

6 · Count events, not hopes

One last planning problem. At 5 + 5 GeV you record about 8×10⁵ muon pairs per fb⁻¹, and you need ten times more. Which change do you think delivers that?

The Physics

Virtual e⁺e⁻ collider · a complete revision sheet · energy budget, cross-section, event yield

A collision's energy isn't E₁ + E₂. It's what's left once the wreckage stops moving as a whole — and only that part can be turned into new matter.

01

What can this collision afford?

The sim's six threshold chips, with the sim's own masses. The dashed line is the default budget √s = 10.00 GeV: two pairs affordable, four out of reach.
  • An electron and a positron annihilate completely: charge, lepton number and every other quantum number cancel, so the pair can collapse into a single virtual photon — or a Z⁰ — carrying the whole invariant mass √s.
  • That lump of energy re-materialises as one particle and its antiparticle. A lone μ⁻ is impossible; you must pay for μ⁻ and μ⁺.
  • So a channel opens at 2m, not m — and a threshold is a wall, not a rarity. One MeV below it the rate is exactly zero: the sim's readout prints "0, closed" and draws the beams passing through.
τ⁺τ⁻ costs 2 × 1.777 = 3.554 GeV; the sim's chip lights the instant √s reaches it.
The pair leaves back-to-back in the centre of mass; the sim samples every event's angle from this 1 + cos²θ shape.
  • Only μ⁺μ⁻ is counted. The τ, B, W, Z and t pairs the display occasionally draws are there to show the menu widening — σ and the event count always refer to muon pairs.
02

Do the two beam energies just add up?

√s is a Lorentz invariant: every observer agrees on it, whatever frame they watch from.
Same √s, different bills — both computed with the sim's own p = √(E² − mₑ²).
  • Net momentum cannot be destroyed. Whatever p₁ − p₂ the beams bring in, the products must carry out — and that kinetic energy is not available for making mass.
  • Equal head-on beams are the only free lunch: p₁ − p₂ = 0, nothing is reserved for motion, and √s = 2Ebeam.
  • 25 + 1 GeV spends 26 GeV to buy 10. The sim labels the difference "lost to boost": 16.00 GeV, with the whole collision flying downstream at β = 0.923 (γ = 2.60).
  • The same arithmetic is why colliders beat stationary targets — there √s grows only as √(2mE). This sim does head-on beams only.

How many events will you actually record?

The rate of the counted channel: a 1/s photon term, a threshold factor, and the Z⁰ enhancement.
Three independent accounts. In the sim's units, 1 nb × 1 fb⁻¹ = 10⁶ events.

These are the sim's real sliders — drag here and the beams follow. "link beams" stays in charge: with it ticked, moving one moves both.

  • Away from the Z⁰, σ ∝ 1/s. Double both beams and s quadruples, so the muon count drops to a quarter. Energy buys reach; luminosity buys statistics.
  • ε can never rescue you — it is a fraction. From 0.90 even a perfect detector adds 11%, while ten times the luminosity is ten times the events.

04

Why does 91 GeV break the 1/s rule?

χ is the Z⁰ propagator. Its denominator never reaches zero — the width ΓZ keeps it finite.
  • Off resonance the denominator is dominated by s − MZ², χ is tiny, FZ → 1, and you are back to the pure photon 1/s.
  • At √s = MZ = 91.1876 GeV the real part vanishes and only iMZΓZ survives. |χ|² peaks, FZ ≈ 190, and σ jumps from 0.0104 nb to 1.98 nb.
  • The peak is ΓZ = 2.4952 GeV wide at half height. Move each beam about 0.6 GeV (√s 1.25 GeV off the pole) and σ halves; 5 GeV off in √s it is down to 6–7 % of the peak, yet still 10–14× the photon-only rate.
  • A resonance is not a threshold. The Z⁰ pole sits at 91.19 GeV; the ZZ pair chip is a separate wall far above, at 2MZ = 182.4 GeV.
The sim's own σ(√s) curve, sampled from its engine. Dashed: the photon term alone. The spike is the only place the 1/s rule fails; the machines sit on the ticks above the frame.
Improved Born, no initial-state radiation — LEP measured ≈1.5 nb once ISR is included.
05

Where does nature run this?

  • LEP 1 sat on that spike from 1989 to 1995; its four experiments recorded about 17 million Z decays. Muon pairs are 3.4% of them — roughly 6×10⁵ events of exactly the kind this sim counts.
  • That is what this sheet predicts for about 0.3 fb⁻¹ of Born-level running. Real machines delivered more and got the same number, because initial-state radiation bleeds rate away — the one effect this model leaves out.
  • LEP 2 then climbed to 209 GeV, crossing 2mW = 160.74 GeV in 1996 — the sim's W⁺W⁻ chip — to make W pairs to order.
  • B factories (PEP-II, KEKB) parked at √s = 10.58 GeV, just above the sim's B⁺B⁻ chip at 10.56, and logged more than 1000 fb⁻¹. The sim's ∫L dt slider stops at 100.
  • The tt̄ chip never lights: it needs 345.4 GeV and the sliders cap each beam at 105 GeV, so √s ≤ 210 GeV. No e⁺e⁻ collider has ever reached top-pair threshold — that is one reason the proposed machines aim at ~365 GeV.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "More energy means more events." Wrong twice: away from the Z⁰ the rate falls as 1/s, and unequal beams waste energy on boost. Energy widens the menu; ∫L dt fills the plate.
  • "Beam energies add." Only for equal head-on beams. Otherwise √s = √((E₁+E₂)² − (p₁−p₂)²), and the sim shows you the shortfall as "lost to boost".
  • "Below threshold the particle is just rare." It is impossible. σ carries β = √(1 − 4m²/s), which is zero at √s = 2m and undefined below.
  • "The Z⁰ is the ZZ threshold." No — the Z⁰ is a single resonance in the propagator at 91.19 GeV. Making a pair of Z bosons costs 182.4 GeV.
07

Ninety-second self-test

Both beams at 0.1 GeV, so √s = 0.20 GeV. How many muon pairs per fb⁻¹?

Exactly zero. 2m_μ = 0.2113 GeV, so β = √(1 − 4m_μ²/s) has no real value and the channel is shut. The sim prints "0, closed" and draws the e⁻ and e⁺ flying past each other.

Go from 5 + 5 GeV to 10 + 10 GeV at the same ∫L dt and ε. What happens to the muon count?

It falls to a quarter: √s doubles, s quadruples, and σ ∝ 1/s far below the Z⁰. 7.82×10⁵ events become 1.95×10⁵ — the sim's numbers, not an approximation.

25 + 1 GeV and 5 + 5 GeV both give √s = 10 GeV. Are the two runs the same?

Same creation budget and the same σ, so the same event count. Not the same collision: the 25 + 1 centre of mass flies downstream at β = 0.923, the products come out lopsided, and you paid 26 GeV of beam energy instead of 10.
