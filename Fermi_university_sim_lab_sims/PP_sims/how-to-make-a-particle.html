About: How To Make a Particle

Concept
Turning energy into matter has sharp thresholds: creating a particle of mass m always creates its antiparticle too, so the photon must bring at least 2mc². Near a heavy nucleus (which absorbs the recoil momentum but almost none of the energy) a photon converts once Eγ ≥ 2mc²(1 + m/MN) ≈ 2mc², measured in the chamber (lab) frame. Any surplus becomes kinetic energy T = Eγ − 2mc² — visible as longer, straighter tracks, since the radius in the chamber field is r = p/qB. Climbing the beam opens rungs at 1.022 MeV (e⁺e⁻), 0.2113 GeV (μ⁺μ⁻), 0.2791 GeV (π⁺π⁻), 0.9874 GeV (K⁺K⁻), 1.877 GeV (p p̅), 3.554 GeV (τ⁺τ⁻), 3.739 GeV (D⁺D⁻) and 10.56 GeV (B⁺B⁻) — eight real, observable particle–antiparticle channels.
Serves
Modern physics / intro particle physics: pair production, antimatter, E = mc² energy budgeting, why colliders chase higher energy.
Inquiry
The beam starts parked below every rung with the chamber empty and the clock paused. Predict whether 1 GeV can make p p̅, then fire the beam and climb the ladder: each species switches on at exactly 2mc². "Rung above / below" parks the beam exactly on a threshold, where the pair is born at rest (T = 0).
Misconception
"E > mc² is enough." Creation is pairwise — charge (and, for the proton, baryon number) conservation doubles the bill: threshold 2mc², not mc². The faint thin ticks on the ladder mark mc²; the beam marker sails past them without lighting anything. A second trap: "protons can't be created" — they can, as p p̅. And a lone photon in empty space can never convert at any energy.
Caveats
Rates are schematic: the lightest open pair overwhelmingly dominates real photoproduction, so heavier channels are shown occasionally and tagged "rare channel". The photon's drawn wavelength is log-compressed, not to scale. √s for γ + Pb is ≈ 194 GeV at every setting of the beam knob — it is shown in The Physics sheet only, never as the control.

The Physics

Pair production · a complete revision sheet · a gamma ray, a lead nucleus and a price list

Light doesn't fade into matter. It buys it — at a fixed price, in a shop where nothing is ever sold singly.

01

What happens when the photon reaches the nucleus?

The chamber at three beam settings — geometry, radius and track shape computed from the sim's own numbers.
  • Below the lowest rung the gamma ray passes straight through. The chamber stays empty however long you watch — 0.8 MeV simply is not 1.022 MeV.
  • At or above a rung the photon converts into a particle and its antiparticle, born together at the nucleus. Never one alone.
  • The lead nucleus is not a spectator: it absorbs the recoil momentum while taking almost none of the energy. That is the whole reason the conversion is allowed.
The chamber-frame threshold the sim uses for every rung. The recoil surcharge m/MN is a millionth for electrons and 2.7% for B mesons.
02

Why does it always cost two masses?

The photon carries zero charge, so the charges it leaves behind must cancel.
  • Every rung on the ladder is a charged species. A lone X⁺ would leave the universe one unit of charge richer, so X⁻ must come with it — and that doubles the bill to 2mc².
  • At the proton rung the sim's own feedback names a second book to balance: baryon number. One proton alone breaks it; the full channel, p p̄, does not.
  • The faint short stubs on the ladder mark mc² — half the price. The beam marker sails past every one of them and nothing lights up.
  • Rungs are sharp, not gradual. One keV below the rung: nothing. One keV above: a pair, born at rest.
All eight rungs at 2mc²(1+m/MN), plotted on the sim's own log axis, with the beam at its start value.
03

What does the surplus buy you?

Everything above the rung is kinetic energy — the pair's, not the nucleus'.
The sim shares Eγ equally between the twins and lets the heavy nucleus soak up the recoil.
More momentum means a bigger radius — surplus energy straightens tracks, it does not tighten them.
Eγ

This is the sim's real energy slider and its real rung chips — move them here and the chamber follows.

  • On the rung, T = 0: β ≈ 0, p ≈ 0, r ≈ 0. The pair is born at rest and the sim draws two motionless dots with no path at all.
  • Far above the rung, pc → Eγ/2 and the opening half-angle θ½ = arctan(mc²/pc) collapses toward mc²/pc — the twins come out nearly along the beam.
04

Why can't a photon do this on its own?

The invariant mass of the γ + nucleus system, and the threshold written invariantly.
  • A lone photon has zero invariant mass: there is no frame in which it stands still. A pair, even at rest, has invariant mass 2mc². Zero cannot become 2mc², so the conversion is forbidden in empty space at any energy.
  • The nucleus fixes this by being a momentum sink. Recoiling costs it almost nothing in energy because it is enormously heavy — hence the tiny 1 + m/MN surcharge, and nothing more.
  • So √s is not the knob. For γ + Pb it is 193.69 GeV at 0.5 MeV and only 205.3 GeV at the top of the slider: almost all of it is the nucleus' own rest energy, sitting there whether you fire or not.
  • The quantity that actually crosses a threshold is the chamber-frame photon energy Eγ — which is exactly what the sim gives you to turn.
05

Where does nature run this?

A PET scan is the electron rung read backwards: the pair annihilates and hands the 1.022 MeV back as two photons.
  • PET scanners. A positron from the tracer annihilates on an electron and two 511 keV photons fly out back to back. 511 keV is mec²; the pair of them is the same 1.022 MeV the first rung charges.
  • Showers in dense matter. Above a few MeV, pair production is how high-energy photons stop in lead — the chamber in this sim is a cartoon of a real conversion in a detector. (context: the sim models the threshold, not the cross-section.)
  • Why colliders keep getting bigger. Every new particle is quoted at 2mc². The ladder here stops at B⁺B⁻ — 10.85 GeV — and the real ladder keeps going: a top pair would cost about 345 GeV.
  • Honest scale: the sim's ladder spans four decades, from 1.022 MeV to 10.85 GeV, for a mass range of only electron to B meson.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "E > mc² is enough." No — creation is pairwise. The bill is 2mc², and the faint stubs at mc² on the ladder are there precisely to be sailed past.
  • "Protons can't be created." They can — as p p̄, at 1.886 GeV. What cannot happen is a proton appearing alone.
  • "A hot enough photon will convert anywhere." Not in empty space, at any energy. It needs a heavy body to take the recoil momentum.
  • "More energy means tighter curls." Backwards. Surplus T becomes momentum, and r = p/qB grows — the tracks straighten out.
07

Ninety-second self-test

You park the beam exactly on the e⁺e⁻ rung. How long are the two tracks?

Zero length. On the rung T = 0, so pc = 0, β = 0 and r = p/qB = 0 — the pair is born at rest. The sim draws two stationary dots and labels them "born at rest, T = 0".

You double the beam from 4 GeV to 8 GeV. Does the number of open channels double?

No — it doesn't change at all. Seven channels are open at 4 GeV (e, μ, π, K, p, τ, D) and the same seven at 8 GeV; the next rung, B⁺B⁻, needs 10.85 GeV. A threshold ladder is a price list, not a proportionality.

√s for γ + Pb barely moves as you sweep the knob. Why isn't √s the threshold variable here?

Because √s = √((MNc²)² + 2EγMNc²) is dominated by the nucleus' 193.687 GeV rest energy. The threshold condition √s ≥ MNc² + 2mc² is a fractional change of one part in 10⁵ for e⁺e⁻ — invisible on √s, but a hard edge in Eγ, which is why the sim puts Eγ on the slider.
