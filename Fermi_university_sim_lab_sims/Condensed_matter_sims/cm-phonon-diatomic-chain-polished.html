Guided Inquiry

1 · A wave down a chain

The shaker drives the left atom at 2 THz. Watch the long ripple run down the chain — neighbours swaying almost together — find the white marker where the drive line meets the lower branch on the ω(k) plot, and read the marker's wavelength λ in the Drive & marker panel.

2 · Climbing the dial

Suppose you raise the drive into the frequency region between the two branches. Predict what the chain does there.

3 · Into the gap

Drag the drive up into the region between the two branches — around 8 THz. What happens to the motion?

4 · Closing the gap

The shaded band on the plot is the forbidden band — read its edges in the band panel. Atom A is fixed at 4 u. Predict which mass for atom B makes the band vanish completely.

5 · Masses matched

With the drive still at 8 THz, slide atom B down toward 4 u. The band narrows and the once-dead 8 THz springs onto the lower branch; keep going and the two branches touch exactly at m_B = 4 u — the width readout hits 0.00 and the band is gone.

6 · Why "optical"?

Drag the drive up onto the upper branch and watch neighbouring atoms. In NaCl the two ions carry opposite charge — what does that antiphase motion create, and what kind of light could grab it?

The Physics

Phonons in a diatomic chain · a complete revision sheet · acoustic and optical branches

The forbidden band isn't a stretch of chain where the wave gets weak. It's a stretch of the dial for which the chain has no wave at all.

01

Why does one chain carry two kinds of wave?

The sim's own dispersion, computed at its defaults — 4 u and 12 u atoms on 15 N/m springs. Two branches, one band between them, and the marker where the 2 THz drive line meets the lower branch.
  • Each unit cell carries two atoms, so for every k there are two ways the cell can vibrate. The dispersion relation is a quadratic in ω², and its two roots are the two branches.
  • The lower (acoustic) branch runs down to ω = 0 at k = 0 — at long wavelength the whole cell just translates, and that is sound.
  • The upper (optical) branch never reaches zero. Its floor sits at the zone edge, above the acoustic ceiling, and the two never meet unless the masses are equal.
  • The plot runs only from −π/a to +π/a — the whole first Brillouin zone. A k outside it describes atomic motion already drawn inside it, so nothing is missing. Here a = 0.5 nm is the cell, holding one A and one B.
The sim's ω(k): the ± is the whole story, one sign per branch.
Both branches at the zone centre k = 0, and the zone the sim plots. The canvas axis is f = ω/2π in THz.
02

What are the atoms actually doing on each branch?

The ratio the Drive & marker panel prints as U_B/U_A. Its sign is the whole distinction between the branches.
Both patterns computed at the sim's defaults: the acoustic mode at the 2 THz marker, and the optical mode at k = 0.
  • Acoustic: the ratio is positive. Partners in a cell move the same way; near k = 0 they move as one body, and the readout says neighbours move together (in phase).
  • Optical at k = 0: the ratio is −mA/mB. That is exactly mAUA + mBUB = 0 — the two atoms beat against each other and the centre of mass never moves. The lighter atom takes the bigger swing.
  • That mode is a two-body oscillator: stiffness 2K, reduced mass μ = mAmB/(mA+mB), so ω²(0) = 2K/μ. μ is smaller than either mass, which is why the optical branch sits so high — not because those springs are stiffer.
  • At the zone edge one sublattice stops dead. On the acoustic branch only the heavy atoms move; on the optical branch only the light ones. Each moving atom then hangs between two stationary neighbours on two springs — hence ω = √(2K/m).
03

Where are the band edges — and what closes them?

Both edges live at k = π/a. Only the mass contrast separates them.
f mB K

These are the sim's real sliders — drag here and the chain follows. Atom A is fixed at 4 u.

  • Every frequency carries a factor √(K/m). Stiffen the springs and the whole picture rises by √K — the band moves up and widens, but the ratio of its two edges never budges.
  • The edge that belongs to atom A, √(2K/mA), stays put while you drag mB; the other edge is the one that travels. Bring mB to 4 u and they meet: the width readout reads 0.00 THz.
04

So what happens if you drive inside the band?

The gap solution: k picks up an imaginary part, and the pattern decays instead of travelling. This is the κ the sim's envelope readout reports.
  • Put ω between the branches and the relation demands sin²(ka/2) > 1. No real k solves it — so there is no travelling wave available to be weak.
  • The algebra returns k = π/a + iκ instead: neighbouring cells still alternate, but the amplitude falls by e−κa every cell. At the sim's default 8 THz that is ×0.35 per cell — ten cells in, about 3×10⁻⁵ of the drive.
  • The chain's equations are linear, so the decay rate cannot depend on how hard you shake. Turning the amplitude up moves the dead point by nothing — which is exactly the prediction the guided inquiry asks you to commit to.
  • Above the optical maximum the same thing happens for a different reason: there is no mode there either, so k = iκ near k = 0 rather than the zone edge, and the sim labels that trace evanescent — above both branches.
05

Where does nature run this?

At long wavelength the acoustic branch is a straight line — and its slope is the speed of sound.
The two branches, in a real crystal: one is sound, the other is a charge oscillation light can grab.
  • The acoustic branch is sound. At the sim's defaults vs = a√(K/2(mA+mB)) ≈ 8.4 km/s — the right order for a real solid.
  • "Optical" is about light. In an ionic crystal the two ions carry opposite charge, so the antiphase motion is an oscillating dipole, and infrared light of the same frequency can drive it. Real NaCl has its zone-centre optical pair (TO and LO) near 4.9 and 7.9 THz — 61 and 38 µm — and the wavelengths it reflects between them are the reststrahlen band.
  • Light must match both ω and k. A photon at this sim's optical maximum of 12.35 THz carries k = 2πf/c ≈ 2.6×10⁵ m⁻¹, which is 4×10⁻⁵ of the zone edge π/a. On the sim's k axis the light line is indistinguishable from the ω axis — so light couples only at k ≈ 0.
  • Honest limits of this model: nearest-neighbour springs only, one dimension, no long-range Coulomb force, so it shows no LO–TO splitting the way a real ionic crystal does. And it is classical — quantise these same modes and the energy comes in steps of ħω. Those quanta are the phonons.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "Frequencies in the band do get through, just weakly." No travelling solution exists there at all. k is complex, the motion dies within a couple of cells, and no amount of drive changes that.
  • "More mass contrast closes the band." Backwards. The edge ratio is √(mheavy/mlight) — equality closes the band, and every step away from it opens the band wider.
  • "Two atoms per cell always forbids a band." Only if they differ. At mB = mA the branches touch at k = π/a: you are looking at a monatomic chain drawn in a cell twice as big as it needed to be, and the "optical" branch is the acoustic one folded back.
  • "Beyond k = π/a there are more waves." There are not. k and k + 2π/a move the atoms identically, because the atoms only exist at discrete sites — which is why the sim plots one zone and stops.
07

Ninety-second self-test

Set mB = 4 u, equal to atom A. What happens to the two branches?

They touch at the zone edge and the width readout reads 0.00 THz. With identical masses the cell you drew is twice as long as it needs to be, so the upper branch is just the lower one folded back at k = π/a. Nothing is forbidden any more.

Double K from 15 to 30 N/m. What happens to the band?

Every frequency scales as √K, so both edges and the width grow by √2 ≈ 1.41: 6.18 → 8.73 THz and 10.70 → 15.13 THz. The ratio of the edges, √(mheavy/mlight), does not move. Stiffness shifts the band; mass contrast sizes it.

At the defaults, drive at 13 THz — above the optical maximum of 12.35 THz, and outside the shaded band. Does the wave travel?

No. "Forbidden" means "no branch at this ω", not "between the branches". Above the top of the optical branch there is no mode either, so k goes imaginary again — this time k = iκ near k = 0, so neighbouring cells stay in step while the amplitude dies. The sim labels that trace evanescent — above both branches.
