Guided Inquiry

1 · An arrow on a sphere

The atom’s state is the gold arrow, starting at the south pole |g⟩ (ground). The pink line is the laser’s rotation axis. Press ▶ Play to set the arrow swinging about it.

2 · Turn up the laser

On resonance the arrow flops all the way to |e⟩ and back. Suppose you raise the drive strength Ω/2π. What changes about the swing?

3 · Strength buys speed

Drag Ω/2π up and down. Watch the period stretch and shrink while the arrow still reaches |e⟩. Read Ω′/2π in the Effective-Rabi panel.

4 · Speed, not reach

On resonance the axis lies flat in the equator, so every turn sweeps pole to pole — P_e always tops out at 1. Strength sets only how fast, never how far.

5 · Now detune it

Keep Ω fixed and, from resonance, detune the laser by δ/2π = 1 MHz. What happens to the excited population P_e as time runs on?

6 · Watch it stall

Drag δ/2π toward 1 MHz: the pink axis tilts, the trail shrinks to a small circle near |g⟩, and P_e levels off under the dashed cap line. Watch the laser arrow miss the upper level by ħδ in the energy diagram — the flop never reaches 1 again.

7 · Capped — and faster

The tilted axis pins the circle below |e⟩. The Cap panel shows the ceiling: P_e can never beat Ω²/(Ω²+δ²). The Effective-Rabi panel shows the rate: Ω′/2π = √((Ω/2π)²+(δ/2π)²) grew. Less complete, yet more frequent.

8 · Why precision beats power

An atomic-clock laser drifts slightly off resonance. Using what you just saw — why can’t extra laser power fix it?

Raising Ω pushes the cap Ω²/(Ω²+δ²) back toward 1 but never reaches it; only retuning to δ = 0 restores the full flop. That is why atomic clocks and qubit gates chase resonance, not intensity.

The Physics

Rabi oscillations · a complete revision sheet · a driven two-level atom on the Bloch sphere

Driving an atom isn't lifting it up a ladder. It's turning one arrow about a fixed axis — and detuning is what tips that axis away from the pole you were aiming at.

01

What is the gold arrow actually doing?

Both panels are the sim's own solution, drawn in the sim's camera: on resonance the trail is a great circle that touches |e⟩; at 1 MHz of detuning it is a small circle that never leaves the southern cap.
  • The whole state of the atom is one unit arrow on the Bloch sphere: south pole |g⟩, north pole |e⟩, and the Excited-population readout is just Pe = (1 + bz)/2.
  • The laser never pushes the arrow north. It makes it turn rigidly about the pink laser axis at one fixed rate, forever — the sim starts every run at |g⟩ and lets it turn.
  • So everything on screen comes from two facts about that one axis: how far it is tipped, and how fast the arrow goes round it.
The rotating-frame Hamiltonian the sim solves. Ω is the drive strength, δ = ωL − ω₀ the mistuning — the two sliders.
One rigid rotation about the laser axis at rate Ω′, always starting from the ground state.
02

Which way does the laser axis tip?

The sim's own axis formula: the drive axis stays in the x–z plane, tipped out of the equator by θ.
Pythagoras on the two sliders. Ω′ can only be larger than Ω — detuning never slows the turn down.
  • δ = 0 — the axis lies flat in the equator. The turn is a great circle through both poles, so the arrow reaches |e⟩ exactly. This is the only case that does.
  • δ > 0 (laser above resonance) tips the axis below the equator, toward |g⟩; δ < 0 tips it above, toward |e⟩. Either tilt shrinks the circle through |g⟩.
  • The tilt sets how high the arrow can climb; the sign of δ only decides which way the trail leans along x. Pe itself depends on δ², so ±1 MHz look identical on the chart.
The same sphere cut through the x–z plane, at the sim's default Ω/2π = 0.5 MHz.

04

Is anything really spinning at 400 THz?

The three components the sim evaluates every frame — a closed form, not a numerical integration.
  • The sphere is drawn in the frame rotating with the laser. The optical oscillation has been transformed away; what is left is the slow MHz motion you watch.
  • That transform keeps only the half of the field that nearly matches the atom — the rotating-wave approximation, which the sim labels ✓ Actual (RWA). Here it is excellent: Ω and δ are MHz against a ~400 THz transition.
  • bz is the population difference; bx and by are the coherence — the phase of the superposition relative to the laser. The sim plots exactly these three numbers.
  • Nothing damps: the arrow's length stays 1 forever. This atom cannot decay, so the flopping never dies down and no steady state is ever reached.
05

Why does a clock chase resonance instead of power?

The ceiling Ω²/(Ω²+δ²) across the sim's whole detuning slider, at both ends of the strength slider.
  • As a function of detuning the ceiling is a Lorentzian of half-width Ω: it has fallen to ½ when |δ| = Ω, whatever Ω is.
  • More power widens that peak; it never lifts the wings. Holding the ceiling above 0.99 needs |δ| ≤ Ω/√99 ≈ 0.1 Ω — at the sim's default 0.5 MHz drive, about 50 kHz.
  • In the sim's energy panel the pink ħωL arrow misses the upper level by ħδ. That gap is drawn magnified ×10⁸: against a ~400 THz transition, 1 MHz really is one part in 4×10⁸.
  • A π-pulse — the gate that carries |g⟩ to |e⟩ — exists only at δ = 0, at t = π/Ω. Detune, and there is no instant at all when the gate is complete. That is why clocks and qubit gates buy tuning, not intensity.
  • Real atoms also decay and dephase. This sim runs the closed, lossless limit, so it describes the coherent part only — the first few flops of a real experiment, not its long-time steady state.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "Detuning doesn't stop it — the atom still flops fully to |e⟩, just as on resonance." No. A tilted rotation never reaches the far pole; no amount of waiting helps.
  • "A stronger laser excites more." On resonance the flop is already complete. Ω buys rate, never reach.
  • "Detuning slows the oscillation down." Backwards — Ω′ = √(Ω²+δ²) ≥ Ω. Detuning makes it faster and smaller at the same time.
  • "Red and blue detuning behave differently." Pe depends on δ², so ±1 MHz share a cap and a rate; only the tilt direction and the sign of bx flip.
07

Ninety-second self-test

The strength slider stops at 0.2 MHz. What would happen at exactly Ω = 0 with δ ≠ 0?

The drive axis would become (0, 0, −1) — the rotation axis is the arrow. Nothing moves: Pe stays 0 and the cap Ω²/(Ω²+δ²) is 0. With no drive, detuning alone does nothing at all.

Double Ω and double δ together. What changes?

The cap Ω²/(Ω²+δ²) is unchanged — it only knows the ratio δ/Ω. Ω′ doubles, so the oscillation runs twice as fast and its period halves. Same height, half the time.

The laser field oscillates at ~400 THz, yet the pink axis never moves. Why?

Because the sphere is drawn in the frame rotating at the laser frequency, with the counter-rotating half dropped (the RWA). In that frame the drive is static and only the difference δ = ωL − ω₀ survives.
