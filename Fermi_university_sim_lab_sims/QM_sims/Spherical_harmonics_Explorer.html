About This Simulation

Physics. Spherical harmonics Yℓm(θ, φ) are the angular eigenfunctions of orbital angular momentum in quantum mechanics. The integer ℓ sets the overall nodal complexity, while m sets the azimuthal structure around the z-axis.

Axes. The white z-axis (vertical) is the polar/quantization axis from which θ is measured. This matches the standard QM convention where Lz eigenstates have definite m.

Visuals. In Polar Surface, the radius is proportional to |Y|. In Projection, the surface is a fixed sphere colored by Y (real: sign + magnitude emphasis; complex: hue and brightness from phase and |Y|). In Point Cloud, angular acceptance is proportional to |Y|² while radius is mapped as r ∝ |Y|. Color encodes the field: Real uses a red↔blue sign/magnitude scale, while Complex uses hue for phase arg(Y) and brightness for |Y|.

Guided Inquiry

1 · The angular functions

The spherical harmonics Yℓm(θ, φ) are the angular eigenfunctions of orbital angular momentum: ℓ fixes its magnitude and m its projection on the white z axis. The surface has radius |Y| in each direction, blue where Y is positive and red where it is negative. Where Y vanishes the surface pinches to the origin — here, on the equatorial plane.

2 · Zero angular momentum

A state with ℓ = 0 carries no orbital angular momentum. Predict the shape of Y00.

3 · Counting the nodes

Nodal lines are the directions where Y vanishes and the surface pinches in. Keep m = 0 and try several values of ℓ. How many nodal lines does Yℓ0 have?

4 · Around the equator

Now set m = 2 at ℓ = 2. Follow the equator once around. How many times does the surface change between blue and red?

5 · The reach of m

Raise m while ℓ stays 2. How large can |m| become?
