About This Simulation

Physics. Spherical harmonics Yℓm(θ, φ) are the angular eigenfunctions of orbital angular momentum in quantum mechanics. The integer ℓ sets the overall nodal complexity, while m sets the azimuthal structure around the z-axis.

Axes. The white z-axis (vertical) is the polar/quantization axis from which θ is measured. This matches the standard QM convention where Lz eigenstates have definite m.

Visuals. In Polar Surface, the radius is proportional to |Y|. In Projection, the surface is a fixed sphere colored by Y (real: sign + magnitude emphasis; complex: hue and brightness from phase and |Y|). In Point Cloud, angular acceptance is proportional to |Y|² while radius is mapped as r ∝ |Y|. Color encodes the field: Real uses a red↔blue sign/magnitude scale, while Complex uses hue for phase arg(Y) and brightness for |Y|.

The Physics

Spherical harmonics · a complete revision sheet · the angular eigenfunctions of orbital angular momentum

A spherical harmonic is not a picture of where the electron is. It is the only angular pattern a quantised angular momentum leaves room for — and the radius you see on screen is |Y|, not a distance.

01

What is the surface actually showing?

The sim's default harmonic Y20, computed, cut through the white z axis and drawn both of the sim's ways. Blue is Y > 0, red is Y < 0 — the sim's own diverging scale.
  • A full orbital splits into a radial part and an angular part: ψ = R(r) · Yℓm(θ, φ). This sim draws only the angular half; R(r) never appears.
  • In Polar Surface the distance from the origin is the value of |Y| in that direction, rescaled so the peak sits at radius 1.2. Where Y vanishes the surface pinches to the origin — those are the nodal lines.
  • In Projection the radius is frozen at 1.5 and only the colour carries Y. Same harmonic, same nodes, no lobes — the lobes were a plotting choice, not a shape in space.
  • Surface Cutoff only fades the surface below a fraction of its peak radius; it changes the picture's alpha, never the geometry or the physics.
The sim owns the second factor. The normalisation is over solid angle, not over r.
The two plotting rules, exactly as the sim's code writes them.
02

Why only these shapes, and why two integers?

ℓ fixes the magnitude of the angular momentum; m fixes its projection on the white z axis.
  • Yℓm is not a guess — it is the simultaneous eigenfunction of L² and Lz. Nothing else on the sphere survives both conditions.
  • Going once round the equator must return you to the same wave, so eimφ forces m to be an integer. That is the whole origin of the quantum number.
  • |m| ≤ ℓ: mħ is a projection of a vector of length √(ℓ(ℓ+1))ħ, and a projection cannot beat the vector. The sim enforces it for you — the m slider re-ranges to ±ℓ the moment you move ℓ.
  • At ℓ = 0 there is no axis to point along: Y00 is the constant 1/√(4π), and the surface is a featureless sphere.
The three factors the sim's code computes separately: normFactor, assocLegendre, and the phase.
03

What do ℓ and m actually buy you?

ℓ m

These are the sim's real ℓ and m sliders — drag here and the harmonic on screen follows. Raising ℓ never moves m; lowering ℓ drags m back inside ±ℓ, exactly as the sim does.

The node budget the Nodal Structure panel prints: Angular Nodes (ℓ) = Nodal planes (|m|) + Latitudinal (ℓ−|m|).
Every |m| allowed at ℓ = 3, computed: teal rings are the θ-zeros of P3|m|(cos θ), amber rings the |m| planes where cos(mφ) = 0. The total is always 3.
  • Raise ℓ at fixed m and you add latitudinal pinches — rings of latitude. Raise |m| at fixed ℓ and cones convert into planes through z, one for one.
  • In Real mode, walking once round the equator crosses 2|m| sign changes — blue, red, blue, red. That is the count the sim's fourth inquiry card asks for.
04

If Lz = mħ, where is the rest of L?

The vector model at ℓ = 2, computed: five allowed projections, and not one of them reaches √6 ħ.
  • Even the stretched state m = ℓ falls short: at ℓ = 2 the best you get is 2ħ out of 2.449ħ, a tilt of 35.3° that can never be closed.
  • The missing length is real but directionless. Lx and Ly average to zero while their squares do not — the vector is smeared round the cone, not parked on it.
  • That is not sloppiness, it is the algebra: Lx and Ly do not commute with Lz, so all three components can never be sharp at once.
  • The gap closes only in the limit: √(ℓ(ℓ+1)) → ℓ + ½ for large ℓ, which is why a spinning classical top never notices any of this.
Why a definite Lz costs you Lx and Ly.
05

Real or complex — which one is the state?

The sim's Real toggle builds exactly these two combinations from the complex harmonic.
  • Complex is the Lz eigenstate. |Y| has no φ dependence at all — the surface is axially symmetric and the whole azimuthal story lives in the hue: arg(Y) advances as mφ, with a π flip wherever Pℓ|m|(cos θ) changes sign.
  • Real is an equal mix of +|m| and −|m|. It has lobes and sign changes, it is the chemist's px, py, dxy set — and it carries ⟨Lz⟩ = 0. Same L², different Lz story.
  • At m = 0 the two coincide: there is no eimφ factor to remove, and the sim's own note says so when you set m = 0.
  • Which set nature picks is set by what breaks the symmetry: a magnetic field along z selects the complex set (the Zeeman splitting goes as m), a molecular or crystal field selects the real one.
  • Nothing here is animated, and that is correct. A stationary state's time factor e−iEt/ħ is the same number in every direction — it would shift every hue together and change no shape. The sim plots Yℓm(θ, φ) only.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "The lobes show how far the electron is from the nucleus." The radius on screen is |Y| in that direction, rescaled. Distance from the nucleus lives in R(r), which this sim never draws. Switch to Projection and the lobes vanish while the physics does not.
  • "Point Cloud is the electron cloud." It samples directions with probability proportional to |Y|², but each dot is still placed at a radius proportional to |Y| — the sim's own legend says exactly that. It is an angular probability map wearing a three-dimensional costume.
  • "Red means negative probability." Red is the sign of Y in Real mode. |Y|² is never negative; the sign only earns its keep when you add harmonics together, which is precisely how the real set is built.
  • "m is the angular momentum, and it can be anything." mħ is only the z projection, it is bounded by |m| ≤ ℓ, and the full magnitude √(ℓ(ℓ+1))ħ is always strictly larger. Watch the m slider re-range itself when you drop ℓ.
07

Ninety-second self-test

What does the surface look like at ℓ = 0, and why can it be nothing else?

A featureless sphere. Y00 = 1/√(4π) ≈ 0.282, a constant: |Y| is identical in every direction so the radius is too, and with zero nodes there is nowhere for it to pinch. With no angular momentum there is no axis to single one direction out — the state must look the same from everywhere.

Set ℓ = 4, m = −3. How many cones, how many planes, and how many sign changes round the equator?

|m| = 3 nodal planes through z, ℓ − |m| = 1 nodal cone about z, totalling ℓ = 4 angular nodes — which is what the Nodal Structure panel prints. In Real mode the azimuthal factor is sin(3φ), so the equator crosses 2|m| = 6 sign changes. The same state in Complex mode shows no sign changes at all: |Y| is axially symmetric and the 3 is in the hue winding.

Why does the Real/Complex toggle change nothing when m = 0?

The real combinations are √2·Re Yℓ|m| and √2·Im Yℓ|m|. At m = 0 the factor eimφ is 1, so Yℓ0 = N·Pℓ(cos θ) is already real: there is nothing to combine and nothing to remove. The state is simultaneously the Lz eigenstate with Lz = 0 and its own real standing wave.
