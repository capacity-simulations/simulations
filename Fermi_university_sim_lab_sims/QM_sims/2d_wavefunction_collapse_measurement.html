The Physics

Wavefunction collapse · a complete revision sheet · one spreading packet, one measured point

A measurement does not reveal where the particle was hiding. It takes a cloud and hands back a point — and the cloud only ever told you the odds.

01

What is the hump actually made of?

A cut through the sim's own surface at y = y₀, computed from σ = 0.75 and the sim's spreading law. Same area under every curve; only the shape changes.
  • Surface height in 3D and colour in Heatmap are the same quantity: |Ψ(x, y, t)|², the probability density. Not Ψ — this sim never evaluates a phase, only the density.
  • It is a density per unit area, so ∫∫|Ψ|² dx dy = 1 at every t. That identity is what the Total Probability readout states; Visible Probability is the part still inside the plotted 20 × 20 square.
  • It is not a smeared-out particle. Press Measure and the collapse table gets one Final X and one Final Y, to three decimals.
  • Left alone the packet only flattens in place. Its centre never moves, because this packet carries zero mean momentum — nothing is pushing it.
The density the sim evaluates at every vertex of the mesh.
02

Where does the point land?

The Born rule — and the one honest correction this sim makes for its finite window.
  • Bright means likely, dark means rare — never forbidden. The chance of an outcome in a patch is |Ψ|² integrated over that patch.
  • The sim draws x and y from the exact Gaussian and rejects anything outside [−10, 10]. That is |Ψ|² renormalised on the visible square, the same square Visible Probability is measuring.
  • The most likely single point is the centre. The most likely distance is r = σ(t): a point has no area, and rings further out are longer.
  • Only 39% of outcomes land inside r = σ(t), and 86% inside 2σ(t). The 68% you remember is one axis at a time, not the radius.
Turn the 2D density into a distribution over distance and the peak moves off the centre.
Left: the sim's own colour map at t = 0 with twelve outcomes placed at the exact quantiles of that radial law. Right: the law itself.
03

How fast does it spread, and how far?

Free spreading: the width the sim recomputes on every frame, and the peak that follows from it.
σ speed

These are the sim's real controls — drag here and the packet follows. Note the sim's own rule: changing σ rebuilds the packet at t = 0, clears the collapse table and pauses the run.

Why a narrow packet cannot stay narrow: squeezing Δx is what widens Δp.
  • Halve σ and the starting peak goes up four times — but τ = 2mσ²/ℏ falls four times too, so the sharper packet blurs sooner.
  • Animation Speed is not physics. It only sets how much simulation time passes per real second; σ(t), the peak and the statistics of the outcomes are untouched.
04

What does the measurement leave behind?

One outcome is drawn, and the state it came from is replaced.
  • What is left is a fresh Gaussian of width σ centred on the outcome — the Measurement Width slider sets that width. It approximates a position eigenstate; it is not one.
  • So an immediate repeat is reproducible only to about σ. A true eigenstate would repeat exactly; this one scatters by roughly a measurement width.
  • One slider sets both widths here, so measuring at t = 0 barely narrows anything — it mostly just moves the centre to the outcome. Let the packet spread first and the collapse becomes a real localisation.
  • The spreading clock restarts at the measurement: σ is rebuilt from t − tm, and nothing remembers the original centre.
05

Does anything real spread this way?

  • An electron pinned to 1 nm doubles its width in about 30 fs. Confine an electron and it refuses to stay confined — that is the wave side of every nanoscale device.
  • Because t₂ ∝ mσ², a 1 µg dust grain localised to 1 µm would need about 10⁶ years to double. Its packet is frozen on any human timescale, which is exactly why the classical picture survives.
  • Free neutrons and atoms sit between those extremes, and matter-wave interferometers depend on the spreading: a packet has to broaden before it can overlap with itself.
  • Nothing in the law is about measurement apparatus. Spreading is free evolution; the measurement is the separate step that resets it.
One electron, computed from t₂ = 2√3 mσ²/ℏ: doubling time against packet width, straight on log axes.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "The hump is just our ignorance of where the particle already is." Then measuring would read off a pre-existing value and leave the state alone. Here the state is replaced — a new narrow packet at the outcome — and everything measured afterwards comes from that replacement.
  • "A measurement returns a blurred patch the size of the hump." It returns one pair of coordinates. The blur lives in the statistics of many runs, which is what the collapse table accumulates.
  • "The dark surround is forbidden." |Ψ|² there is small but positive, and the sim samples it exactly. Rare is not impossible: run long enough and an outcome lands out there.
  • "Collapse pins the particle down." Only at that instant. The narrow packet starts spreading again immediately — and faster than before, because a smaller σ means a smaller τ = 2mσ²/ℏ.
07

Ninety-second self-test

You press Measure at t = 0, before any spreading. What actually changes?

Not the width. This sim builds the collapsed packet with the same σ that built the initial one — one slider sets both — so the shape is identical. What changes is the centre: it jumps to the sampled outcome, typically about σ away, and the spreading clock restarts from there. Collapse only narrows a packet that had already spread.

Halve the Measurement Width. What happens to the starting peak, and to the time the peak takes to halve?

The peak is 1/(2πσ²), so halving σ multiplies it by four. The halving time is τ = 2mσ²/ℏ, so it divides by four. A sharper packet is both taller and shorter-lived — that is the uncertainty principle stated as a rate.

Total Probability never leaves 1.000000 while Visible Probability slowly falls. Is probability leaking away?

No. The prefactor 1/2πσ(t)² is exactly what makes ∫∫|Ψ|² dx dy = 1 on the whole plane at every t, so the first readout is stating an identity. Visible Probability is the fraction still inside the plotted 20 × 20 square, and it drops as the tails grow past the edge. The sampler handles that by renormalising on the square before it draws an outcome.
