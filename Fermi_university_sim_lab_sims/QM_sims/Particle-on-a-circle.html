About this simulation

A quantum particle on a ring (\(R=1\); natural units \(\hbar=1\), \(2m=1\)) in periodic eigenstates labeled by the integer \(n\).

Physics

Energy \(E_n \propto n^2\), with lecture notation \(\psi_n(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi R}}e^{inx/R}\). The plot around the ring uses the angular coordinate \(\phi=x/R\). For \(n\neq 0\), \(\pm n\) are degenerate in energy.

\[E_n = \frac{n^2\hbar^2}{2mR^2}\]
\[\psi_n(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi R}} e^{inx/R}\]
\[P(x)=|\psi_n(x)|^2=\frac{1}{2\pi R}\]
Visuals

Energy levels: ladder of states vs energy; the chosen \(n\) is highlighted.
Wavefunction: real (blue) and imaginary (green) parts vs \(\phi\), or the same data wrapped on the ring; optional \(| \psi |^2\) band (yellow). Panels can be maximized.

Controls

Quantum Number: slider for \(n\); degeneracy note below it. Display Mode: ring vs \(\phi\) plot toggle; Show Re part / Show Im part; Show Probability Density \(|\psi|^2\); Animate Time Evolution. Visuals: Time Speed slider. Current State: ring radius, \(E_n\), wavelength, and the lecture equations. Top bar: Reset / Pause.

Guided Inquiry

1 · The ring

A particle moves freely on a circle of radius R — no walls, no forces, but the wave must join up with itself after one full turn. The energy ladder shows which energies are allowed; beside it, the real (blue) and imaginary (green) parts of ψ are drawn around the ring. The integer n counts the whole wavelengths that fit into one circumference.

2 · The classical bead

Before quantum mechanics: a bead slides on a frictionless circular wire, with nothing to push it or slow it. Which energies can the bead have?

3 · Whole numbers only

On the ring the label n is restricted to integers. What imposes that restriction?

4 · The lowest rung

A particle in a box can never have zero energy: there ψ would vanish everywhere. The ring’s ladder has a rung at E = 0. What does that state look like?

5 · Reversing the sign

n may be negative as well as positive. Put side by side, how do the states n = +2 and n = −2 compare?

6 · Where is the particle?

The yellow band is |ψ|², the probability of finding the particle at each point. Take |n| from 2 up to 5. How does that band change?
