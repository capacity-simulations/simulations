📖 About the Simulation
Physics:
A spin-½ in a constant magnetic field \(B\hat{z}\) picks up opposite phases on \(|\uparrow\rangle\) and \(|\downarrow\rangle\), producing Larmor precession of the Bloch vector about the z-axis at rate \(\mu B\). Measuring \(\hat S^x\) gives outcomes \(\pm \hbar/2\) with time-dependent probabilities, while the \(\hat S^z\) probabilities stay fixed. The display uses \(\hbar=1\).
Conventions:
The state is restricted to \(\alpha|\uparrow\rangle+\beta|\downarrow\rangle\) with \(\alpha\) real and \(\beta=\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}\), so \(|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1\). Sign convention: \(H=-\omega_{0}\hat S_{z}\), \(\omega_{0}=\mu B\), and \(\hbar=1\).
Visuals:
Top: Bloch sphere showing the Bloch vector \(n=\langle\sigma\rangle\) and its circular precession path (drag to rotate). Bottom: \(P_+\) for an \(\hat S^x\) measurement. The probability axis is fixed to \([0,1]\).
Controls:
\(\alpha\) sets the \(|\uparrow\rangle\) amplitude, \(\beta=\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}\), and \(\mu B\) sets the precession rate. Speed scales the animation clock. Use Reset/Pause to control playback.

Guided Inquiry

1 · Spin in a magnetic field

Here’s our setup. A spin-½ sits in a uniform magnetic field along z. Its state is α|↑⟩ + β|↓⟩, with β = √(1 − α²), and you can see it drawn as the arrow on the Bloch sphere. Our question, at every instant, is P+ — the probability that a measurement of Sx returns +ℏ/2.

2 · Prepared along z

Let’s start at the pole. Set α = 1, so the spin is prepared exactly along +z. What do you think P+ does as time runs?

3 · Prepared along x

Now let’s tip it right over. With α = β = 1/√2, the arrow starts along +x. Over a long run, what range do you think P+ covers?

4 · A small tilt

So what about somewhere in between? Set α = 0.98, a spin tilted only slightly from +z. Over a long run, what range do you expect P+ to cover?

5 · A stronger field

One last experiment. Keep this state and double μB from 1.0 to 2.0. How long do you think one full cycle of P+ takes now?

The Physics

Larmor precession · a complete revision sheet · measuring a spin-½ along x

P+ is not the size of the spin's x-component. It is the odds that one measurement of Sx — which can only ever hand back +ℏ/2 or −ℏ/2 — comes back positive.

What is the blue curve the probability of?

The faint arc is the path swept since t = 0, the small dot on the x axis is nx, and the cursor on the right reads the matching P+ = 0.770. The sphere is drawn tilted up from the sim's default view — drag the sim's sphere to match.
  • A measurement of Sx has exactly two possible answers, +ℏ/2 and −ℏ/2. P+ is the probability of the first — a number in [0, 1], never a component.
  • Rewrite the state in the x basis. |↑⟩ = (|→⟩ + |←⟩)/√2: a spin sharp along z is an equal superposition along x, so its P+ is exactly ½.
  • The arrow's shadow on the x axis is nx, and P+ = ½(1 + nx). The field turns the arrow, so the shadow moves — and with it, only the odds.
  • Nothing is measured on screen. The state evolves freely and never collapses; the curve is what a measurement would give, instant by instant.

02

What sets how wide P+ swings, and how fast?

The field gives |↑⟩ and |↓⟩ opposite phases; only their relative phase μBt moves.
  • The two phases interfere, and the cross term is a cosine. Its half-width is αβ, so the swing is 2αβ — exactly the arrow's projection onto the equatorial plane, sin θ.
  • Widest at α = β = 1/√2, the sim's default: swing 1, and P+ covers the whole 0 to 1. Zero at α = 1 or β = 1, where the arrow sits on a pole and P+ is pinned at ½.
  • α sets how wide; μB sets how fast. The period is T = 2π/μB, so doubling the field halves it. The two sliders do genuinely different jobs.
  • The plot's x axis is ruled in units of π/μB, so changing μB does not change the drawn shape — only the numbers on the t readout.

The Bloch vector the sim draws, and the two quantities that do not move.
  • P+ + P− = 1 at every instant: those are the only two outcomes, so what one gains the other loses.
  • The z odds are frozen: P(Sz = +ℏ/2) = α² for all t. Precession about z cannot change them — only the x and y components move.
  • ⟨Sx⟩ = (ℏ/2) nx = (ℏ/2)(P+ − P−): the average of the two allowed answers, weighted by these odds. The sim displays ℏ = 1.
04

If P+ keeps changing, has anything been measured?

  • No. The sim evolves one pure state and reports the odds a measurement would give. There is no collapse, no record of outcomes, no randomness anywhere in the code.
  • |↑⟩ and |↓⟩ are the energy eigenstates of this field. Prepare α = 1 or β = 1 and the state is stationary: it picks up an overall phase only, the arrow never moves, and the curve is a flat line.
  • Any α strictly between 0 and 1 is a superposition of two different energies. Their relative phase winds at μB, and that winding is the whole of the time dependence you see.
  • Pause freezes the clock, Reset returns t to 0, and Speed only rescales the animation. None of the three touches the physics — the curve they ride is already drawn in full.
05

Where does nature run this?

  • The sim's μB is the Larmor rate ω0 = γB in disguise. Change the particle and only γ changes; the whole of this sheet carries over.
  • A proton in a 1.5 T clinical MRI scanner precesses at 63.9 MHz — the radio frequency the scanner transmits and listens on.
  • An electron's γ is about 658 times larger: 0.35 T puts it at 9.81 GHz, the microwave band used for electron spin resonance.
  • Ramsey and spin-echo sequences read exactly this phase: let the spin precess, then measure along x. The swing 2αβ is the fringe contrast, and μBt is the fringe.

06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "The spin points along z, so it has no x-component — P+ = 0." An Sx measurement cannot return 0; it returns ±ℏ/2. |↑⟩ is an equal mix of |→⟩ and |←⟩, so P+ = ½, and the field never changes it.
  • "P+ is the x-component of the spin." The component is ⟨Sx⟩ = (ℏ/2)(P+ − P−). P+ lives in [0, 1]; the component lives in [−ℏ/2, +ℏ/2]. At the poles P+ = ½ while ⟨Sx⟩ = 0: the odds are even, and the average component is zero.
  • "It was prepared along x, so it stays along x." |→⟩ is not an energy eigenstate of a z field. Half a Larmor period later the arrow has swung to |←⟩ and P+ has fallen to 0.
  • "A stronger field makes P+ swing wider." μB changes the rate only. The swing is 2αβ, set by the preparation alone — the curve gets faster, never taller.
07

Ninety-second self-test

Set α = 1. What does the arrow do, and what does P+ do?

The arrow sits on the north pole and never moves: |↑⟩ is an energy eigenstate of H = −μB Sz, so it only collects an overall phase. The swing 2αβ is zero, so P+ is pinned at exactly 0.5 for all time — while P(Sz = +ℏ/2) = α² = 1. Sharp along z, maximally uncertain along x.

Halve α from 1/√2 to 0.354 and double μB. What happens to the swing and to the period?

The period halves, T = 2π/μB. The swing does not halve: β = √(1 − 0.125) = 0.935, so 2αβ goes from 1.000 to about 0.66, and P+ runs 0.17 … 0.83. The swing depends on the product αβ, which is maximal at α = β and falls off slowly on either side.

The curve keeps moving, but the QUANTUM STATE readout and the Sz odds never change. Why?

Because the field lies along z. It winds the relative phase between |↑⟩ and |↓⟩ and does nothing else, so |α|² and |β|² are constants of the motion. The readout is labelled |ψ(0)⟩ and lists exactly those two amplitudes; the moving quantity is a phase, and a phase is only visible in the x and y components — which is where P+ is looking.
