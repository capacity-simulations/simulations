About this simulation

Physics

For a 1D symmetric rectangular barrier V(x) = U for −a < x < a (full width 2a, height U), the plane-wave transmission probability T(E) depends on mass m and energy E. Below the barrier (E < U), transmission occurs via quantum tunnelling; above (E > U), T oscillates with barrier width. Conservation of probability gives R + T = 1, where R is the reflection probability.

Formulas

E < U (tunneling — derived in Lecture 9):

\[ T = \frac{4k^2\eta^2}{4k^2\eta^2 + (k^2+\eta^2)^2\sinh^2(2\eta a)} \]

E > U (above barrier — obtained by substituting η → iκ):

\[ T = \frac{4k^2\kappa^2}{4k^2\kappa^2 + (k^2-\kappa^2)^2\sin^2(2\kappa a)} \]
\[ \eta = \sqrt{\frac{2m(U-E)}{\hbar^2}}\,,\quad \kappa = \sqrt{\frac{2m(E-U)}{\hbar^2}}\,,\quad E = \frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m} \]

The simulation sets ℏ = 1 in the computation. The E > U formula extends the lecture derivation, which covers the tunneling case explicitly.

The Physics

Transmission through a rectangular barrier · a complete revision sheet · T at every energy at once

Tunnelling is not a particle borrowing energy to climb the wall. It is a wave that decays inside the wall instead of stopping — and if the wall ends before the wave has died, part of it carries on out the far side.

01

Why does anything cross a wall it cannot climb?

The sim's own screen, computed: T against E at the first-load settings a = 1.00 Å, U = 5.00 eV, m = 1.00 me. Dashed grey is the classical prediction; magenta dots are the resonance markers the sim draws.
  • The beam is a steady plane wave, not a stream of balls. The sim solves the time-independent Schrödinger equation once and reads off the transmitted fraction at every energy at once — that whole curve is one calculation, not a simulation of trajectories.
  • Classically the answer is a step: below the top nothing crosses, above it everything does. That is the dashed grey line — and dragging the half-width a never moves it.
  • Quantum mechanically ψ and its slope must be continuous at both edges. A solution that is exactly zero beyond x = +a cannot match on smoothly, so there is always a transmitted piece.
  • T is that piece, |t|², and R = 1 − T is the rest. Nothing is absorbed: the sim's two readouts always add to 1.
The barrier the sim models, and the three pieces of the stationary state it matches together.
02

What is the wave doing inside the barrier?

Inside, the equation loses its minus sign — so the solution stops oscillating and starts decaying.
  • Below the top, ψ″ = η²ψ has real exponentials for solutions, not waves. The amplitude falls like e−ηx, so the probability density falls like e−2ηx.
  • The wall is only 2a thick, so the decay is cut short. Whatever amplitude survives to x = +a leaves as the transmitted wave — that is the entire mechanism.
  • This is a stationary scattering state, not a particle sitting in the barrier. The sim never measures a position; it only reports the transmitted fraction, so “how long it spends inside” is a question this model does not ask.
  • Above the top the interior turns back into an oscillation with κ instead of η — and κ < k, so the wavelength is longer inside than outside. Less kinetic energy, longer wave.

Bottom: the default a = 1.00 Å at the first resonance, where the reflected pieces cancel and the wave crosses undiminished. How small is the chance — and what kills it fastest?

Once the wall is a couple of decay lengths thick, only the exponent matters.
  • The exponent is 2η × (2a): two decay lengths' worth of wall, counted over the full width. Every quantity that matters — width, height, mass, energy — reaches T through it.
  • Width is the brutal one, because it multiplies the exponent directly. Height and mass enter through η, i.e. under a square root: quartering m only halves the exponent.
  • That is why the deck's card 3 lands where it does: at a = 1.00 Å, U = 5.00 eV, E = U/2, dropping m from 1.00 to 0.25 me lifts T from 5.2 × 10⁻⁴ to 4.5 × 10⁻² — nearly ninety times, from one factor of √m.

04

Why does the curve touch 1 above the top, then dip again?

Above the top, sinh becomes sin — and a sine has zeros.
  • Above the top there are still two edges, and each one reflects. The two reflected waves meet with a phase difference set by how much wave fits across the barrier.
  • When a whole number of half-waves spans 2a — that is 2κa = nπ — the two reflections cancel exactly. R = 0, T = 1, and the sim puts a magenta dot there.
  • Between the dots they do not cancel, so T dips below 1: a wave reflects off any sharp change in potential, including a step down. Classical physics has no word for this.
  • The markers crowd together as a or m grows, since En − U goes as n²/(ma²) — widen the wall and the ripples get denser, exactly as the plot shows.
The seam at E = U, where the two formulas meet — and the conservation law that holds on both sides.
05

Where does nature run this?

Real barriers are not rectangles; the exponent becomes an integral, and the sim's case is the one where it can be done in your head.
  • The scanning tunnelling microscope. Electrons tunnel a vacuum gap against a work function of roughly 4 eV, so η ≈ 1.0 Å⁻¹ and the current falls about eightfold for every extra ångström. That ferocious exponent is what resolves single atoms.
  • Alpha decay. The α particle is trapped behind a Coulomb barrier and leaks out. A factor of about two in decay energy spans roughly 24 orders of magnitude in half-life — microseconds for 212Po, ten billion years for 232Th. Only an exponential does that.
  • The Sun. Core thermal energies are around 1 keV against a proton–proton Coulomb barrier near 1 MeV. Classically hydrogen would never burn; the Sun runs on the tunnelling tail.
  • Flash memory. Electrons are driven through an oxide barrier to charge a floating gate. The same exponent gives both properties you want: writable in microseconds under a field, and stable for years without one.
  • These four use SI constants (ħ²/2me = 3.81 eV·Å²) and standard textbook values — not the sim's ħ = 1 convention, and not numbers the sim itself computes.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • “A thin enough wall would let a classical particle through too.” No. The dashed classical step depends on E alone — drag a from 0.05 to 2.5 Å and it does not move. Thickness only matters to something that has a wavelength.
  • “The particle borrows energy and hops over the top.” Nothing is borrowed: E is the same on both sides and never appears above U in the algebra. What crosses the wall is wave amplitude.
  • “Halve the mass and you double T.” Mass sits under a square root inside an exponent. At a = 1.00 Å, U = 5.00 eV, E = U/2, quartering m takes T from 5.2 × 10⁻⁴ to 4.5 × 10⁻² — nearly ninety times, not four.
  • “Above the top, everything gets through.” Between the magenta markers T dips well below 1. Reflection from a potential step is a wave effect, and it does not care that the step goes down.
  • “Make it thin enough and T reaches 1 below the top.” sinh²(2ηa) is strictly positive for every E < U, so T < 1 always. Perfect transmission needs an oscillating interior, which only exists above the top.
07

Ninety-second self-test

What happens to the whole curve as the half-width a goes to zero?

T → 1 at every energy. sinh(2ηa) → 0 kills the only term that can suppress it, and the E = U value 1/(1 + 2mUa²/ħ²) → 1 as well. With no wall left there is nothing to reflect from. Even at the slider's smallest setting, a = 0.05 Å, T at E = U/2 is already 0.95.

Double the half-width and halve the mass. What happens to T below the top?

It falls. The exponent 2η(2a) = 4a√(2m(U − E)) gains a factor 2 from the width and loses 1/√2 from the mass — net √2. At the sim's defaults with E = U/2 it goes from 8.94 to 12.65, and T drops from 5.2 × 10⁻⁴ to 1.3 × 10⁻⁵, about forty times smaller. Doubling the width costs more than halving the mass saves.

At a resonance T is exactly 1. Has the barrier stopped existing?

No — the wave still enters, and inside it has its own wavelength κ. At 2κa = nπ the waves reflected at the two edges come back exactly out of phase and cancel, so R = 0. It is interference, not absence: move a little off the marked energy and T drops again.
