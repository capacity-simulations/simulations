📊 About This Simulation

Physics: The particle is prepared in a Gaussian wavefunction ψ(x). Measurement outcomes follow the Born rule, so the position probability density is |ψ(x)|². Repeated measurements on identically prepared states reproduce the theoretical distribution.

Simulation visuals: The top plot shows the theoretical wavefunction view (probability, real part, imaginary part, or both real/imaginary). The bottom plot shows measured outcomes as a normalized histogram, overlaid with the theoretical |ψ(x)|² curve for comparison.

Controls: Run single-shot or batch measurements, enable auto-measurement and set its rate, adjust animation speed, switch view mode, and reset measurements. The Statistics panel reports total count, mean position, measured standard deviation, theoretical σ, and convergence.

The Physics

Wavefunctions and |ψ|² · a complete revision sheet · the Born rule, one measurement at a time

The wavefunction is not where the particle is. It is an amplitude — and only its square, measured over many identical preparations, tells you how often you will find the particle there.

01

What is the top plot actually showing?

The sim's upper plot, computed at its two main View Modes: |ψ(x)|² (left) and Re ψ with Im ψ at one instant (right). Same state both times; the amplitude view is rescaled so the envelope peaks at 1, the density view is drawn in real density units.
  • The curve is the prepared state, not the particle. In |ψ(x)|² view it is a probability density; in Re(ψ) / Im(ψ) view it is the complex amplitude itself.
  • The state is a Gaussian of width σ, and σ is fixed — the Statistics panel prints Theoretical σ = 1.00 and it never changes. Both axes are ruled in units of σ, from −4σ to +4σ.
  • |ψ(x)|² is a density, not a probability: it carries units of 1/length, which is why the y-axis says "Probability Density". Only its area over an interval is a probability.
  • Every press of Measure acts on a freshly prepared copy of the same state, and returns exactly one position. The smear is a property of the ensemble, not of any one shot.

02

Why does a wave that dips below zero still mean "likely"?

Computed at the instant the phase puts the whole real part below the axis — the sim's card 3 situation. The trough of Re ψ sits exactly under the peak of |ψ|².
  • Squaring discards the sign: amplitudes −0.8 and +0.8 give the same |ψ|² = 0.64. The deepest point of the curve is the most likely place, not the least.
  • A negative amplitude is not a negative probability. |ψ|² = (Re ψ)² + (Im ψ)² is a sum of squares, so it can never go below zero.
  • Watch the Re(ψ) view at low Animation Speed: the curve swings from all-positive to all-negative and back, while the histogram underneath keeps rebuilding the same shape.
  • Signs are not wasted in general — they decide what happens when two amplitudes are added before squaring. This sim prepares a single Gaussian, so there is nothing to add to and only the magnitude survives.

How many measurements before the histogram is |ψ(x)|²?

What the Statistics panel is estimating, and how fast each estimate tightens.
shots rate speed

These are the sim's real controls — Measurements per click, Auto-measure rate and Animation Speed. Drag here and the panel on the right follows.

  • Each shot is one draw from |ψ(x)|². No single outcome is predicted; only the frequencies are.
  • The bars are densities: count ÷ total ÷ bin width, over the sim's 50 bins of 0.16σ each. That is why more data sharpens the shape instead of raising it — the enclosed area stays pinned at one.
  • Everything converges as 1/√N. The Convergence bar is 100(1 − |s − σ|/σ), so closing its last few percent is expensive: halving the error costs four times the data, and ten times the data buys only about three times the accuracy.

04

If the phase is turning, why doesn't the probability move?

One global phase factor, and the cancellation that makes the state stationary.
  • The sim's time dependence is a single global phase e−iωt — the same factor at every x. Re ψ and Im ψ trade amplitude; the dashed envelope A(x) never moves.
  • In the square the phase meets its own conjugate and cancels, so |ψ(x,t)|² = |ψ(x)|². That is what stationary means — and it is why Animation Speed changes nothing the histogram can see.
  • The sim prints ⟨p⟩ = 0 under View Mode: this state has no net momentum, so the packet does not drift left or right either.
  • Honesty about what is not modelled: a real free Gaussian packet also spreads, because each momentum component picks up its own phase e−iℏk²t/2m. This sim applies one x-independent phase only, so nothing spreads. Read the top plot as a stationary state's clock, not as free propagation.
05

Where does nature actually run this?

The price of localisation, for this exact Gaussian shape.
  • Put real units on σ. An electron localised to σ = 1 nm has σp = ℏ/2σ ≈ 5.3×10−26 kg m/s, a velocity spread of about 5.8×104 m/s. Squeezing a particle costs momentum. (The sim never plots momentum — this is what its ψ implies, not a readout.)
  • Tonomura, 1989: electrons fired through a biprism one at a time arrived as individual dots, and only after tens of thousands did the interference pattern appear. That experiment is the lower plot of this sim, with an interference |ψ|² in place of a Gaussian.
  • In a quantum corral imaged by a scanning tunnelling microscope, the standing-wave ripples map the local density of states of the trapped surface electrons — |ψ|² at the Fermi energy, scanned out point by point.
  • Chemistry's orbital pictures are the same object: a p-orbital "shape" is the surface enclosing about 90% of ∫|ψ|², a conventional contour rather than a solid body.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "ψ is the probability." ψ is a complex amplitude with units of 1/√length. The probability is |ψ|² integrated over an interval — the y-axis reads "Probability Density" for exactly that reason.
  • "A negative amplitude means a smaller chance." Squaring removes the sign. When the sim's Re(ψ) curve is at its deepest, that point is the most likely place to find the particle.
  • "More measurements make the central bars taller." Every bar is divided by the same total and by the bin width, so its height is a density. More data shrinks the scatter; the area under the bars stays one.
  • "The phase is turning, so the particle must be moving." A phase multiplying the whole wave cancels in the square. Run the Animation Speed slider from 0.2x to 4.0x and the measured mean, spread and histogram shape are statistically identical.
07

Ninety-second self-test

You press Measure exactly once. What has ψ predicted?

Nothing about that one number — it is a single draw from |ψ(x)|² and could land anywhere the density is non-zero. That is why the sim's Measured Std Dev stays "—" until N > 1: a spread is a property of a record, not of a shot. ψ predicts frequencies, and frequencies need an ensemble.

At N = 500 the measured σ is about 3% off. How many measurements to halve that?

About four times as many — roughly N = 2000 — because the error on the measured spread falls as σ/√(2(N−1)), i.e. as 1/√N. At the default Auto-measure rate of 20/s that is about 100 s of running. Accuracy is expensive: every extra digit costs a hundredfold more data.

Set Animation Speed to maximum and take a thousand measurements. What changes on the lower plot?

Nothing statistical. The sim's time dependence is the single global phase e−iωt, which cancels against its conjugate in |ψ|², so the sampler is drawing from the same fixed Gaussian the whole time. Only the upper plot changes: Re ψ and Im ψ trade amplitude faster inside a stationary envelope.
