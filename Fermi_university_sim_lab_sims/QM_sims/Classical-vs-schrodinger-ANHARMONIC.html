Classical vs Quantum Anharmonic Oscillator

Physics

Classical Newtonian motion in the anharmonic well V(x) = ½mω²x² + εx⁴ is compared with quantum time evolution from the Schrödinger equation using a split-operator method. The classical particle and the Gaussian wave packet start at the same initial displacement; anharmonicity makes the classical period amplitude-dependent and causes the quantum packet to spread and interfere differently than in a pure harmonic oscillator.

The Physics

The anharmonic oscillator · a complete revision sheet · one quartic well, a Newtonian particle and a wave packet

The x⁴ term is not a small correction to the spring. It is the end of the two things only a parabola ever gave you: one round-trip time for every amplitude, and a packet that never changes shape.

02

Why does a wider swing take less time, not more?

The restoring force, and the term that breaks the amplitude-independence.
  • With ε = 0 the force is strictly proportional to x. Pull twice as far and you double both the journey and the pull — they cancel exactly, and the round trip is 2π/ω at every amplitude. That is isochronism, and it is a property of parabolas, not of wells in general.
  • The quartic term adds −4εx³, which grows faster than the stretch. A wider swing therefore sits in a stiffer part of the well, and stiffer means quicker.
  • So for ε > 0 the round trip shortens as A grows: 4.83 at ε = 0, 4.21 at A = 3, 3.22 at A = 6 for this sim's m = 1, ω = 1.3, ε = 0.02.
  • Changing m or ω rescales the period but keeps it amplitude-independent. Only ε decides whether every swing agrees.
Left: the pull grows faster than the stretch. Right: so the round trip falls as the swing widens — both computed for the sim's m = 1, ω = 1.3, ε = 0.02.

04

Why does ⟨x⟩ stop following the dot?

The packet obeys the same well the dot does — but through a wave equation.
Ehrenfest's pair is always exact; what it needs is ⟨x³⟩, and that is not ⟨x⟩³.
  • The centroid's equations look Newtonian, but the force they need is ⟨F(x)⟩, the force averaged over the whole packet — not F(⟨x⟩), the force at its centre.
  • For ε = 0 the two agree exactly, because a linear force commutes with averaging. That is why a coherent state in a parabola swings rigidly for ever and ⟨x⟩ traces the classical path perfectly.
  • With ε ≠ 0 the difference is 3⟨x⟩(Δx)² plus the packet's skew — so the wider the packet spreads, the less its centre behaves like a particle. At ε = 0.10 the sim's ⟨x⟩ stays within about ±1 while the dot still reaches ±3.
  • Nothing has been damped. ⟨E⟩q is conserved — a |ψ|² spread over both turning regions simply averages the swing away.
How the sim actually advances ψ: half a potential kick, a free kinetic step in k-space by FFT, half a kick back.
  • The grid is 8192 points over x ∈ [−12, 12] and periodic, so the sim watches the norm, both energies, the probability at the edges and the high-k occupancy, and warns you before any of that contaminates the physics.
05

Where does nature run this?

  • Every chemical bond is anharmonic. Stretch it and it softens rather than stiffens, so the vibrational rungs close up instead of staying evenly spaced: in HCl the 0→1 step is 2885 cm⁻¹ and the 1→2 step only 2780 cm⁻¹. A parabola would give infinitely many equal rungs and no dissociation at all.
  • Thermal expansion is pure anharmonicity. In a perfectly parabolic bond the average length would not move when you heat it; solids expand only because the well is lopsided.
  • A superconducting transmon qubit is deliberately anharmonic — the 0→1 and 1→2 transitions are typically a few hundred MHz apart, and that gap is the whole reason a microwave pulse can address two levels and ignore the rest of the ladder.
  • Watch the sign. This sim's ε > 0 makes a hardening well, so the period falls as the swing widens. An ordinary pendulum softens instead (sin θ < θ) and its period grows with amplitude, T ≈ (2π/ω)(1 + θ₀²/16).
A real bond, computed from HCl's spectroscopic constants ωe = 2990.9 cm⁻¹ and ωexe = 52.8 cm⁻¹: the Morse well against the parabola that fits its floor, with the first ten rungs closing up towards dissociation.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "The period belongs to the well, not to the swing." Only if the force is strictly proportional to x. Here the same well gives 4.21 from A = 3 and 3.22 from A = 6 — and 4.83 at every amplitude the moment you set ε = 0.
  • "The blue curve is the wave function." It is |ψ(x,t)|², the probability density, drawn on a rescaled axis. ψ itself is complex; the sim keeps its real and imaginary parts but never plots them.
  • "The packet spreads because the numerics are getting sloppy." Spreading is the physics. The sim separately tracks the norm, both energy drifts, the edge probability and the high-k occupancy — the warning banner is what a numerical problem looks like, not the spreading.
  • "⟨x⟩ shrinking to nothing means the quantum particle lost its energy." ⟨E⟩q is constant. The packet has spread across both sides of the well, so its mean sits near the middle while the density itself still reaches the turning points.
  • "ε = 0.02 is tiny, so it barely matters." Compare terms, not coefficients: the quartic term grows as x⁴, so at A = 6 it already supplies 26 of the 56 units of the classical energy.
07

Ninety-second self-test

Set ε = 0 and change nothing else. What do the round trip and Δx do?

The well becomes a pure parabola: every amplitude takes exactly 2π/ω, and Δx sits at √(ħ/2mω) for ever. The sim starts the packet at precisely the width σ₀ = √(ħ/mω) of the parabola's ground state, so the displaced Gaussian is a coherent state — it swings rigidly at ω without ever changing shape.

Double ω at fixed m. What happens to the starting packet width, and to the anharmonicity?

Δx₀ = √(ħ/2mω) shrinks by √2 ≈ 1.41 and the harmonic round trip 2π/ω halves. The same ε also buys less anharmonicity: the quartic term is compared with ½mω²x², so a stiffer spring makes the well look more parabolic at the same A.

The dot keeps reaching ±A for ever while ⟨x⟩ decays to nearly nothing. Has the quantum particle stopped?

No — ⟨E⟩q is conserved and the sim watches its drift. ⟨x⟩ is an average over a probability density that now covers both sides of the well, so the average sits near the middle even though |ψ|² itself is still spread out to the turning points. An average going quiet is not the motion going quiet.
