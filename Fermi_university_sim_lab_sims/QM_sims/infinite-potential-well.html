About this simulation

This simulation models a quantum particle in a one-dimensional infinite square well, where the walls are impenetrable and only discrete stationary states are allowed.

Physics
  • Energy is quantized: En = n²E1.
  • The wavefunction is ψn(x, t), and measurement probability is |ψ|².
  • In physical mode, oscillation scales as ωn = n²ω0.
Visuals
  • Top plot: wave function ψ(x, t) inside the box walls.
  • Bottom plot: probability density |ψ|² with optional measurement histogram.
  • Left axis: discrete energy levels, with the selected level highlighted.
Controls
  • Choose energy level n and adjust measurement rate.
  • Toggle override mode to set animation ω directly with the speed slider.
  • Show/clear histogram, then use Pause/Reset to control the run.

The display links wave evolution to measured outcomes, showing how repeated position measurements build the probability distribution predicted by |ψ|².

The Physics

The infinite square well · a complete revision sheet · a particle in a 1D box

A trapped particle is not a ball rattling between two walls. It is a standing wave — and only the waves that fit exactly between the walls are allowed to exist.

01

Why can't a trapped particle have just any energy?

The sim's two plots, computed: ψn(x) above, |ψn|² below — at the default n = 1 and at n = 3. Red dots are the nodes the sim marks.
  • The walls are infinitely high, so the particle can never be outside: ψ must reach exactly zero at x = 0 and at x = L.
  • Inside, V = 0, so ψ is a sine. Only the sines that land on zero at both walls survive — the ones that fit a whole number of half-waves.
  • n counts those half-waves. The n − 1 red dots inside are nodes: places where the particle is never found.
  • There is no n = 0. Zero half-waves means ψ ≡ 0 — that describes no particle at all.
The whole problem is a boundary condition.
Schrödinger's equation inside the box, and the only solutions the walls permit.
02

Why do the rungs spread out instead of stacking evenly?

Fitting n half-waves fixes the wavelength, and de Broglie turns that into momentum.
  • Raise n and the wave gets shorter: λn = 2L/n. The sim's Eigenstate card prints exactly this.
  • Shorter wave → larger momentum, pn = nπħ/L — and energy is p²/2m, which squares it.
  • So the ladder is En = n²E₁, and the gaps grow: E₂ − E₁ = 3E₁, E₃ − E₂ = 5E₁, E₄ − E₃ = 7E₁.
  • This is the ladder drawn down the left of the sim: rung height goes as n², never evenly spaced.
The sim's energy ladder, computed for n = 1…5.
03

What do the numbers actually say?

n rate

These are the sim's real controls — drag here and the box follows. Note the sim's own rule: changing n clears the histogram and resets the rate to 10 /s.

What the green dots are sampling — and why ⟨x⟩ never moves off centre.
  • Double n → four times the energy, half the wavelength, twice the momentum. Box length L is fixed at 1 in this sim; it is a readout, not a slider.
  • ⟨p⟩ = 0 for every n: a standing wave is equal parts left- and right-moving. The energy is real, the average momentum is not.
04

If the state is "stationary", what is moving on screen?

The whole time dependence is one phase factor — and it cancels in |Ψ|².
  • The top plot is the real part of Ψn: ψn(x) cos(ωnt). The shape never changes — only its amplitude breathes. The "Time (phase)" readout is that clock.
  • The bottom plot never moves, because |Ψn|² = |ψn|² has no t in it. That is what "stationary state" means.
  • In Physical Mode the sim sets ωn = n²ω₀, because ω = E/ħ and E goes as n². At n = 3 the wave breathes nine times faster.
  • Override Mode is a viewing speed, not physics. It detaches ω from n² so you can slow the breathing down; the energy readout and |ψ|² are untouched.
05

Where does nature build boxes this small?

  • An electron confined to 0.5 nm — a dye molecule, a small quantum dot — has E₁ ≈ 1.5 eV. That is a visible photon: confinement sets the colour.
  • Because E₁ ∝ 1/L², a bigger dot glows redder. Grow a CdSe dot from 2 nm to 6 nm and its emission walks from blue to red — the first-order explanation is this box.
  • For a 1 g bead in a 10 cm box, E₁ ≈ 3×10⁻⁴⁴ eV and a 1 mm/s drift sits at n ≈ 3×10²⁶. The rungs are still there; nothing could ever resolve them.
  • The same E₁ is confinement energy: squeeze a particle and it fights back. That cost is why matter has a size.
One electron, computed: E₁ against box width, straight on log axes with slope −2.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "The lowest state is n = 0 — the particle can sit still." Put n = 0 into sin(nπx/L) and ψ vanishes everywhere. The ladder starts at n = 1, and E₁ is the zero-point energy: confinement forces motion.
  • "Triple n, triple the energy." Momentum triples; energy is p²/2m, so it goes up nine times. The sim's readout says 9.00 E₁.
  • "At a node the particle is just moving through quickly." A node is not a fast point on a trajectory — it is a place with zero probability. There is no trajectory here.
  • "The smeared-out wave is the particle." Every measurement returns one position. The smear is the pattern in the ensemble — which is exactly what the histogram builds.
07

Ninety-second self-test

What would the n = 0 state look like?

It would not exist. ψ₀ = √(2/L) sin(0) = 0 at every x, so the probability of finding the particle anywhere is zero. The lowest allowed state is n = 1 with E₁ = π²ħ²/2mL² — the zero-point energy a confined particle can never give up.

Double the box length and double n. What happens to the energy?

Nothing — En = n²π²ħ²/2mL² depends on n²/L², and doubling both leaves it unchanged (the wavelength λ = 2L/n is also unchanged). This sim fixes L = 1, so you can only test the n half of it.

The top plot moves and the bottom one doesn't. Why?

The time dependence of a stationary state is the single phase factor e−iEnt/ħ. The sim draws the real part of Ψ, so the wave breathes at ωn = n²ω₀. In |Ψ|² the phase multiplies by its own conjugate and cancels, so the probability density — and the statistics of the measured dots — is frozen.
