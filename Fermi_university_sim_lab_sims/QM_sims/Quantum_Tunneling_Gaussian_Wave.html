About this simulation

Physics

A localized Gaussian wavepacket propagates toward a symmetric rectangular barrier with V(x) = U for |x| < a. The model is one-dimensional, non-relativistic, spinless quantum mechanics (free-electron mass). Depending on energy and barrier geometry, the packet partially reflects and partially transmits; for E < U, the transmitted component is quantum tunneling.

Simulation visuals

The top panel shows the barrier profile and carrier-energy reference. The lower panel shows Re(ψ), Im(ψ), and |ψ|² versus position, including packet spreading and barrier interaction. Absorbing layers at both boundaries suppress wrap-around reflections from the FFT domain and support cleaner reflection/transmission estimates.

Controls

Use sliders to tune U, a, carrier E, packet width σ, initial center x₀, and playback speed. Toggle Re(ψ), Im(ψ), and |ψ|² traces; the regime badge compares carrier E to U, while Wavepacket Info reports broader packet-energy context.

Guided Inquiry

1 · The barrier

A Gaussian wavepacket — an electron spread over a few nanometres — travels toward a rectangular barrier of height U and width 2a. The top panel shows the barrier and the packet’s carrier energy E; the lower panel shows ψ and |ψ|². Here E = 0.20 eV and U = 0.30 eV.

2 · The classical particle

First, classical physics: a particle with kinetic energy 0.20 eV heads toward a barrier that takes 0.30 eV to cross. What happens when it arrives?

3 · The quantum packet

Now the quantum wavepacket approaches the same barrier — E = 0.20 eV against U = 0.30 eV. When it arrives, what happens to |ψ|²?

4 · The transmitted packet

The run repeats. Compare the small transmitted packet with the incident one: how does it travel on the far side of the barrier?
