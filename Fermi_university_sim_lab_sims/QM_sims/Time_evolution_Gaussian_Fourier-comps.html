About This Simulation

⚛ Physics

A Gaussian wave packet evolving under the free-particle Schrödinger equation, built from a superposition of plane waves with different momenta.

  • Dispersion: Each momentum component k travels at phase velocity ω/k = k, causing the packet to spread over time
  • Wave function: ψ(x,t) = Σ cₖ exp(ikx − iωt) where ω = k²
  • Spreading: Width grows as σ(t) = σ₀√(1 + t²/σ₀⁴) — narrower packets spread faster

  • Gold line: Classical particle position (group velocity)

The Physics

A wave packet and its modes · a complete revision sheet · free evolution on a circle

A wave packet is not a lump of stuff. It is a chord — a fixed set of plane waves — and everything it does in time is those waves drifting out of step, and then back into step again.

01

What is the packet actually made of?

The sim's own packet at t = 0, computed: on the left the dashed target built from all 80 waves and the solid curve from the 3 the sim draws; on the right the amplitudes |ck| those 3 come from. Narrow in x, wide in k.
  • Here x runs from −π to π and joins up, so only whole-number k fit. The sim carries 80 of them, k = −40 … 39.
  • Each wave gets one complex number ck: the |c| printed on its card and the direction its phasor points. Those two numbers are the packet.
  • A Gaussian of width σ has a Gaussian spectrum, |ck| ∝ e−k²σ². So Δx = σ, Δk = 1/2σ, and the product is exactly ½ — as tight as a packet is allowed to be.
  • At the default σ = 0.25 that means every k out to about ±2, which is why the 3 waves on screen capture only 55.2% of the packet. That number is the sim's Captured readout.
One number per plane wave — that is all a Fourier decomposition is.
Gaussian in x, Gaussian in k, and the two widths are reciprocal.
02

What does time actually change?

The amplitudes are frozen. The only thing time touches is the phase of each wave.
  • No |c| ever moves. Nothing pushes this particle, so its momentum content is fixed at t = 0 for all time — the Total Norm readout sits at 1.0000 and the card amplitudes never change.
  • What does change is each phase, turning at its own rate ωk = k². That is exactly what the phasor arrow on each card is doing.
  • So k = 0 never turns at all, and k = 3 comes round nine times for every single turn of k = 1. The rate climbs as k², not as |k|.
  • Different rates mean the waves fall out of step, and their sum changes shape. A moving picture built from a frozen spectrum — that is the whole trick.
Each wave has its own phase velocity; the packet travels at the group velocity — which is zero here.
  • The gold vg marker never moves, because this sim builds its packet around k0 = 0: the mean momentum is zero, so vgroup = 2k0 = 0. The packet breathes in place; it never travels.
03

How fast does it spread — and how much of it are you looking at?

The free-particle spreading law, and the time τ at which the width has grown by √2.
The spreading law at the sim's default σ0 = 0.25 and at the wide σ0 = 1.0, on the sim's own clock. Halve the width and the packet spreads four times sooner. The dashed line is the ring full: width π/√3 ≈ 1.81, the spread of a probability smeared evenly round the ring.
σ N

These are the sim's real σ and N sliders — drag here and the packet follows. The sim's own rule: changing σ rebuilds the packet and sends the clock back to t = 0, while changing N only changes how many waves are drawn and summed.

What the white curve really is, and what the Captured readout is counting.
  • A narrow packet needs a wide range of k, and the widest k turn the fastest — which is why halving σ makes the packet spread four times sooner (τ = σ²).
  • Captured is not an accuracy of the physics, it is how much of the packet you have chosen to draw. The dashed curve is the full packet at t = 0, kept as a fixed reference: it never evolves. Once the clock runs the full packet spreads too; at N = 13 (Captured 99.9% at the default σ) the white curve carries all but 0.1% of it, so that is the one to watch.
04

Why does it come back?

Every turning rate is a whole number, so every phase returns together.
|ψ|² computed from the sim's own 80 coefficients at four times, all on the same scale. The dashed line marks x = 0.
  • Each rate k² is a whole number, so by t = 2π every phase has turned a whole number of times and the packet is back exactly: t / 2π reads 1.000 and the white curve re-sharpens into its t = 0 shape. The ⟳ FULL REVIVAL badge is generous — it lights whenever t is within 0.3 of a multiple of 2π, including the first moments after every restart — so trust the t / 2π readout, not the badge.
  • Halfway, at t = π, the factor is (−1)k — which is a shift by π. The same shape is rebuilt on the far side of the circle; the readout says t / 2π = 0.500.
  • Truncating does not spoil it. Each wave you keep returns on the same schedule, so even the 3-wave curve revives — it just revives to the wrong shape.
  • On an infinite line k is continuous, there is no common return time, and the packet spreads for ever. The revival is a property of the circle.
05

Where does nature run this?

  • Restore the units and σ(t) = σ0√(1 + (ℏt/2mσ0²)²). An electron pinned to σ0 = 1 nm doubles its width in about 30 fs, and is roughly 60 µm wide one nanosecond later. Localising a light particle is expensive and does not last.
  • The same law says a 1 g bead localised to 1 µm would take longer than the age of the universe to visibly spread. Spreading is not slow physics; it is physics with ℏ/m in front of it.
  • The circle is not a toy either. An electron going round a semiconductor nanoring — or, in the standard free-electron model, round a benzene ring — carries whole-number angular momentum ℓ with E ∝ ℓ², which is the same k² law and gives the same exact revivals.
  • In a real atom the levels go as −1/n², not as n², so Rydberg wave packets revive only approximately, and long after their classical orbit period. Exact revival needs a spectrum that is exactly quadratic in a whole number — which is what this sim hands you.
  • These are order-of-magnitude figures from the same formulas restored to SI; the sim itself works in units where ℏ = 2m = 1 and never states them.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "The packet spreads, so its momenta must be spreading too." No force acts, so the momentum content is conserved: every |ck| is fixed for all time. Watch the card amplitudes while the curve below churns — they do not move.
  • "The white curve is the packet." It is the sum of the N waves you chose. At the defaults that is 55.2% of the packet. The dashed curve is the whole packet, but only as it was at t = 0 — a fixed reference that does not spread.
  • "Re ψ is the particle, |ψ|² is the wave." Backwards. The lime and pink curves are the two components of the complex amplitude ψ; the white curve is the probability density |ψ|², and that is the only one a position measurement is distributed by.
  • "The revival means the particle came back." Nothing travelled — vgroup = 2k0 = 0 and the gold marker never moves. The phases came back into step, so the shape rebuilt.
07

Ninety-second self-test

Drag σ to the slider's minimum, 0.15. What happens to Δk, to τ, and to Captured at N = 3?

Δk = 1/2σ rises from 2.0 to 3.33, so the packet now needs k out past ±3. τ = σ² falls from 0.063 to 0.023 — it spreads nearly three times sooner. And three waves now capture only about 34.9% instead of 55.2%, so the white curve sits even further below the dashed target.

Now take σ to the maximum, 1.5. Does the packet stop spreading?

No — it still fills the ring, but it takes about t ≈ 1.5 instead of t ≈ 0.45, roughly a quarter of the 2π period instead of a fourteenth. What does change is the spectrum: Δk = 1/3, so everything past k = ±1 has collapsed to nothing and three waves already capture 100%.

The spectrum never changes, yet the picture does. How?

|ψ|² is not the sum of the |ck|, it is the squared modulus of their coherent sum. Each wave carries a phase φk − k²t, and those phases turn at different rates, so the interference between the waves changes even though every amplitude is frozen. At t = 2π the phases are all back where they started, and so is the picture.
