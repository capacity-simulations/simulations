Guided Inquiry

1 · The packet in the bowl

A Gaussian wave packet is released from rest at displacement x₀ inside the parabolic well V(x) = ½ω²x². The left panel draws |Ψ(x,t)|² over that well, with a classical particle released from the same point; the right panel plots the state in the (x, p) plane. Units are ℏ = m = ω = 1, so the coherent width is σ₀ = 0.71.

2 · Sitting at the bottom

The packet is centred at x₀ = 0 with σ = 0.71. It is not at rest — the zero-point energy ½ℏω is still there. As time runs, what does |Ψ|² do?

3 · Pull it aside

The packet now starts at x₀ = 2, still at σ = 0.71, and the classical particle starts there too. Over several swings, how does the peak of |Ψ|² move relative to that particle?

4 · Squeeze it narrower

Same start at x₀ = 2, but the initial width is now σ = 0.40, tighter than σ₀. As the packet swings, what does the width of |Ψ|² do?

5 · Counting the breaths

The narrow packet is back at the start. In the time its centre takes to make one full round trip, how often does the packet pass through its narrowest shape?

The Physics

Time evolution in a quantum harmonic oscillator · a complete revision sheet · coherent and squeezed packets

A packet in a parabolic well is not a fuzzy ball being pushed around. It is a probability density whose centre obeys Newton exactly — while its width keeps a clock of its own, running at twice the speed.

01

What is the shaded curve, and what is it doing?

The sim's left panel, computed at its defaults (x₀ = 2, σ = σ₀ = 0.71): the same packet at three phases of one orbit. The shape never changes; only the centre moves.
  • The teal curve is |Ψ(x,t)|² — the probability density. The sim never plots Ψ or Re Ψ; there is no oscillating wiggle to look for, only a hump whose position and width change.
  • A Gaussian is fixed by two numbers, and those are exactly the two the sim computes each frame: the centre ⟨x⟩(t) and the width Δx(t).
  • The pink dot is a classical particle released from the same x₀, drawn on the well at its own potential energy. It is a yardstick laid beside the quantum state, not part of it.
  • Screen time is the phase ωt: one full orbit is 2π. This sim fixes ħ = m = ω = 1, so σ₀ = 0.71 and the orbit period is 2π.
The well the packet lives in, and the curve the sim actually draws.
02

Why does the centre move exactly like a classical ball?

Ehrenfest's theorem — and the one potential for which it closes exactly.
  • Quantum averages always obey d⟨x⟩/dt = ⟨p⟩/m and d⟨p⟩/dt = −⟨V′(x)⟩. That much is true in any potential.
  • Only for a parabola is ⟨V′(x)⟩ = mω²⟨x⟩ — the average of the force equals the force at the average. The mean position then satisfies Newton's equation exactly, with no approximation.
  • So ⟨x⟩(t) = x₀ cos ωt for any initial width. Squeeze the packet as hard as the slider allows and the peak still lands on the pink dot.
  • This is a property of the well, not of the packet. In an anharmonic well ⟨V′(x)⟩ ≠ V′(⟨x⟩), and the two would slowly drift apart.
03

What do the numbers say for your settings?

σ₀ is the one width matched to the well; for every other σ the two spreads swing.
x₀ σ

These are the sim's real controls — drag here and the packet follows. The 0.01-step σ slider cannot land on σ₀ = 1/√2 exactly, which is why the sim carries a Snap to Coherent button.

The energy of this Gaussian: a swing term plus an internal cost that bottoms out at ½ħω.
  • x₀ sets the swing and nothing else — it never touches the width, the breathing or the breathing rate.
  • σ sets the internal energy. Move it either way from σ₀ and ⟨H⟩ rises: a narrow packet pays in momentum, a wide one pays in potential.
04

Why does the packet breathe at twice the orbit frequency?

Rewrite the cos² and sin² and the width carries cos 2ωt — one orbit, two breaths.
Δx over one full orbit, computed from the sim's own getWidthAtTime: flat at σ₀, two full breaths at σ = 0.40, the width the guided inquiry uses.
  • A narrow packet carries a wide momentum spread, Δp(0) = ħ/2σ. A quarter orbit later the well has rotated that momentum spread into position spread, and the packet is fat.
  • The width is σ at phases 0, π, 2π and σ₀²/σ at π/2, 3π/2 — so a packet narrower than σ₀ starts narrow and fattens, and a wider one does the reverse. At σ = σ₀ the two ends coincide, the swing has zero amplitude, and nothing breathes: that is the coherent state.
  • The width cannot tell left from right: it is the same at +x₀ and −x₀. That is the whole reason it repeats twice per orbit, at 2ω — the sim's state banner says exactly this.
  • Watch the Δx projection bracket under the phase-space panel. It is Δx(t) drawn directly, and it opens and closes twice per lap.
At t = 0 every width is a minimum-uncertainty state; only σ₀ stays matched to the well as it evolves.
05

Where does nature run this?

The (x, p) plane, computed from the sim's own Δx(t) and Δp(t): a coherent state is a circle of noise that never changes, a squeezed state trades width between the two axes.
  • A coherent state is what each mode of a laser field is in, and what a laser-cooled ion in a trap becomes when you displace it and let go. Its quantum count is ⟨n⟩ = mωx₀²/2ħ — at the sim's default x₀ = 2 that is exactly 2 quanta.
  • Squeezed light is this same picture applied to the electromagnetic field: push noise out of the quadrature you are measuring and into the one you are not. LIGO injects squeezed vacuum to drop its shot-noise floor below the coherent-state circle.
  • Near its equilibrium length a molecular bond is a harmonic oscillator with ħω of a few tenths of an eV — which is why vibrational spectra sit in the infrared.
  • The floor ½ħω never goes away. The same zero-point energy is why helium stays liquid at atmospheric pressure all the way down to absolute zero.
The coherent-state amplitude, and the quantum count at the sim's defaults.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "The packet spreads out for ever, like a free particle." A free packet has nothing to pull it back. Here the restoring force bounds Δx between σ and σ₀²/σ, and returns it to its starting width every half orbit.
  • "A coherent state is just a classical particle." Its centre follows the classical path, but it still has width σ₀, still carries ½ħω when x₀ = 0, and a single measurement still returns one position drawn from |Ψ|².
  • "|Ψ|² is the particle, smeared out." The teal curve is the odds, not the object. The sim draws a density; it never draws a spread-out particle.
  • "Breathing means the packet is gaining or losing energy." ⟨H⟩ is constant — the well does not depend on time. The breathing only moves that fixed energy back and forth between the kinetic and potential terms.
  • "σ = 0.71 is just where the slider happens to start." It is σ₀ = √(ħ/2mω), the unique width whose position and momentum spreads are matched to this well.
07

Ninety-second self-test

Set x₀ = 0 with σ = σ₀. What does the screen do?

Nothing moves. ⟨x⟩ = 0 at every phase and Δx stays at σ₀, so |Ψ|² is frozen — this is the ground state. It is not "at rest": ⟨H⟩ is still ½ħω, and the phase-space blob is still a circle of radius σ₀ sitting on the origin.

Halve x₀ from 2 to 1, keeping σ = σ₀. What happens to the energy, and to the breathing period?

The swing term ½mω²x₀² falls by a factor of four, from 2 ħω to 0.5 ħω, so ⟨H⟩ goes from 2.5 ħω to 1.0 ħω (the internal ½ħω is untouched). The breathing period does not change at all — it is π/ω, set only by the well. And at σ = σ₀ there is no breathing to time in the first place.

The centre completes one lap while the width completes two. Why?

⟨x⟩ carries cos ωt, but Δx² is built from cos²ωt and sin²ωt, which rewrite as a constant plus cos 2ωt. Physically: the width is the same at +x₀ and at −x₀, so it cannot distinguish the two halves of the orbit and must repeat twice as often.
