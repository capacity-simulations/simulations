About: LC Oscillations

Source

Topic
Electromagnetic Induction — free oscillation of an ideal LC circuit
Simulation
A capacitor charged to V0 discharges through a pure inductor. The circuit is IDEAL: R = 0 exactly, so the oscillation never decays. A real coil has resistance and the swings die away — that case is the damped LCR circuit, not simulated here.
Concept
q(t) = q0cos ω0t and I(t) = −q0ω0sin ω0t with ω0 = 1/√(LC): current is largest exactly where charge vanishes. Energy shuttles between q²/2C and ½LI² while the sum stays fixed.
Inquiry
Predict what the charge does after the switch closes — drain away and stop, or come back the other way?
Misconception
“The capacitor discharges and that is the end of it.” That is what a resistor would give. The inductor's magnetic field carries the current past q = 0 and recharges the plates with the opposite sign.

Guided Inquiry

1 · The circuit

A capacitor charged to V0 is wired straight across a coil of inductance L. There is no resistor anywhere in the loop.

q is the charge on the capacitor, I the current through the coil. The switch closes when the clock starts — press ▶ Play.

2 · Predict — after the switch closes

The capacitor starts fully charged. What does its charge q do from then on?

3 · Predict — the instant the plates are empty

Take an instant when the capacitor's charge is exactly zero. What is the current in the coil then?

4 · Predict — the two energies together

UE in the capacitor and UB in the coil both rise and fall. What does their sum do?

5 · Predict — a bigger capacitor

The capacitance C is doubled and L is left alone. What happens to the period T?

6 · One sentence

An LC circuit is a mass on a spring built out of fields: L is the inertia, 1/C the stiffness, charge the displacement and current the velocity.

This one is ideal. A real coil has resistance, so a real circuit's swings shrink and die away.
