Topic
Electromagnetic Induction — self-inductance and the LR transient
Simulation
One circuit, one clock, one pair of axes. A two-position switch puts the battery in the loop (growth, I = I∞(1 − e−t/τ)) or takes it out and closes a freewheeling loop through R and L alone (decay, I = I₀e−t/τ). Both transients are drawn on the same time axis at the same pixels-per-second, so they are directly comparable.
Concept
An inductor opposes CHANGE in current — not current itself. The steady current is ε/R, set by the resistor alone; L only sets τ = L/R, the time scale of both the rise and the fall.
Inquiry
Predict whether the current jumps to ε/R when the switch closes, and whether it vanishes when the battery is taken out.
Misconception
“Current follows the battery instantly.” It cannot: a step in I would need an infinite dI/dt, and εL = −L·dI/dt would be infinite with it.

Guided Inquiry

1 · The circuit

A battery of EMF ε = 12 V, a resistor R and a coil of inductance L sit in one loop. A two-position switch either puts the battery into that loop (position ①) or takes it out and closes a freewheeling loop through R and L alone (position ②).

Whenever the current changes, the coil sets up a back-EMF εL = −L·dI/dt.

2 · Predict — the switch goes to ①

The switch is thrown to position ① at t = 0. What does the current do?

3 · Predict — double the inductance

Hold R fixed and double L. What happens to the curve?

4 · Predict — the battery is taken out

The switch moves to position ②: the battery leaves, and R and L are left joined in a closed ring. What does the current do?

5 · Predict — what pays for the heat?

In position ② the current still heats the resistor. What supplies that energy?

6 · One sentence

An inductor never opposes current — it opposes change in current. One time constant τ = L/R governs the rise and the fall alike, and the coil's EMF turns round between them: against the battery while the current grows, along the current while it dies.
