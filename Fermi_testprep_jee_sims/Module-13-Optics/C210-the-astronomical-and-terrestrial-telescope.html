Topic
Geometric optics — refracting telescopes (Class 12, NCERT Ch. 9)
Simulation
Two refracting telescopes on one bench and one scale: the astronomical (Keplerian) pair, and the terrestrial version with an erecting lens between them.
Concept
You buy an erect image with tube LENGTH, not with magnification.
Serves
Angular magnification M = tan α′ / tan α = −fo/fe in normal adjustment; tube length fo + fe; the unit-magnitude erecting relay (u = v = 2f) and the terrestrial tube fo + 4f + fe; the intermediate real image; image inversion; the near-point form.
Inquiry
Predict — before dragging — which eyepiece magnifies more, and what the erecting lens actually costs.
Misconception
"A bigger eyepiece magnifies more" (fe is in the denominator), and "turning the image up must cost magnification" (it costs 4f of tube instead).

Guided Inquiry

1 · A star, and two lenses

A star is so far away that its light reaches the objective as a bundle of parallel rays, tilted by the angle α the star makes with the axis. The objective brings that bundle to a real image in its own focal plane, and the eyepiece — one focal length further on — sends the light out parallel again at a new angle α′. The angular magnification is M = tan α′ / tan α.

2 · Which eyepiece magnifies more?

The objective stays at fo = 1.00 m. Two eyepieces are available: one of focal length 50 mm, one of 20 mm. Which gives the larger angular magnification?

3 · The image arrives upside down

The emerging bundle leaves the eyepiece on the opposite side of the axis from the bundle that arrived, so the final image is inverted — that is the minus sign in M. A terrestrial telescope inserts a third converging lens of focal length f between objective and eyepiece, placed so it re-forms the intermediate image at the same size the other way up.

4 · What does the erecting lens cost?

The objective and the eyepiece keep the focal lengths they already had. What does adding the erecting lens cost?

5 · Why exactly 4f?

The erecting relay adds 4f, not 2f and not 6f. Which fact about a single thin lens fixes that number?

6 · The one thing to notice

Both telescopes magnify by the same amount: |M| = fo/fe. The only thing the erecting lens changes is the tube: 4f longer, and an image the right way up. Astronomers do not mind which way up a star field is, so they leave the lens out and keep the shorter tube; a spotting scope used on the ground puts it in and pays the length.

Angular magnification — the definition

M is a ratio of TANGENTS, not of angles. Using the angles themselves breaks as soon as α′ approaches 90°, which it does whenever fo/fe is large.

Astronomical telescope — normal adjustment

Both focal points coincide, so the system is afocal: parallel in, parallel out, final image at infinity and the eye relaxed. M < 0 — the final image is inverted.

Terrestrial telescope — the erecting relay

u = 2f is the one object distance a single lens images at unit magnitude, and it returns v = 2f with m = −1. That 2f + 2f is the whole extra tube, and it leaves |M| alone.

Near-point adjustment (the other convention)

Only if the eyepiece is pushed in so the final image lands at the near point D = 25 cm. The eye then accommodates. Everything drawn in the scene is the relaxed-eye normal adjustment above.
