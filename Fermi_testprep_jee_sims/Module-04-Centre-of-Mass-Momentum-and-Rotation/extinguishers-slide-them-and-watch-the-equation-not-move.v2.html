Concept
Figure 4.2: the same beam and same two extinguishers can balance or not depending on where they sit. The force ledger (ΣF = 0) is blind to that difference; the twist ledger (weight × distance) is what changes.
Serves
Distinguishing translational equilibrium from rotational equilibrium. The force sum stays 0 in every arrangement of the level beam; only the torque ledger tips.
Inquiry
Slide an extinguisher outward and watch: which ledger changed, and which stayed put? Where in the force equation does position appear?
Misconception
That moving a weight along the beam changes the support’s normal force Np. While the beam stays level it does not — Np = 2Wf + Wb wherever they sit.

Guided Inquiry

▼

1 · Two arrangements of the same beam

The same beam, the same mount and the same two fire extinguishers appear twice: balanced in the top panel, unbalanced in the bottom one.

Every force arrow is identical between the two panels. The only thing that differs is where the extinguishers sit.

2 · The complete force diagram

One rigid plank rests on one mount, which touches the underside at a single point, so the beam can turn about that point. Two identical extinguishers stand on it, one each side, about 29 cm out. Each presses down with its weight Wf, drawn in crimson where it stands.

The beam’s own weight Wb acts through its middle, on the mount’s vertical line, and the mount pushes up with a single force Np, drawn in gold:

∑Fy = Np − 2Wf − Wb = 0

Not one distance appears in it.

3 · Predict: slide one outward

Both extinguishers weigh the same. If you slide the right extinguisher farther from the mount and leave the left one alone, what happens to the support force Np that the mount pushes up on the beam?

4 · Three sliders: two distances and a weight

Unlocked below — Left distance, Right distance and Weight of each extinguisher.

You are holding the beam level. Holding it level takes a twist, not an extra push, so the hold adds nothing to the force ledger. The arrows now scale with the numbers, and a measuring bar appears under the beam.

Drag a distance: the Np row flashes gold to show it did not change, and only the twist ledger moves. Drag the weight: every entry in the force ledger changes at once, and the twist ledger stays balanced whenever the two distances match.

5 · Which ledger moved?

After sliding an extinguisher outward, which of the two ledgers actually changed?

6 · Let go, and name the product

Unlocked — Let go of the beam and the two panel presets.

Make the two distances unequal, then press it. The beam is free to turn about the mount, so this stops being a statics problem: both ledgers freeze at their last held-level values, and while the beam turns the vertical forces no longer sum to zero. The side with the larger weight × distance goes down — never the heavier side, because the two extinguishers weigh the same.

That product, the force times its distance from the pivot, is called torque (Section 4.1). Full equilibrium needs both ledgers at zero: ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0. Press Finish to tuck this away and explore freely.

The Physics

Two extinguishers on a beam · a complete revision sheet · force ledger vs twist ledger

Two ledgers watch the same beam. Only one of them cares where anything sits.

01

Every force on the beam

The complete force diagram at the sim's own defaults — arrow lengths drawn to scale.
  • Three forces pull down: the beam's own weight Wb at the middle, and one Wf under each extinguisher.
  • One force pushes up: the mount's Np. While the beam hangs level and still, up must exactly carry all three downs.
  • That balance is the force ledger — the sim's first table.
Translational equilibrium — the vertical forces cancel.
Solve for the mount's push: only the weights enter. No a, no b.
02

Why sliding changes nothing — in that ledger

  • Forces add as vectors: the sum of the downs is 8.0 + 8.0 + 12.0 = 28.0 N wherever the extinguishers stand.
  • Slide one to the very end of the beam — the force ledger does not move. Position is simply not a column it keeps.
  • That is the sim's title acted out: the equation that stays the same while the beam is level.
03

The ledger that does care: weight × distance

The same 8.0 N at 15 cm and at 40 cm — the twist grows with the arm, the weight doesn't.
Rotational equilibrium about the mount — this is where position enters.
a b Wf

These are the sim's real sliders — drag here and the beam follows.

  • Move a or b and the twist ledger changes at once — while Np above refuses to budge.
04

Let go — which ledger decides?

  • Release the beam with a ≠ b and it tips toward the larger product W·d — not toward "the heavier side": both sides carry the same 8.0 N.
  • The force ledger still reads what it read; it never predicted rotation. Turning is the twist ledger's job.
  • While it turns, the mount still pushes up — the beam rotates about the mount instead of falling.
05

Where the real world runs this equation

A seesaw balances a heavier rider by seating them closer — equal products, not equal weights.
  • Seesaws and balance scales: a 16 N toddler at 1.5 m balances an 8 N bag at 3.0 m — 24 = 24.
  • Wrenches and door handles: the same hand-force twists harder on a longer arm, which is why the handle sits far from the hinge.
  • Crane counterweights: a huge weight parked close to the tower cancels a light load held far out.
06

The traps they'll set for you

  • "Sliding it outward makes it press harder." No — its weight is 8.0 N wherever it stands. Only its twist grows.
  • "Np must change when the arrangement changes." While the beam is level and still, Np = 2Wf + Wb — no position in it.
  • "It tips toward the heavier side." With equal weights it still tips — toward the larger weight × distance.
  • "Torque is just another force." Different quantity, different units: the sim's twist ledger reads N·cm, not N.
07

Ninety-second self-test

Both extinguishers slide from 29 cm out to 48 cm. What happens to Np?

Nothing — Np = 2Wf + Wb = 28.0 N. Positions never enter the force ledger, and the beam stays balanced because the two twists still cancel (a = b).

Left at 19 cm, right at 46 cm, equal weights. Which way does it tip when released, and why?

Toward the right — 8.0 × 46 = 368 N·cm beats 8.0 × 19 = 152 N·cm. Same weights; the longer arm wins the twist ledger.

Could you balance the beam with the left extinguisher at 20 cm and the right at 40 cm?

Only by making the weights unequal so the products match: WL × 20 = WR × 40, i.e. the left one must weigh twice as much. In this sim both share one weight slider, so it can't — which is the point of the a = b condition.
