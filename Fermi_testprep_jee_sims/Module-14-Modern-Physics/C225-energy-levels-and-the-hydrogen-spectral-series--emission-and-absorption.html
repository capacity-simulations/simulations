About the simulation

Explore hydrogen spectral lines in the Bohr model, where energy levels follow En = −13.6/n² eV. Switch between emission (photon emitted) and absorption (photon absorbed) transitions.

Visuals
  • Left: quantized energy levels with transition arrows and labels.
  • Right: atom view with circular orbits and the moving electron.
  • Bottom: a log wavelength strip from 50 nm to 2500 nm (UV · visible · IR). Solid lines are visible wavelengths, dashed lines UV or IR. Transitions past 2500 nm (5 → 4, 6 → 4, 6 → 5, 7 → 5, 7 → 6) are flagged at the right edge rather than drawn at a wavelength they do not have.
  • Orbit rings are equally spaced so every jump stays watchable; the true Bohr radii grow as rn = n²a0.
Controls
  • Set transition levels with From n and To n.
  • Toggle Emission/Absorption mode — it mirrors the level pair, so the same λ lights up bright or goes dark.
  • Absorption starts from the level in From n, and the atom must already be there: cold hydrogen sits in n = 1 and absorbs only Lyman. Stellar Balmer absorption needs gas hot enough to hold atoms in n = 2.
  • Use the series buttons (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen) to fire every line of one series.
  • Adjust animation speed and clear recorded lines.

Guided Inquiry

1 · Look before you fire

Left: the Bohr ladder. Right: orbits. Bottom: an empty wavelength strip (UV · visible · IR). Mode is Emission; From n = 3, To n = 1.

Press Next when you have the layout, then predict where the line will land.

2 · Predict — where does 3 → 1 land?

You are about to emit a photon for the jump 3 → 1. Where on the strip will the line appear?

3 · Fire 3 → 1

Press Make Transition. Watch the electron drop and a mark appear on the strip. Read Last transition: region and λ.

Does the line sit in the visible rainbow, or off to the UV side?

4 · Why Lyman is invisible

Every Lyman line lands on n = 1. Those gaps are the largest, so every Lyman photon is ultraviolet. The colours you see from hydrogen are Balmer lines — jumps that land on n = 2 (3 → 2 is 656 nm red).

On the strip, UV and IR lines are drawn dashed: real photons your eye simply cannot register.

6 · Same gap, two arrows

Emission releases a photon; absorption swallows one. For a given pair of levels both carry the same energy, so both sit at the same wavelength: 1/λ = R (1/n₁² − 1/n₂²), with n₁ the lower level, n₂ the upper, R = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹.

Flip the Mode switch and fire again — the wavelength that lit up bright now goes dark. Series are named by the lower level: Lyman n = 1, Balmer n = 2, Paschen n = 3.
