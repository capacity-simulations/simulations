Lecture

Simulation
"Newton's Three Laws — One Idea at a Time": three tabbed 1-D scenes (① inertia · ② F = ma · ③ action–reaction), with a labeled ⚠ counterfactual model per scene.
Concept
Force changes motion — it does not sustain it: Fnet = 0 ⇒ constant velocity; a = Fnet/m; interaction forces come as equal-magnitude opposite pairs acting on two different bodies.
Serves
① zero net force ⇒ constant v (not rest), with balanced mg/N arrows drawn; ② live a = Fnet/m readout — same force, double mass ⇒ half acceleration; ③ equal & opposite pair on different bodies, recoil |aA|/|aB| = mB/mA with Σp = 0.
Inquiry
Does v change while Fnet = 0? What speed does the puck keep after F returns to 0? Same 4 N on 1 kg vs 2 kg — what happens to a? During contact, do the two force readouts differ?
Misconception
"If an object is moving there must be a net force pushing it along" — zero net force means constant velocity, not rest. (Scene ③ adds: "the heavier body pushes harder.")

① Law of inertia

F_net = 0 ⇔ dv/dt = 0

Zero net force means velocity is constant — any constant, not necessarily zero. Rest (v = 0) is just one special case. On the puck, mg and N cancel: balanced forces ⇒ no change of motion.

② F = ma

a = F_net/m ; x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²

Kernel-verified checks: forces +6 N and −2 N on m = 2 kg ⇒ Fnet = 4 N and a = 2 m/s². From rest, 4 N on 1 kg for 2 s ⇒ v = 8 m/s and x = 8 m. Same F on double m ⇒ half a.

③ Action–reaction

F on A = −(F on B) ⇒ |a_A|/|a_B| = m_B/m_A

+5 N on B ⇒ −5 N on A, always — on the two different bodies. From rest, mAvA + mBvB = 0 (total momentum stays zero).

⚠ The counterfactuals (for contrast)

v = F/k (k = 2 N·s/m) ; F_X ∝ m_X

⚠ Aristotle (scenes ①–②): force sustains velocity — F = 0 ⇒ instant rest. ⚠ "Heavier pushes harder" (scene ③): body X pushes with 5 N·mX/m̄ — an unequal pair, so Σp is not conserved. Both are implemented exactly so you can watch them fail.
