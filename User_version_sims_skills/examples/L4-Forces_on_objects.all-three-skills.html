About: Forces on a Block — Vector Sum Sets the Motion

Source

Simulation
Forces on a Block — Vector Sum Sets the Motion (draggable-force sandbox with incline mode and a counterfactual "biggest force" model)
Concept
The block's acceleration is set by the vector sum of ALL forces, a = ΣF/m — never by the single biggest force; on an incline gravity decomposes into mg·sinθ (along the slope) and mg·cosθ (balanced by N).
Inquiry
Can two smaller forces cancel a 5 N force? When Fnet is redirected mid-flight, does the block instantly travel that way (a vs v)? Why is the frictionless slide rate g·sinθ independent of m? How big a force holds the block on the slope?
Misconception
"The block moves in the direction of the largest single force." Reality: motion responds to the vector SUM, and it is the acceleration (not the velocity) that aligns with Fnet.
Verified values
(5,0)+(0,4)+(−2,−1) N → Fnet = (3,3) N, |Fnet| = 4.24264 N at 45° (not along the 5 N arrow). Incline θ = 30°, m = 2 kg: N = mg·cosθ = 16.9914 N; mg·sinθ = 9.81 N; a = g·sinθ = 4.905 m/s² down-slope (g = 9.81 m/s², SI throughout).

Guided Inquiry

1 · Meet the three forces

On a frictionless table (top view): F₁ = 5 N →, F₂ = 4 N ↑, F₃ = 2.24 N ↙. The block sits still until you press ▶. Look at the arrows — one is clearly the biggest.

2 · Predict — which way does it go?

When you press ▶, which way does the block set off?

3 · Watch Fnet and a

Press ▶ if needed. The violet Fnet and green a arrows appear — they lie along each other at ~45°, not along the biggest orange force. Acceleration is set by the sum: a = Fnet/m.

Now turn it mid-flight: drag any force so Fnet points a new way. The green a arrow snaps instantly to the new direction; the cyan v arrow — and the path — bend only gradually. Velocity is accumulated history — only the acceleration must point along Fnet.

4 · Defeat the 5 N force

The arrows are reset so two ~3.9 N forces cancel the 5 N one: Fnet = 0. Read |Fnet| and |a| — both zero. A big force can be erased by smaller ones acting together, component by component.

5 · Tilt the surface

Incline at θ = 30°, m = 2 kg. Gravity splits into mg·sinθ = 9.81 N along the slope; N balances the rest. Frictionless, |a| = g·sinθ ≈ 4.91 m/s² — sweep the mass and watch |a| stay put: m cancels, so every mass slides alike.

To hold it still, press + Force and drag the new arrow up-slope until |a| = 0 — that needs exactly mg·sinθ. Or toggle friction (μ = 0.30): below θ ≈ 16.7° the block sticks.

6 · The invariant

Whatever you build — flat or incline, one force or many — the green a arrow always lies along violet Fnet. No single arrow decides the motion. Only the sum does.

Newton's 2nd law: acceleration is set by the vector sum of every force — its direction and its magnitude.

Components add separately (x with x, y with y). Default set: (5,0)+(0,4)+(−2,−1) = (3,3) N → 4.24 N at 45° from +x.

Incline (θ from horizontal): gravity splits into an along-slope pull mg·sinθ and a surface-pressing part mg·cosθ. With no applied ⊥ forces, N = mg·cosθ; otherwise N adjusts to balance all ⊥ components.

Frictionless slide: m cancels — a = g·sinθ (4.905 m/s² at 30°). With friction, kinetic fk = μN opposes sliding. (N may differ from mg·cosθ if applied forces have a ⊥ component.)
