About the Simulation

The double-slit experiment demonstrates wave-particle duality. When particles pass through two slits, they create an interference pattern on the detection screen.

Quantum (green): |ψ₁ + ψ₂|² — amplitudes add, then square. The interference fringes arise from constructive/destructive interference.

Classical (orange): A broad spray of particles is incident on the slits, and pass through either slit 1 or slit 2.

Close a slit: Use the S1/S2 buttons to close one slit and see single-slit diffraction. Reduce λ into the UV range to see sinc² zeros and secondary maxima. Notice that some screen positions receive fewer particles with two slits open than with one — evidence of destructive interference!

Guided Inquiry

1 · The apparatus

A source emits monochromatic light of wavelength λ towards a barrier with two slits, separated by d and each of width a. A detector screen stands a distance L behind the barrier, and every particle that gets through is recorded where it lands.

2 · Bullets through two slits

Think of the incoming particles as tiny bullets that travel in straight lines, each passing through one slit or the other. After many have landed, what does the screen show?

3 · The real source

The bullets are replaced by the real source: monochromatic light of wavelength λ, both slits open. After many arrivals, what does the screen show now?

4 · One dot at a time

Each arrival is a single localised dot, and the bands only emerge after thousands of dots have landed. What keeps the dots out of the dark regions?

5 · Halving the separation

Both slits stay open and λ, a and L are unchanged. The slit separation d is halved, from 1600 nm to 800 nm. What happens to the spacing between neighbouring bright bands?

Slit Control

Close one slit to see single-slit diffraction (quantum) or a single Gaussian (classical). To see sinc² zeros and side lobes, reduce λ so that a/λ > 2. Compare with both slits open to see where destructive interference makes fewer detections than one slit alone.
