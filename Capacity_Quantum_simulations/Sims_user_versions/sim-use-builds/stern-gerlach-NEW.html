About the Simulation

Physics: In an inhomogeneous magnetic field, atoms feel a spin-dependent force along z. Quantum mechanics predicts discrete outcomes: for total spin \(j\), the beam splits into \(2j+1\) channels labeled by \(m_j=-j,\ldots,+j\).

Visuals: The left canvas shows source, magnet, trajectories, and impact spots on the detector window. In quantum mode you see separated bands by \(m_j\); in classical mode you see a continuous spread.

Controls: Set Total Spin \(j\), Magnetic Field Gradient \(\partial B/\partial z\), and Beam Intensity. Use Switch to Classical Prediction to compare models, Reset to clear counts, and Hover & Explore for inline hints.

$$F_z = -g_J \, m_J \, \mu_B \, \frac{\partial B}{\partial z}$$

Guided Inquiry

1 · The apparatus

A particle source emits particles with a random magnetic moment μ, and the beam passes through an inhomogeneous magnetic field aligned along the z-axis, as shown. The force on each particle is proportional to the projection of the magnetic moment along z, denoted μz. Quantum mechanically, the magnetic moment is proportional to the particle’s spin.

2 · The classical prediction

3 · The split

The source now emits spin j = 1/2 particles, and the beam splits in two. Why?

4 · Counting the bands

For particles of spin j, how many bands does the beam split into? The spin j is yours to change — let the detector decide.

5 · Doubling the gradient

The field gradient is doubled while the source emits j = 1/2 particles. How does the force on each particle change?
