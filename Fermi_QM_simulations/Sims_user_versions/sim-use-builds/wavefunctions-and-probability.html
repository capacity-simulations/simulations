📊 About This Simulation

Physics: The particle is prepared in a Gaussian wavefunction ψ(x). Measurement outcomes follow the Born rule, so the position probability density is |ψ(x)|². Repeated measurements on identically prepared states reproduce the theoretical distribution.

Simulation visuals: The top plot shows the theoretical wavefunction view (probability, real part, imaginary part, or both real/imaginary). The bottom plot shows measured outcomes as a normalized histogram, overlaid with the theoretical |ψ(x)|² curve for comparison.

Controls: Run single-shot or batch measurements, enable auto-measurement and set its rate, adjust animation speed, switch view mode, and reset measurements. The Statistics panel reports total count, mean position, measured standard deviation, theoretical σ, and convergence.

Guided Inquiry

1 · The prepared state

Before every measurement the particle is prepared in the same Gaussian state ψ(x), of width σ. The upper plot draws that state — either its probability density |ψ(x)|² or its real and imaginary parts. The lower plot collects the positions measurements actually return, as a normalized histogram against the theoretical |ψ(x)|².

2 · Identical preparations

Each measurement acts on a freshly prepared copy of the same state. What would classical physics predict for the record of a hundred position measurements?

3 · A wave that dips below zero

The upper plot now draws Re(ψ), which swings above and below the axis as the phase turns. At an instant when the whole curve lies below the axis, where is the particle most likely to be found?

4 · A thousand more

About a hundred measurements now sit on the lower plot. Add a thousand more. What happens to the height of the central bars?

5 · While the phase turns

The upper plot now shows Re(ψ) and Im(ψ) together, with the phase turning quickly, and the histogram has been cleared. What will the next thousand measurements build?
