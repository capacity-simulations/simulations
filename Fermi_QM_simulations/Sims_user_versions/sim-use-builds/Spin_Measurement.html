About the simulation

This simulation visualizes a single-qubit spin state on the Bloch sphere and shows how projective measurements along the X, Y, and Z axes collapse the state.

Use the Measure Spin buttons to perform axis measurements, Set Custom State to enter amplitudes or presets, and Reset in the header to return to the default state. The wavefunction panel and probability bars update after each action.

Guided Inquiry

1 · The Bloch sphere

A spin-½ particle is drawn as the white arrow on the sphere: its tilt θ from the Z axis and its azimuth φ around that axis fix the state |ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|↑⟩ + eiφsin(θ/2)|↓⟩. The poles are |↑⟩ and |↓⟩, and the X and Y axes carry their own pairs of states. Measuring along an axis asks the particle which of that axis’s two states it is in.

2 · The classical picture

Before quantum mechanics, a spin is a small arrow of fixed length ℏ/2 that may point in any direction. Tilt it 60° away from the Z axis and measure its component along Z. What comes back?

3 · One measurement

The arrow now lies on the equator along +X, an equal mix of |↑⟩ and |↓⟩. Measure Z once. What does the history record?

4 · How often up?

The state is now |ψ⟩ = (√3/2)|↑⟩ + (1/2)|↓⟩, the arrow tilted 60° from Z. Prepared afresh many times and measured along Z each time, what fraction of the runs give Spin Up?

5 · Measuring twice

The arrow is back on the equator, an even mix. A Z measurement snaps it to one pole. What does a second Z measurement on that same particle return?

6 · An X measurement in between

The particle sits at the |↑⟩ pole, the definite Z result of a first measurement. Measure X, then measure Z again. How does this second Z result compare with the first?
