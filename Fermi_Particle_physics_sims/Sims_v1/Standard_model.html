About: Exploring the Standard Model

Source

Concept
The Standard Model is not an arbitrary list of particles — it is three generation columns of identical structure with only the mass changing, plus the force carriers (W⁺ and W⁻ shown separately) and the Higgs. The compact Lagrangian is shorthand for exactly what the table shows.
Simulation
Two-mode explorer: (1) the table with a logarithmic mass ladder; (2) the compact ℒ_SM with plain-English term expansions.
Serves
Recognising the three-generation pattern; reading a logarithmic axis over five and a half orders of magnitude; distinguishing exactly-massless (photon, gluon) from tiny-non-zero (neutrinos).

Guided Inquiry

Welcome to the chart

Every known fundamental particle is on this canvas. Before we look for a pattern, ask yourself: which of these particles have you already met, and which are new? Click a tile to open its detail panel — mass, charge, spin, the year we found it.

No answer needed. Explore a few tiles, then hit Next.

Look down a row

The chart has three vertical columns — the three generations. Compare the top row across all three columns: u, c, t. Or the electron row: e⁻, μ⁻, τ⁻. What stays the same across a row?

The mass ladder

Look at the mass ladder running down the right side of the canvas. The axis is logarithmic — each labelled tick is 10× the one below. The electron sits near 0.5 MeV; the top quark near 173 GeV. How many orders of magnitude is that?

The zero-mass band

Look at the band below the log axis, labelled "massless / unresolved". Five particles live there: the photon, the gluon, and the three neutrinos. But those two groups mean very different things. Which is true?

The one equation behind it

Now switch the mode selector to Lagrangian. That single compact line is the entire Standard Model — every particle you just met is IN it. Click any term to see it expand into a plain-English sentence.

The point isn't the algebra. The point is: the compact form is shorthand, not magic. Every symbol stands for something you've already seen on the chart.

What the shorthand is FOR

The Lagrangian is a bookkeeping device. Each term encodes one interaction: a fermion coupling to a gauge field, a gauge field self-interacting, a particle getting mass from the Higgs. The Standard Model isn't a list of parts — it's a rule for how those parts talk to each other.

The compact Standard Model Lagrangian

This one line encodes every fundamental interaction of matter and force we have ever measured. It fits on a coffee mug because each symbol is shorthand for a large object — a field, a covariant derivative, a gauge coupling.

Reading the terms

−¼ F F — kinetic terms for the gauge bosons (photon, gluon, W, Z) and their self-interaction.
iψ̄ D̸ ψ — every fermion in the chart, propagating and coupling to the gauge fields.
ψᵢ y ψⱼ φ + h.c. — Yukawa couplings: each fermion's mass comes from its coupling to the Higgs φ.
|D φ|² − V(φ) — the Higgs field itself, and its potential.
